# 03 — Adherence: who keeps going, and what not to reward

**What this file establishes.** Two things, both narrow. Attrition is the default
outcome of solo app-based study and is predicted by dispositional persistence
rather than by motivation or ability; and tangible rewards for completing study
undermine intrinsic motivation more in children than in adults. Almost every
other question in this area came back empty — see §3, which is the longest
section for a reason.

---

## §1 Plan for attrition, and note what does *not* predict it

Sudina, Teimouri & Plonsky (2025), "L2 grit and age as predictors of attrition in
mobile-assisted language learning", *Learning and Individual Differences*,
doi 10.1016/j.lindif.2025.102704. A six-month natural experiment on **601
beginners** learning Spanish or French through user-controlled Duolingo usage.
Verified via OpenAlex after ScienceDirect returned 403 **[V 3-0]**.

> "Attrition rates in mobile-assisted language learning are high" *(paper's own
> highlight)*
>
> "The best-fitting and most parsimonious model included two meaningful
> predictors of attrition: L2 grit PE and a natural log of 'age.'"

Of seven candidate predictors, only two survived: **L2 grit
perseverance-of-effort** and **log(age)**. The ones that did *not* meaningfully
predict dropout are the interesting half — **L2 motivation, grit
consistency-of-interest, self-rated proficiency, and measured pretest C-test
score**.

**Two design consequences, and they cut against the obvious move:**

- Because dropout tracked persistence rather than motivation, **motivational
  instrumentation aimed at "making it more fun" has no support here.**
- **Low measured proficiency is not a dropout risk signal.** A struggling
  learner is not, on this evidence, a learner about to quit.

**Limits.** The actual attrition **percentage could not be retrieved** (paywall),
so this supports a qualitative rule and **never a numeric figure**. No odds
ratios are obtainable, so this is direction-of-finding only. The sample is
self-selecting adult Duolingo users on Spanish and French, and the log(age) term
implies an adult spread — **nothing here establishes any of it for a
13–14-year-old**. And exclusion from a model-selected fit is not the same as a
null effect. Single study; no replication located.

## §2 Do not reward completion with points, badges or streak prizes

Deci, Koestner & Ryan (1999), *Psychological Bulletin* 125(6):627–668 — verified
by local text extraction of the scanned PDF **[V 3-0]**:

> "For 57 tangible-reward studies of free-choice behavior with children, the
> composite **d = −0.39** (CI = −0.47, −0.32), and for 38 with college students,
> the composite **d = −0.27** (CI = −0.36, −0.19). Thus… tangible rewards… tend
> to be more detrimental to the intrinsic motivation of children than of college
> students."

The age moderator is significant, Qb(1) = 4.18, p < .04 — though note the paper
hedges to "tended to be more detrimental" in its abstract while the test itself
clears p < .05.

**The caveat that bites hardest for a 13–14-year-old**, in the paper's own words:

> "Most of the children were preschool or elementary students, but 6 studies used
> high school students."

Six is an upper bound within that subset. **Adolescents are barely represented in
the very cell this rule leans on.**

**Two things verification removed, which must not be carried forward:**

- The per-contingency effect sizes (engagement −0.40 / completion −0.36 /
  performance −0.28) were **refuted 0-3** **[X]**. Do not quote them.
- The verbal-praise claim — that positive feedback *enhances* intrinsic
  motivation — was **not sustained (1-2)** **[X]**, and its age split runs the
  other way, carried by college students. **This file therefore contains no
  warrant for any positive claim about praise**, only a warrant against tangible
  completion rewards.

The paper reports no significant age effects for the other reward and
reward-contingency categories.

## §3 What this file does not establish — most of the question

The research brief asked five things of this area. **Four came back with nothing
that survived verification.**

> **GAP** — **Streaks specifically.** The rule in §2 is about *tangible rewards*
> in a 1999 laboratory meta-analysis. Nothing was retrieved on streak mechanics
> as implemented in modern language apps — not their effect on retention, not
> their effect on motivation, not what happens when one breaks. Extending §2 to
> cover streaks is an inference, and a streak that carries no tangible prize may
> not be a "tangible reward" at all.

> **GAP** — **Reminders and notifications.** Nothing retrieved.

> **GAP** — **Implementation intentions and goal-setting for study behaviour.**
> Nothing retrieved. This was an explicit ask and it returned no verifiable
> claim.

> **GAP** — **Habit-formation timelines.** Nothing retrieved. The widely repeated
> "N days to form a habit" figures were not sourced, verified, or admitted here
> in any form.

> **GAP** — **The L2 self-regulated-learning meta-analysis was neither replicated
> nor refuted.** `../ielts/09-design-principles.md` §2.0 carries it at g = 0.72
> with the standing note that its abstract and results disagree and that
> publication bias was detected. This pass was asked to find independent
> replications or refutations and **found neither**. Its marker stays **[S]** and
> its caveat stays attached.
