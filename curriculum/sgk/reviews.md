# Reviews 1–4

Four two-page cumulative sections, after Units 3, 6, 9 and 12. Each covers the
**three preceding units at once**, and is the only place in the book where
material from more than one unit is tested together.

Every Review has the same two halves:

| Half | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| **Language** (Review 3 calls it *Language Focus*) | Pronunciation (1 ex) · Vocabulary (2 ex) · Grammar (2 ex) |
| **Skills** | Reading · Speaking · Listening · Writing — one task each |

The writing task is always a paragraph of **80–100 words**, and it is always a
*recombination* — a topic that draws on two or three of the units at once, never
a repeat of a single unit's task.

### Skills

- **Reading** — a five-strategy text on managing teen stress (*Getting a good
  night's sleep* — 8 to 10 hours; *Doing exercise* — at least 60 minutes a day;
  *Talking it out* — to a teacher, parent, or someone you trust; *Writing about

- **Speaking** — pair interview on three questions (reasons people like / dislike
  living in the countryside; advantages or disadvantages of living in the city),
  take notes, report.
- **Listening** — Tom and Mai on city vs countryside life; fill a two-column
  table with **ONE word** per gap (5 gaps).
- **Writing** — a paragraph of **80–100 words** about the leisure activities **one
  of your family members** does, cued by three questions. Frame: *In his / her
  free time, my ______ likes ______*

**2 · Vocabulary** — match a verb in A with a noun in B: *weave* → **clothing** ·

**2 · Vocabulary** — gap-fill from a box: *customers · bargain · single-use ·

**3 · Vocabulary** — **word formation**, correct form of the word given:
*(destroy) → destructive* · *(advantage) → disadvantage* · *(extinct) →

**4 · Grammar** — correct tense: past simple / past continuous, present simple for

**5 · Grammar** — complete open-ended complex sentences so they are true for you,

### Skills

- **Reading** — a text on **landslides** (the mass movement of rocks or earth down
  a slope; more frequent in mountainous areas; may come suddenly after a storm or
  heavy rain) with six bulleted safety instructions. Five True/False items.
- **Speaking** — pair: *Do you face any of the following disasters where you
  live?* (volcanic eruptions · droughts · storms · earthquakes · floods ·
  landslides), then talk about the damage they may cause.
- **Listening** — a passage on air pollution; fill five gaps with **NO MORE THAN
  TWO WORDS** (exhaust fumes; bad gases harmful for children; transport is not
  the only reason; less pollution today comes from ___ than in the past).
- **Writing** — a paragraph of **80–100 words** about **where your family shops
  and why**, referring to convenience, prices, freshness of food, etc. Frame:
  *My family usually shops ______*

## Review 4 (Units 10–11–12) — book pp. 134–135

### Language

**1a · Pronunciation** — choose the word with a different **stress pattern**:

| | A | B | C | D |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | referee | amazing | historic | invention |
| 2 | digital | conference | Japanese | difficult |

**1b · Pronunciation** — read two sentences aloud with correct **sentence
stress** and count the stressed words: *How will people travel to work in the
future?* · *Will technology replace humans in the future? — No, it won't.*

**1c · Pronunciation** — **draw an intonation arrow** above each underlined word
in two list sentences: *I can remember the names of some planets, such as Venus,
Neptune, and Mars.* · *They have a TV, a fridge, a table, and four chairs.*

**2 · Vocabulary** — three-option MCQ, five items: *a small digital image used on
social media to express emotions* (**emoji**) · *a ___ with students from
different countries* (**video conference**) · *the results of the ___*
(**experiment**) · *when you look at the ___ for too long, your eyes may get
tired* (**computer screen**) · *how many stars there are in the ___* (**galaxy**).

**3 · Vocabulary** — **word formation**: *(possible) → possibility* ·
*(recognise) → recognition* · *(communicate) → communication* · *(connect) →
connection* · *(translate) → translation.*

**4 · Grammar** — **error identification** (A/B/C), five items, mixing
prepositions of time (*did an experiment **on** three hours* · *replace human
teachers **will** by 20 years*), possessive pronouns (*they aren't **your*** ·
*a friend **in** mine*) and prepositions of place/time (*be at school **for**
7:00 a.m.*).

**5 · Grammar** — change five sentences into **reported speech**, mixing
statements and questions: *"What planet do you want to visit?" my friend asked
me.* · *"I'm now reading a book about future ways of communication," she told me.*
· *"How will teachers check attendance in the future?" Lan asked Nam.* · *"We are
having a video conference with other clubs next week," our club president said.* ·
*I asked my mum, "When will there be a full moon?"*

### Skills

- **Reading** — **three notices**, A, B and C, then a five-row grid ticking which
  notice each statement matches. A **matching-features across multiple short
  texts** task.

  | | Notice | Content |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | A | New club members wanted | Science club; must be **12–14 years old**; creative and want to learn new things; meets **once a week on Sundays in the town library**; email science@fastmail.com or call 091234567 |
  | B | School speaking contest | An English-speaking contest about the planets in our solar system; students **create posters** of a planet of their choosing and **present them to the teachers**; contact your English teacher **no later than April 25** or email speaking@fastmail.com |
  | C | A talk about future means of communication | Learn about future means of communication from experts; **2 p.m., April 8**, School library; **phone 094738224** for more information |

  Statements: mentions the age of the participants · participants give a
  presentation · participants discuss ways of communication in the future ·
  participants meet once a week · interested people call for more details.

- **Speaking** — pair: ask and answer about **a device or an app you use for
  studying** (What kind? · How often? · What are the benefits? · Are there any
  problems?), then report your partner's answers.
- **Listening** — Mark on the solar system; fill five gaps with **NO MORE THAN TWO
  WORDS** (many planets have ___; the four inner planets are quite small and
  have ___; the asteroid belt has millions of rocky ___; the outer planets are ___
  and mostly made up of gas; these outer planets all have thick layers of clouds
  and ___ around them).
- **Writing** — a paragraph of **80–100 words** about **your favourite means of
  communication**, cued by four questions (What is it? · How often do you use
  it? · What are the benefits? · Do you think you will use it in the future?).
  Frame: *My favourite means of communication is ______*
