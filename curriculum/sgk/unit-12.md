## 1 · Getting Started — "A thrilling science fiction novel" (track 74)

**Dialogue.** Mai and Nick. Nick is on the last page of *A journey back to
Soduka*, a **science fiction** book about four **creatures** — Titu, Kaku, Hub and
Barb — travelling back to **Soduka, a planet like Earth**. They have to land on
Earth because their **spaceship breaks down**; they meet **Tommy** and become
friends with him. Tommy helps them **repair their spaceship** so they can travel
back to their home planet, but their **commander** forces them to return to Earth
to **destroy** it. Tommy and the four creatures try to **oppose** the commander,
and manage to stop him from destroying Earth. Mai asks what Nick thinks about the
**possibility of aliens attacking Earth**; Nick: *"I'm not sure about it. But I'm
starting to think about it. I sometimes ask myself **what we would do if aliens
took over our planet**."*

Both the Everyday English function (*I'm not sure about it*) and the grammar
(*I ask myself what we would do*) are planted in the last turn.

### Pronunciation — Intonation for making lists (tracks 75, 76)

> **Remember!** When we make a list, our voice slightly **rises** on the previous
> item(s) and **falls** on the last item.

> ***Mercury ↗, Venus ↗, Earth ↗, and Mars ↘***
> *We've got **bananas ↗, apples ↗, pears ↗, and oranges ↘***

## 3 · A Closer Look 2 — Reported speech: questions (book pp. 127–128)

> **Remember!** We normally use ***ask, wonder,*** and ***want to know*** when we
> report questions. In reported questions we use **the same word order as in
> statements** but **omit the question mark**.
>
> *She asked me, "What are you doing now?"*
> → *She **asked** me **what I was doing then**.*
>
> Remember that **pronouns, possessive adjectives, verb tenses, place
> expressions, and time expressions change** in reported questions just as in
> reported statements.

| Ex | Type | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Underline the correct word or phrase | *what she was doing now / **then*** · *what plants Vietnamese people **grow** / grew for food* (a general truth) · *what specialities they are selling **there** / here* · *what forms of life can / **could** exist on Mars* · *how many planets **there were** / were there in our solar system* (word order) |

## 5 · Skills 1 (book p. 130)

### Reading — the possibility of life on other planets

**Ex 1** — pair lead-in: What do you know about other planets? · Would you like
to live on another planet? Why / Why not?

**Text.** Three paragraphs, ~200 words. Humans are still wondering what planets
in outer space might **support life**. Scientists say planets need to meet
**three main conditions**:

1. **Liquid water** — so their temperature must not be too high or too low.
2. The **correct amount of air**, so they can **hold an atmosphere** around them.
3. The right **size** — if a planet is too small, its **gravity** is not strong
   enough to hold enough air; if too big, its gravity will be so strong that it
   will hold too much air.

Scientists are using **space telescopes** to find **habitable** planets.
According to them, **Mars** is one of the most **promising** planets for life in
our solar system: it is a planet like Earth, its days last for **24.5 hours**,
and its seasons are similar to Earth's. Although scientists have not found actual
water on Mars, there seem to be **traces** of it on the planet's surface.
However, the climate on Mars is **unsuitable for human life** because it is
**too cold** and Mars **lacks oxygen** to support human life.

| Ex | Type | Items |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | Match five highlighted words to meanings | **liquid** = in the form of a substance that flows freely and is not a solid or a gas · **gravity** = the force that causes something to fall to the ground · **habitable** = suitable for people to live in · **promising** = showing signs of being good or successful · **traces** = marks, objects, or signs that show that somebody or something existed |
| 3 | Short-answer, five questions | What are humans still wondering nowadays? · Why does a habitable planet need to have the correct amount of air? · What happens if a planet is too small? · How long does a day on Mars last? · Why can we not live on Mars? |

### Speaking — conditions a planet needs

**Ex 4** — pair: tick which of five statements are true conditions for supporting
human life (two are distractors):
1. There must be enough liquid water on the planet. ✓
2. The planet must have craters on its surface. ✗
3. The planet must hold an atmosphere. ✓
4. The planet must have at least two moons. ✗
5. The planet must have enough oxygen in the air. ✓

Follow-up: *Can you add other conditions for a habitable planet?*

**Ex 5** — group: talk about the conditions you think are required. Frame: *There
are some conditions planets must have to support human life on them. First, the
most important condition is that …*

it gets) · **appearance** · **something special**. Present it to the class.
