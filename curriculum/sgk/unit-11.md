## 1 · Getting Started — "Great news for students" (track 68)

**Dialogue.** Minh and Ann. Minh really enjoys **learning online**; Ann prefers
**face-to-face classes** because she likes to **interact** with classmates. Minh
argues online classes are **convenient during bad weather or epidemics**, and
students can still interact in **breakout rooms**. Ann counters that the
**Internet connection** doesn't always work well enough, and her **eyes get
tired** in front of a **computer screen** for a long time. Minh has good news:
**3D contact lenses** will soon be available, so eyes won't get tired. Another
helpful invention is **robot teachers** — they will teach when human teachers are
not available or get ill, and *"My uncle said the robots **would** be able to
**mark our work** and give us **feedback** too."*

That last line is the reported-speech model the grammar section works from.

### Pronunciation — Sentence stress (tracks 69, 70)

> **Remember!** Sentences have stressed and unstressed words. We usually stress
> **nouns, main verbs, adjectives, adverbs and negative auxiliaries, and question
> words**. We normally stress the words ***Yes*** and ***No*** as well as
> **auxiliary verbs in short answers**.
> *How **important** is **science** to **students**?*
> *Will you **use** your com**pu**ter to **learn**?*
> *Did he **buy** a new **smart**phone yesterday? — **No**, he **didn't**.*

- **Ex 4** — listen and repeat five sentences, attending to the bold syllables:

## 3 · A Closer Look 2 — Reported speech: statements (book pp. 117–118)

**Ex 1** — go back to the *Getting Started* dialogue and match Minh's uncle's
direct speech to the reported version.

> **Remember!** Reported speech is used to report what someone said.
> *Mi said, "I want a new computer."* → *Mi said (that) she wanted a new computer.*
>
> When we turn a direct sentence into a reported one, we change:
>
> **verb forms**
> | Direct | Reported |
> | --- | --- |
> | Present simple — *"I love science."* | Past simple — *she loved science* |
> | Present continuous — *"I am learning English."* | Past continuous — *she was learning English* |
> | *will* — *"I will become a scientist."* | *would* — *he would become a scientist* |
>
> **time and place expressions**
> *now → then · today → that day · ago → before · tomorrow → the following day /
> the next day · this week → that week · here → there*
>
> **pronouns according to situations**
> *I → he / she · we → they · me → him / her …*

| Ex | Type | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | Complete the reported sentence with the correct verb form | Five items across present simple, *will*, present continuous, *be going to*, and a negative present simple |
| 3 | Complete with time/place words from a box | Box: *there · that year · that day · then · the next day* — five items |
| 4 | Full **sentence transformation** into reported speech | Five direct quotations attributed to a scientist, a teacher, a dad, Tom, and a teacher |
| 5 | **GAME** — He / She said that … | One student says a sentence, the other converts it to reported speech; then swap |

## 5 · Skills 1 (book pp. 120–121)

### Reading — two **advertisements**

**Ex 1** — pair: name the technological applications in three pictures
(eye-tracking, fingerprint scanner, face recognition). Can they be used in
schools?

**Text A — Biometrics.** An advert. *No more worries about **truancy** and
**cheating**!* Introduce biometric applications at your school: with **fingerprint
scanners, or facial or voice recognition** technologies, schools can **check
students' attendance**; teachers will no longer need to call students' names to
find out who is absent, making more time for activities. Schools can also use
biometrics for students who **borrow books and equipment**. Teachers can use
**eye-tracking** applications to **check students' understanding** of a lesson and
to motivate them. *For more information, visit www.biometricappsolution.com*

**Text B — Nanolearning.** An advert. *Tired of sitting in front of a computer
all day long? Unable to concentrate for very long in your classes? Or frequently
forgetting large amounts of information?* The solution is **Nanolearning**,
created by **Junglemap in 2006**. It provides **small amounts of information over
a short period of time**, so learning becomes **effortless**. Receive bits of
information **within two to five minutes** via the platform and you will
**increase your learning attention and ability**. The app also **reports your
study activities and results to your teacher**.
*Contact us at www.nanolearningsolution.edu.com*
