## 1 · Getting Started — "Tet is coming!" (track 26)

**Dialogue.** Elena and Trang look at a photo of Trang's cousin at **Sa Dec
Flower Village**. Before Tet many people visit flower villages to take pictures
with the **blooming flowers**. Trang's family usually visits **Nhat Tan Village**
to buy **kumquat trees** and **peach blossoms**. Vietnamese people use plants and
flowers for **decorations** and for **offerings** — an important part of the Tet
tradition. The tall "tree" in the photo is a **bamboo pole** (*cây nêu*), placed
in the yard of the **communal house** and hung with **decorative items** like
small bells and lanterns, to **chase away bad luck** and **pray** for a lucky
new year.

## 3 · A Closer Look 2 — Zero article (book p. 53)

> **Remember!**
> Articles in English include *a / an*, *the*, and the **zero article**.
> Sometimes we use nouns without *a / an* or *the*. We use the zero article
> - with **uncountable or abstract nouns** — *Sugar is not good for your teeth.*
> - when making **general statements** — *Students should work hard.*
> - when describing **general forms of transport** — *We went there by boat.*

| Ex | Type | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Choose the correct option | *more than **a** / an hour* · *It's a / **Ø** tradition for children to wake up early on Christmas Day* · *takes place in the / **Ø** October* · *by an / **Ø** air* · *Where's a / **the** book?* |
| 2 | **Error identification** (A/B/C underlined) | Five sentences on Kitchen God customs, e.g. *The worshipping Kitchen Gods is **a** long-time tradition of the Vietnamese* · *Most families in **the** Viet Nam* · *offerings include a set of flowers and fruits, **a** paper clothes, and three carps* |
| 3 | Fill with *a, an, the,* or *Ø* | five sentences: *have ___ breakfast at home on Sundays* · *bring ___ small gift* · *held on the 10th of ___ January* · *Ancient Egyptians worshipped ___ Sun* · *hard work is ___ important Vietnamese value* |
| 4 | Cloze with *the* or *Ø* | A five-gap text about family-time traditions in the UK — card games, TV programmes, karaoke shows |
| 5 | **GAME** — Bingo! | Groups read teacher-given sentences, identify the article mistake(s), correct them, and say *Bingo!* when finished. First group with all correct wins. |

## 5 · Skills 1 (book pp. 55–56)

### Reading — "A village festival day"

**Ex 1** — pair lead-in on a picture of a boat race: what are the men doing? ·
when do you think this event occurs?

**Text.** First-person, four paragraphs, ~200 words. A small village in northern
Viet Nam; every year people look forward to the **third day of Tet**, one of the
most important festival days. In the morning they gather along the **riverside**
to watch competitions. First a special **boat race**: some team members cook rice
on the boat while others row as fast as they can — **the fastest team with
well-cooked rice wins**. Then the referee **releases a duck** into the middle of
the river and contestants jump in to catch it; the atmosphere is loud with drums
and cheers of **festival goers**. At noon there is a village party at the
**communal house** for the elders, and each family holds a home party with
traditional dishes like **sticky rice and steamed chicken**, sometimes including
food children love, such as *bún chả* or even pizzas. The festival helps them
**maintain traditions, connect with other people, and strengthen family bonds**.

| Ex | Type | Items |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | Four MCQs incl. a **phrase-meaning** item | 1 What is the text mainly about? (**A local tradition**) · 2 To win the boat race, a team must ___ (**be the fastest boat and properly cook the rice**) · 3 **The phrase "releases a duck" means** ___ (**gives it freedom** / catches it / takes it home) · 4 Why is the festival important? (**Because it keeps some village traditions alive**) |
| 3 | **Mind-map completion** | A four-branch map of the *Family party*: **When** — on the (1) ___ of Tet · **Where** — at (2) ___ · **Who** — family members · **What to eat** — traditional (3) ___, kids' favourite (4) ___ |

### Speaking — a normal family event

**Ex 4** — **dialogue ordering**: put five questions A–E into the right blanks
1–5 of a printed conversation between Nick and Lan, then role-play it.

- **A** And what do you do at parties?
- **B** Lan, what family event do you often take part in?
- **C** Do you like these parties?
- **D** Where do you hold these parties?
- **E** Who joins you at these parties?

Lan's answers, in order: birthday parties of all her family members · in a
Vietnamese restaurant, but sometimes they *break with tradition* by going to a
Western one · everyone in her family · the birthday person opens the gifts and
everyone has good food · yes, they're a great time for **family bonding**.

**Ex 5** — make notes about a real family event using five cues (What is the
event? · Where and when does it happen? · Who joins with you? · What do you often
do? · Do you like it or not? Why?), then make a dialogue in pairs.

### Project — Local Customs and Traditions

Groups choose a custom or tradition practised in their home town, research it
(they can ask parents or grandparents) against five guiding questions (What is
it? · Who practises it? · Where and when? · What do people do? · Why?), and
report to the class, using visual aids.
