## 1 · Getting Started — "My favourite shopping place" (track 46)

**Dialogue.** Mai and Alice talk about Alice's trip to **Bac Ha**. Alice liked
**Bac Ha Fair** most — an **open-air market** in **Lao Cai**. The people at the
market wore **colourful costumes** and came from different **minority groups**;
most products sold there were **home-grown and home-made**. In Alice's city,
**Auckland, New Zealand**, there is a **farmers' market** every Saturday where
farmers sell their products; her mother loves shopping there and **rarely misses
one**. Mai prefers the **supermarket**: she can find almost everything, doesn't
have to **bargain**, and all the items have **fixed prices** on their **price
tags**. Mai has to go — her **art lesson starts at one o'clock** — and wants to
call at a **convenience store** on the way.

The last turn is the grammar planted in the dialogue: *"My art lesson **starts**
at one o'clock"* — present simple for a scheduled future event.

## 3 · A Closer Look 2 — Frequency and future timetables (book p. 85)

Two grammar points.

### Adverbs of frequency

**Ex 1** — gap-fill from a box: *always · often · sometimes · rarely · never* —
five sentences, incl. *You can ___ bargain at a supermarket because the prices
are fixed* (**never**) and *How ___ do you return things you buy online?*
(**often**).

### Present simple for future events

> **Remember!**
> We use the present simple with a **future meaning** to talk about
> **timetables or schedules**.
> *I have to go now. My art lesson **starts** at one o'clock.*
> *The train **leaves** at 4:30, so we still have a lot of time.*

| Ex | Type | Content |

## 5 · Skills 1 (book pp. 87–88)

### Reading — "Why We Go to Shopping Centres"

**Ex 1** — lead-in tick: the most common reason(s) why you go shopping —
*hanging out with friends · reducing stress · buying goods · exercising ·
browsing*

**Text.** Three paragraphs, ~200 words. Shopping centres attract a lot of
customers, especially at weekends, on holidays, or during sales. People go there
to shop: shopping centres offer a **wide range of products**, and customers can
**touch the products and try on clothes and shoes**, which makes them feel more
comfortable deciding to buy. But people also go for many other reasons. Some go
for **entertainment** — these centres often offer **year-round free
entertainment** for customers of all ages, such as **live music and special
performances**. During holidays shoppers can see **decorations** and join in the
holiday excitement, which is a good way to relax. Some go just to **hang out with
friends**, browsing and chatting while wandering through the malls. Others visit
to **get exercise**: they enjoy walking for one or two hours in **clean and
well-lit areas**. Some even go there to **avoid the heat or cold outside** —
shopping centres offer **free air conditioning and heating**.

- **Ex 3** — complete with a suitable **adverb of frequency**, five sentences,

the range of products / services / entertainment it offers · what is special
about it.
