## 1 · Getting Started — "I'm keen on doing DIY (do-it-yourself)." (track 1)

**Dialogue.** Tom meets Trang in a shop. Trang is buying a knitting kit; she is
keen on DIY and likes doing things on her own (knitting, building dollhouses,
making paper flowers). Tom is the opposite — he hangs out with friends, goes to
the cinema, cycles, plays sport in the park. Tom invites Trang to a new comedy
at New World Cinema on Sunday with him and Mark; Trang accepts and asks to bring
Mai. They agree to meet outside the cinema at 9 a.m.

| Ex | Type | Asks | Target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | Sentence completion | Five gaps from the dialogue: what Trang is looking for; when she does DIY; who Tom spends free time with; what Tom and his friends do; when they will see the comedy | Recall + leisure lexis |
| 3 | Picture labelling from a word box | Box: *doing DIY · doing puzzles · surfing the net · cooking · playing sport · messaging friends* — six photos | The six core activity phrases |
| 4 | Matching benefits to activities | 1 improve memory, a mental exercise · 2 save money, increase creativity · 3 improve physical health, make friends · 4 keep in touch, be relaxed · 5 learn something about IT, computer skills | Reasons for doing an activity |
| 5 | Group speaking | *If you have some free time this weekend, what will you do?* — ask, then report to the class | Report-back |

## 2 · A Closer Look 1 (book p. 10)

### Vocabulary — expressions about likes and dislikes

| Ex | Type | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Matching two columns | A: *be fond · be keen · be interested · be crazy · be* → B: *about · in · on · into · of*. Answers: fond **of**, keen **on**, interested **in**, crazy **about**, be **into** |
| 2 | Gap-fill from a box | Box: *into · fond · keen · interested · about* — five sentences (taking photos; history/museums; cooking, wants to be a chef; surfing the net; DIY projects) |
| 3 | Personalised completion | *I'm crazy about ___ · I'm keen on ___ · I'm fond of ___ · I'm not interested in ___ · I'm not into ___*, using the same six activities or the learner's own. Example given: *I'm interested in doing DIY.* |

> **Remember!** To turn these expressions into dislikes, add **not** after the
> verb *be*: *She is keen on cooking.* → *She is **not** keen on cooking.*

Note that every one of these expressions is followed by a **gerund**, which
sets up A Closer Look 2.

### Pronunciation — /ʊ/ and /uː/ (tracks 2, 3)

- **Ex 4** — listen, repeat, sort into two columns:
  *cook · group · push · June · school · would · woman · move*
- **Ex 5** — listen and practise five sentences; underline the bold /ʊ/ words
  and circle the bold /uː/ words. Bold words: **books, pool · cool, football ·
  drew, puddings · soup, sugar · cartoon, wolf**

## 3 · A Closer Look 2 — Grammar (book p. 11)

> **Remember!**
> - Some verbs of liking and disliking are followed by **gerunds only**.
>   *They **dislike surfing** the net.*
> - Some are followed by **both gerunds and *to*-infinitives**.
>   *I **love going / to go** to the cinema.*

| Ex | Type | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Sorting into two columns | *love · like · detest · fancy · dislike · enjoy · hate · prefer* → "gerunds only" vs "both gerunds and *to*-infinitives" |
| 2 | Three-option multiple choice | 1 *I love ___* (travelling / travelled / a travel) · 2 *Tom enjoys ___ puzzles* · 3 *When do you like ___ TV?* · 4 *Do you fancy ___ to the cinema?* · 5 *I detest ___. I think it's cruel to harm animals.* (hunting) |
| 3 | Picture-cued sentence writing | Five pictures, verbs given: *like · enjoy · dislike · hate · prefer* |
| 4 | Personalised — "All about me!" | Two columns, ☺ and ☹: *I like / I prefer / I love* · *I dislike / I hate / I detest* |
| 5 | **GAME** — Likes and dislikes mimes | One student mimes an activity; the others guess with *Yes/No* questions using the verbs learnt. Example: *Do you like surfing the net? — No, I don't.* |

## 4 · Communication (book p. 12)

### Everyday English — Inviting and accepting invitations (track 4)

Two highlighted model exchanges:

| Inviting | Accepting |
| --- | --- |
| **Would you like to** go to the cooking club with me this Sunday? | **I'd love to. Thanks.** |

badminton · to make paper flowers with you · to try your home-made pizza.

## 5 · Skills 1 (book p. 14)

### Reading — Trang's leisure activities with her family

**Ex 1** — lead-in: name the activities in three pictures (cycling, dressmaking,
board games/crafts).

**Text.** A first-person account, ~170 words, one paragraph of framing plus one
of detail. Trang loves spending time with her family because it is a great way
to **connect** with them. At the weekend they go for a bike ride to nearby
villages for fresh air and take photos. She and her big brother are into
cooking: he looks for easy recipes, then they prepare ingredients and cook —
sometimes the food is good, sometimes not, but they love whatever they cook.
The activity she likes **most** is doing DIY projects with her mum, who teaches
her to make her own dresses and doll clothes; on special occasions they make
special dresses together. She once won first prize in a costume contest at
school.

| Ex | Type | Items |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | Gist + **vocabulary in context** | 1 The text is about Trang's leisure activities ___ (in the past / with her friends / **with her family**) · 2 The word **"connect"** is closest in meaning to ___ (**join with something** / better understand someone / speak to someone by phone) |
| 3 | Short-answer, five questions | What does Trang do with her family members at the weekend? · Who looks for recipes when Trang and her brother cook? · Which leisure activity does she love the most? · What does Trang's mum teach her to do? · What did she win? |

### Speaking — Talking about leisure activities with family

**Ex 4** — group: take turns asking three questions and recording answers in a
four-column table (*Friend's name · Question 1 · Question 2 · Question 3*):

1. What leisure activities do you usually do with your family?
2. Which one do you like the most? Why?
3. How do you feel when you spend time with your family members?

**Ex 5** — report to the class: *What activities are the most common?*

## 6 · Skills 2 (book p. 15)

### Listening — an interview with Mark (tracks 5, 6)

**Ex 1** — lead-in discussion: what activities can we do with friends in our
leisure time, and why should we spend time with friends?

**Ex 2** — multiple choice, three options:
1. When does Mark usually have free time? (At weekends / On Sundays / On Saturdays)
2. Who does he spend his free time with? (His neighbours and friends / His family and relatives / **His family and friends**)

**Ex 3** — table completion, **NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS** per gap, six gaps
across an *Activities* / *Reasons* grid:

| Activities | Reasons |
| --- | --- |
| Inviting friends to his house · Cooking and watching a (1) ___ | Fun and better than going to the (2) ___ |

### Writing — an email about your free time

**Ex 4** — pair preparation, three questions: When do you usually have free
time? · What do you usually do with your friends in your free time? · Why do you
do these activities?

**Ex 5** — write an email of **80–100 words** to a penfriend about what you
usually do with your friends in your free time, using the answers in 4.

A "New Message" window is printed with **From / To / Subject: My free time**,
and the frame is given:

> Hi ______,
> It's nice to hear from you again.
> Let me tell you about ______
> …
> Bye for now,

## 7 · Looking Back + Project (book pp. 16–17)

### Vocabulary

- **Ex 1** — five sentences completed with leisure activities (doing puzzles
  online; doing DIY — paper flowers and bracelets; messaging friends; playing
  sport; surfing the net).
- **Ex 2** — write complete sentences from cues, using the like/dislike
  expressions: *my cousin / crazy about / play / computer games* · *they /
  interested / play / badminton / after school?* · *I / not fond / make models /
  because / I / not patient* · *why / you / not into / cook? — because / often /
  burn myself* · *my friends / keen / do judo / and / they / go / judo club /
  every Sunday*

### Grammar

- **Ex 3** — five gaps, correct form(s) of the verb: *Does Tom enjoy (cycle)…* ·
  *Some teenagers don't like (read)…* · *Mai detests (play)…* · *Trang and Ann
  love (chat)…* · *What do Nam and Mark prefer (do)…*
- **Ex 4** — a picture-cued cloze passage about Ann: six gaps, verbs *ride,
  read, message, make, knit, play*, with pictures supplying the object.
  Add more words if necessary. Worked example given: *(1. ride) **riding a
  horse***.

### Project — Leisure Time Survey

Groups interview students from their own or other classes with five questions
(When do you have free time? · What do you like doing with your family / with
your friends / on your own? · What would you like to try in the future?),
collect the answers in a six-column table, then report the group's findings
against six guiding questions, including *How many students did you interview?*

- invite and accept invitations.

- listen about leisure activities with friends.
