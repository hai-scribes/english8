Eight of twelve are some form of paragraph, and eleven of twelve run to 80–100 words. Unit 7's notice is the only formal public register and the only longer piece.

One structure, taught once, reused twice

Units 6, 8 and 11 all ask for advantages/disadvantages or a for-and-against stance. Teach that rhetorical move properly around Unit 6 and it pays off twice more — the last time on the year's hardest grammar.

Grammar builds sentence complexity

  • Simple → compound at Unit 3
  • → complex with subordination at Unit 7
  • → reported speech across Units 11–12

Reported speech is the heaviest load of the year and arrives when the calendar is tightest. It deserves the deepest practice bank.

Phonology turns abstract at Unit 9

Units 1–4 are vowel contrasts, 5–8 nasals and consonant clusters — all teachable on paper. From Unit 9 the targets become word stress, sentence stress and intonation, which print cannot carry.

Themes alternate by register

Personal and local topics — leisure, countryside, teenagers, shopping — trade off against civic and global ones. The personal units carry easier lexis, so they absorb harder grammar well.

Listening has no text solution

Every unit's listening topic is known, but the audio ships with the book. Either use the official recordings, or script and record your own. The app works around this with browser speech.

Seven reading text types unverified

Units 2, 3, 4, 8, 9, 11 and 12 have confirmed topics but unconfirmed genres. Topics were recorded and genres left blank rather than guessed.

How to use this

Four habits do most of the work. They are built in, but they only pay off if you let them.

  1. Answer before you reveal. Typing a wrong answer teaches you more than reading the right one.
  2. Come back tomorrow. Fifteen minutes on five days beats two hours on one. The schedule assumes you return.
  3. Speak out loud. Silent reading will not fix /ʊ/ and /uː/. Use the ▶ buttons and copy them.
  4. Overrule the app when it is wrong. If your answer was also correct, say so — it will be remembered.

Grammar — liking & disliking

After these verbs, the next verb changes form. Three groups.

Group 1 — always + V-ing enjoy · don't mind · can't stand · dislike · avoid · practise · fancy I enjoy reading comics. · She can't stand waiting. ✗ I enjoy to read.
Group 2 — both forms, same meaning like · love · hate · prefer I love cooking. = I love to cook.
Group 3 — always + to-V would like · would love · would prefer I would like to try that café. ✗ I would like trying that café.
Bẫy thường gặp: like + V-ing nhưng would like + to-V. I like swimming. = Tôi thích bơi (nói chung).
I would like to swim. = Tôi muốn đi bơi (bây giờ).

Units 2–12

Unit 1 is built. The remaining eleven follow the same seven-lesson template, and each will appear here as a section of this same page — one link, everything inside it.

Why one page: a shared link grants access to that page only. Keeping every unit inside this single page means one share covers the whole course, now and as it grows.

What each unit will carry

  • ~18 vocabulary items with IPA and Vietnamese glosses, seeded with that unit's target sounds
  • Pronunciation training on the unit's contrast, with voice-varied listening
  • Grammar taught as patterns, with graded production exercises
  • An original reading passage on the unit's topic, with recall-after-hiding
  • A listening script read by browser speech
  • The writing task in its correct genre, with a fading scaffold

Open questions before the rollout

  • Is the level right? Unit 1 sits around A2–B1.
  • Is 90 practice items per unit too many, too few?
  • Is the amount of Vietnamese support correct?
  • Units 9–12 need stress and intonation, which browser speech renders imperfectly. That may need a different exercise design.

Method. Built on practice testing and distributed practice — the two techniques Dunlosky et al. (2013) rated high-utility — plus interleaving, the generation effect, and confidence calibration. Pronunciation training rotates between voices because variability is what makes perceptual training generalise.

Scope. This records structure and teaching targets compiled from public sources; it contains none of the textbook's own text, passages or word lists. All study material here is original.
