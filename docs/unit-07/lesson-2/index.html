A Closer Look 1

Vocabulary — Environmental protection

pollution

/pəˈluːʃn/1

noununcountable

the process of making air, water or land dirty and dangerous, or the substances that do this

sự ô nhiễm

  • Air pollution in the city is getting worse.
  • Factories are a major cause of water pollution.
  • We must reduce pollution from traffic.

air pollution · water pollution · reduce pollution · cause pollution

Word family

  • pollute verb to make air, water or land dirty and unsafe làm ô nhiễmThe factory polluted the river for years.
  • pollutant noun a substance that causes pollution chất gây ô nhiễmCars release several pollutants.

Watch out

Uncountable — never pollutions. Say a lot of pollution or high levels of pollution.

deforestation

/ˌdiːˌfɒrɪˈsteɪʃn/2

noununcountable

the cutting down of forests over a large area

nạn phá rừng

  • Deforestation destroys the habitat of many species.
  • Deforestation is a major cause of flooding.
  • The government has promised to slow deforestation.

cause deforestation · stop deforestation · the rate of deforestation

Watch out

Built from de- (removing) + forest + -ation. Stress: de-for-es-TA-tion.

endangered

/ɪnˈdeɪndʒəd/3

adjective

at risk of disappearing completely, especially a kind of animal or plant

có nguy cơ tuyệt chủng

  • The Saola is an endangered species.
  • Many endangered animals live in this park.
  • Tigers became endangered through hunting.

an endangered species · critically endangered

Word family

  • endanger verb to put someone or something at risk gây nguy hiểm choPlastic waste endangers sea turtles.

Watch out

Extinct is the next stage: an endangered species still exists; an extinct one is gone.

habitat

/ˈhæbɪtæt/4

nouncountable

the natural place where a plant or animal normally lives

môi trường sống (của sinh vật)

  • The forest is the habitat of many rare birds.
  • Building roads destroys natural habitats.
  • This frog's habitat is the mountain stream.

a natural habitat · destroy a habitat · habitat loss

Watch out

A habitat belongs to an animal or plant. The wider surroundings we all share is the environment.

species

/ˈspiːʃiːz/5

nouncountable, plural: species

a group of animals or plants of the same kind that can breed together

loài

  • This species is found only in Viet Nam.
  • Hundreds of species are lost every year.
  • Scientists discovered a new species of frog.

an endangered species · a rare species · a new species

Watch out

The plural is also species — never specie or specieses. One species, two species.

conservation

/ˌkɒnsəˈveɪʃn/6

noununcountable

the protection of the natural world, or the careful use of something so it is not wasted

sự bảo tồn, bảo vệ (thiên nhiên)

  • She works in wildlife conservation.
  • Conservation of water matters most in the dry season.
  • The park was set up for the conservation of rare plants.

wildlife conservation · energy conservation · a conservation project

Word family

  • conserve verb to use something carefully so it is not wasted, or to protect nature bảo tồn, tiết kiệmTurn off the tap to conserve water.

litter

/ˈlɪtə(r)/7
1

noununcountable

waste paper, cans and other rubbish left lying in a public place

rác vứt bừa bãi

  • There was litter all over the beach.
  • Please don't drop litter in the park.
  • Volunteers collected three bags of litter.

drop litter · pick up litter · a litter bin

2

verbtransitive / intransitive

to leave rubbish lying in a public place

xả rác

  • People who litter can be fined.
  • The path was littered with plastic bottles.

littered with rubbish

Watch out

Uncountable — never litters. Litter is rubbish in the wrong place; rubbish (BrE) / garbage (AmE) is waste in general.

recycle

/ˌriːˈsaɪkl/8

verbtransitive

to treat used materials so that they can be made into something new

tái chế

  • We recycle paper, glass and plastic at home.
  • These bags are made from recycled bottles.
  • Only a small part of our waste is recycled.

recycle paper · recycled materials · a recycling bin

Word family

  • recycling noun the process of recycling materials việc tái chếRecycling has doubled in ten years.

Watch out

One of the three Rs: reduce, reuse, recycle — in that order of usefulness.

reduce

/rɪˈdjuːs/9

verbtransitive

to make something smaller in size, amount or number

giảm, giảm bớt

  • We should reduce the amount of plastic we use.
  • Cycling to school reduces pollution.
  • The shop has reduced its prices.

reduce waste · reduce pollution · greatly reduce

Word family

  • reduction noun the act of making something smaller sự giảm bớtThere has been a reduction in litter.

Watch out

Opposite: increase. Stress the second syllable: re-DUCE.

reuse

/ˌriːˈjuːz/10

verbtransitive

to use something again instead of throwing it away

tái sử dụng

  • Reuse your water bottle instead of buying a new one.
  • These jars can be reused for storage.
  • We reuse the paper by printing on both sides.

reuse a bag · reuse materials

Watch out

Reuse means use again as it is; recycle means break it down and make something new. The verb is /ˌriːˈjuːz/ with a /z/; the noun reuse ends in /s/.

sewage

/ˈsuːɪdʒ/11

noununcountable

waste water and human waste carried away in pipes

nước thải, nước cống

  • Untreated sewage was flowing into the river.
  • The town is building a new sewage treatment plant.
  • Sewage must be cleaned before it reaches the sea.

raw sewage · sewage treatment · untreated sewage

Watch out

Pronounced /ˈsuːɪdʒ/: SOO-ij. Uncountable — never sewages.

pesticide

/ˈpestɪsaɪd/12

nouncountable / uncountable

a chemical used to kill insects and other pests that damage crops

thuốc trừ sâu

  • Too much pesticide contaminates the soil.
  • Pesticides can harm bees as well as pests.
  • The farm has stopped using chemical pesticides.

use pesticides · chemical pesticides · pesticide residues

Watch out

The ending -cide means killing: compare insecticide, herbicide.

contaminate

/kənˈtæmɪneɪt/13

verbtransitive

to make something dirty, polluted or poisonous by adding a harmful substance

làm nhiễm bẩn, làm ô nhiễm

  • Chemicals from the factory contaminated the water.
  • The soil has been contaminated by pesticides.
  • Don't let the raw meat contaminate the vegetables.

contaminate the water · heavily contaminated

Word family

  • contamination noun the state of being made impure or poisonous sự nhiễm bẩnTests showed serious contamination.

protect

/prəˈtekt/14

verbtransitive, + from / against

to keep someone or something safe from harm or damage

bảo vệ

  • We must protect the environment for future generations.
  • The park protects several rare species.
  • Sunscreen protects your skin from the sun.

protect the environment · protect wildlife · protect against damage

Word family

  • protection noun the act of keeping something safe sự bảo vệThese birds are under legal protection.
  • protective adjective giving protection có tính bảo vệWear protective gloves.

preserve

/prɪˈzɜːv/15
1

verbtransitive

to keep something in its original state so that it is not lost or damaged

bảo tồn, gìn giữ

  • The village wants to preserve its traditions.
  • These forests must be preserved for the future.
  • The old town has been carefully preserved.

preserve a tradition · preserve the environment · well-preserved

2

verbtransitive

to treat food so that it can be kept for a long time

bảo quản (thực phẩm)

  • Salt is used to preserve the fish.
  • Fruit can be preserved in sugar.

preserve food · preserved fruit

Word family

  • preservation noun the act of keeping something safe or unchanged sự bảo tồnThe preservation of folk songs matters.

Watch out

Compare conserve (use carefully so it is not wasted) with preserve (keep unchanged).

national park

/ˌnæʃnəl ˈpɑːk/16

nouncountable

a large area of countryside protected by the government for its nature and beauty

vườn quốc gia

  • Cuc Phuong is Viet Nam's oldest national park.
  • Hunting is forbidden inside the national park.
  • We camped in the national park for two nights.

visit a national park · protect a national park

wildlife

/ˈwaɪldlaɪf/17

noununcountable

animals and plants that live and grow in natural conditions

động thực vật hoang dã

  • The park is home to rare wildlife.
  • Pollution threatens wildlife in the river.
  • She photographs wildlife for a living.

protect wildlife · wildlife conservation · a wildlife reserve

Watch out

Uncountable and written as one word — never wildlifes.

awareness

/əˈweənəs/18

noununcountable, + of

knowing that something exists and being interested in it

sự nhận thức, ý thức

  • The campaign raised awareness of plastic waste.
  • There is growing awareness of climate change.
  • The poster aims to increase public awareness.

raise awareness · public awareness · environmental awareness

Word family

  • aware adjective knowing that something exists or is true nhận thức được, biếtAre you aware of the new rules?

Watch out

The usual verb is raise awareness, not make or rise.

Pronunciation — /bl/ and /kl/

Both are consonant clusters: two consonants said together, with no vowel between them. The second sound is /l/ in both. Only the first sound differs.

/bl/ — blue /kl/ — clean
First sound /b/ — lips together, voiced (throat buzzes) /k/ — back of tongue, voiceless (no buzz)
Then slide straight into /l/ slide straight into /l/
Usual spelling bl cl
Examples blue, black, blow, blame, problem, terrible, possible clean, clear, climate, close, clothes, recycle, include, cycle

Mẹo: Đặt tay lên cổ họng. Với /b/ trong blue bạn thấy rung; với /k/ trong clean thì không. Đừng thêm nguyên âm vào giữa: blue là một hơi, không phải "bơ-lu". Cụm /bl/ và /kl/ cũng xuất hiện ở cuối từ: possible, recy**cle.

⚠️ Hai cái bẫy chính tả: rubbish /ˈrʌbɪʃ/ — có chữ b nhưng không có cụm /bl/. Sau /b/ là nguyên âm /ɪ/. chemical /ˈkemɪkl/ — ch ở đầu đọc là /k/ không có /l/ theo sau. (Cụm /kl/ trong từ này nằm ở cuối, phần -cal.)

Beyond the textbookPronunciationCEFR A2

Same drill as Unit 6 — but the support here is weaker, and this says so

Work /bl/ and /kl/ the way you worked /br/ and /pr/ in Unit 6: the voice and the puff of air on the first consonant, not the cluster.

The honest difference, stated rather than borrowed: the Vietnamese study behind the Unit 6 framing tested five onset clusters — /br/, /kr/, /pr/, /st/, /tr/. /bl/ and /kl/ were not among them. Applying its finding here is an extension from /kr/, not a direct result, and this unit does not get to reuse Unit 6's warrant as if it were its own.

What the study does establish, for the clusters it did test, is that the dominant error is feature change on the plosive (77.4%) rather than deletion (22.2%). One source, 36 learners, not independently re-checked.

Tiếng Việt: Cách luyện giống Unit 6, nhưng bằng chứng ở đây yếu hơn: nghiên cứu gốc không kiểm tra /bl/ và /kl/, chỉ suy rộng từ /kr/.
