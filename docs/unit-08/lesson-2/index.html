A Closer Look 1

Vocabulary — Shopping

shopping centre

/ˈʃɒpɪŋ ˌsentə(r)/1

nouncountable

a large building or group of buildings containing many different shops

trung tâm mua sắm

  • We meet at the shopping centre near the swimming pool.
  • The new shopping centre has a cinema on the top floor.
  • It rained, so we spent the afternoon in the shopping centre.

go to the shopping centre · a new shopping centre

Watch out

British English. Americans say shopping mall or just the mall. Note the British spelling centre.

department store

/dɪˈpɑːtmənt stɔː(r)/2

nouncountable

a large shop divided into sections, each selling a different type of goods

cửa hàng bách hoá

  • She bought the shoes in a department store.
  • The department store has six floors.
  • Clothes are on the second floor of the department store.

a large department store · shop at a department store

Watch out

A department store is one shop with many sections; a shopping centre is many separate shops under one roof.

street vendor

/ˌstriːt ˈvendə(r)/3

nouncountable

a person who sells food or goods from a stall or cart in the street

người bán hàng rong

  • A street vendor was selling banh mi on the corner.
  • Street vendors line the road near the market.
  • We bought sugarcane juice from a street vendor.

buy from a street vendor · a food vendor

Word family

  • vendor noun a person or company that sells something người bánThe vendor gave us a discount.

stall

/stɔːl/4

nouncountable

a table or small open shop in a market or at a fair, where things are sold

quầy hàng, sạp hàng

  • Her family runs a fruit stall in the market.
  • There were twenty stalls at the school fair.
  • The stall opens at five in the morning.

a market stall · run a stall · a food stall

customer

/ˈkʌstəmə(r)/5

nouncountable

a person who buys goods or services from a shop or business

khách hàng

  • The shop was full of customers on Saturday.
  • A good shop looks after its customers.
  • She is one of our regular customers.

a regular customer · customer service · serve a customer

Watch out

A customer buys goods; a client buys professional services; a guest stays at a hotel.

bargain

/ˈbɑːɡɪn/6
1

nouncountable

something bought for much less than its usual price

món hời, món mua được giá rẻ

  • These shoes were a real bargain at 200,000 dong.
  • The sale is full of bargains.
  • It's a bargain — buy it!

a real bargain · a bargain price · look for bargains

2

verbintransitive, + with / over

to discuss a price with a seller in order to pay less

mặc cả, trả giá

  • You are expected to bargain at the market.
  • He bargained with the vendor for ten minutes.
  • She bargained the price down to half.

bargain over the price · bargain with a seller

Watch out

In British shops the price is fixed and you do not bargain; at a market you often can. Haggle means the same as the verb.

discount

/ˈdɪskaʊnt/7

nouncountable

an amount taken off the usual price

sự giảm giá, chiết khấu

  • Students get a 10% discount.
  • They offered me a discount because the box was damaged.
  • There is a discount on all winter coats.

get a discount · offer a discount · a 20% discount

Watch out

Stress the noun on the first syllable (DIS-count) and the verb on the second (dis-COUNT).

refund

/ˈriːfʌnd/8
1

nouncountable

money that is given back to you, especially because you have returned goods

tiền hoàn lại

  • I took the shirt back and asked for a refund.
  • You can get a full refund within 14 days.
  • They gave me a refund without any trouble.

ask for a refund · get a full refund · a refund policy

2

verbtransitive

to give someone their money back

hoàn tiền

  • The shop refunded the full amount.
  • They will refund you if the item is faulty.

refund the money

Watch out

Keep the receipt — most shops will not give a refund without one.

receipt

/rɪˈsiːt/9

nouncountable

a piece of paper showing that you have paid for something

hoá đơn, biên lai

  • Keep the receipt in case you want a refund.
  • The cashier printed the receipt.
  • I lost the receipt, so I couldn't return it.

keep the receipt · ask for a receipt

Watch out

The p is silent: /rɪˈsiːt/, ri-SEET. A receipt proves you paid; a bill (BrE) or check (AmE) asks you to pay.

cash

/kæʃ/10

noununcountable

money in the form of coins and notes rather than cards

tiền mặt

  • Do you want to pay by card or in cash?
  • Many street vendors only take cash.
  • I don't have enough cash on me.

pay in cash · pay cash · cash only

Watch out

Uncountable — never cashes. Say pay in cash or pay cash, but pay by card.

in stock

/ɪn ˈstɒk/11

phrase

available in the shop and ready to be bought

còn hàng, có sẵn

  • Do you have this jacket in stock in a smaller size?
  • The book is in stock at the city branch.
  • Sorry, we don't have that colour in stock.

have something in stock · back in stock

Watch out

Opposite: out of stock or sold out.

delivery

/dɪˈlɪvəri/12

nouncountable / uncountable

the act of taking goods to the person who has bought them

việc giao hàng

  • Delivery is free on orders over 500,000 dong.
  • The delivery arrived two days late.
  • They offer next-day delivery.

free delivery · next-day delivery · a delivery charge

Word family

  • deliver verb to take goods to a person or place giao hàngThey deliver on Sundays too.

browse

/braʊz/13
1

verbintransitive

to look at things in a shop without intending to buy anything in particular

xem hàng, ngắm hàng

  • We spent an hour browsing in the bookshop.
  • I'm just browsing, thank you.
  • She likes to browse before she decides.

browse in a shop · just browsing

2

verbtransitive / intransitive

to look through pages on the internet

lướt web, duyệt web

  • He was browsing the web on his phone.
  • I browsed a few online shops last night.

browse the internet · browse a website

Word family

  • browser noun a program used to look at internet pages trình duyệtOpen the link in a different browser.

Watch out

I'm just browsing is the polite English answer when a shop assistant asks if you need help.

try on

/ˌtraɪ ˈɒn/14

phrasal verbseparable

to put on a piece of clothing to see whether it fits and suits you

mặc thử, thử (quần áo)

  • Can I try these shoes on?
  • She tried on four dresses and bought none.
  • Try it on before you buy it.

try on a jacket · a changing room to try things on

Watch out

Separable, and with a pronoun it must split: try it on, never try on it.

queue

/kjuː/15
1

nouncountable

a line of people waiting for something

hàng người xếp hàng

  • There was a long queue at the till.
  • Join the queue over there.
  • The queue moved surprisingly quickly.

join a queue · a long queue · jump the queue

2

verbintransitive, + for / up

to wait in a line

xếp hàng

  • We queued for twenty minutes.
  • People were queuing up outside the shop.

queue for tickets · queue up

Watch out

British English; Americans say line and stand in line. Pronounced simply /kjuː/ — like the letter Q; the last four letters are silent.

convenient

/kənˈviːniənt/16

adjective

useful and easy because it saves you time or trouble

tiện lợi, thuận tiện

  • Online shopping is very convenient.
  • The shop is convenient for the bus station.
  • Would Saturday be convenient for you?

convenient for · a convenient time · quick and convenient

Word family

  • convenience noun the quality of being easy and useful sự tiện lợiPeople shop online for convenience.

Watch out

Opposite: inconvenient. Stress the second syllable: con-VEN-ient.

expensive

/ɪkˈspensɪv/17

adjective

costing a lot of money

đắt, đắt đỏ

  • That jacket is too expensive for me.
  • Fruit is more expensive in winter.
  • It was the most expensive thing in the shop.

very expensive · too expensive · an expensive mistake

Watch out

Opposite: cheap or inexpensive. Things are expensive; prices are high, not expensive.

purchase

/ˈpɜːtʃəs/18
1

verbtransitive, formal

to buy something

mua (trang trọng)

  • Tickets may be purchased online.
  • The company purchased new equipment.
  • Goods purchased in the sale cannot be returned.

purchase tickets · purchase online

2

nouncountable, formal

something you have bought, or the act of buying it

món hàng đã mua; việc mua

  • Keep your receipt as proof of purchase.
  • She was pleased with her purchases.

proof of purchase · make a purchase

Watch out

Formal. In everyday speech use buy: you buy a coffee, but a company purchases equipment. Pronounced /ˈpɜːtʃəs/: PUR-chuss.

Pronunciation — /sp/ and /st/

Both are consonant clusters at the start of a syllable: /s/ followed immediately by another consonant, with no vowel between them. Vietnamese has no clusters like this, so learners often add a small vowel ("sờ-pend") or drop the /s/ altogether.

/sp/ — spend /st/ — store
Second sound /p/ — both lips close, then open /t/ — tongue tip touches the ridge behind the teeth
Where you meet it spend, special, spare, sport, display, expensive store, stall, stock, style, customer, mistake, instead
Full list to drill spend, special, spare, sport, display, expensive store, stall, stock, style, customer, mistake, instead

Notice that the cluster does not have to start the word: display, expensive → /ɪkˈspensɪv/, customer → /ˈkʌstəmə(r)/, mistake → /mɪˈsteɪk/, instead → /ɪnˈsted/.

Mẹo: Sau /s/, âm /p/ và /t/ mất hơi bật (không bật hơi). Vì vậy spend nghe gần như "sbend" và store nghe gần như "sdore" với tai người Việt. Đó là điều bình thường — đừng cố bật hơi mạnh. Điều quan trọng là không thêm nguyên âm vào giữa /s/ và phụ âm sau nó.

2.5

Say these sentences

Read aloud three times, faster each time. Do not put a vowel before the /p/ or the /t/. Record yourself if you can.

  1. I never spend much in that store.
  2. The special stall is next to the sports shop.
  3. It was a mistake — the style on display is too expensive.
  4. That customer paid in cash instead.

Answers will vary — this is a speaking task. Check: no vowel sound between /s/ and /p/ or between /s/ and /t/ (not "sờ-pend"), and no aspiration puff on the /p/ or /t/.
