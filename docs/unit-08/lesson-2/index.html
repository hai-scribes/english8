A Closer Look 1

Vocabulary — Shopping

shopping centre

/ˈʃɒpɪŋ ˌsentə(r)/1

nouncountable

a large building or group of buildings containing many different shops

trung tâm mua sắm

  • We meet at the shopping centre near the swimming pool.
  • The new shopping centre has a cinema on the top floor.
  • It rained, so we spent the afternoon in the shopping centre.

go to the shopping centre · a new shopping centre

Watch out

British English. Americans say shopping mall or just the mall. Note the British spelling centre.

department store

/dɪˈpɑːtmənt stɔː(r)/2

nouncountable

a large shop divided into sections, each selling a different type of goods

cửa hàng bách hoá

  • She bought the shoes in a department store.
  • The department store has six floors.
  • Clothes are on the second floor of the department store.

a large department store · shop at a department store

Watch out

A department store is one shop with many sections; a shopping centre is many separate shops under one roof.

street vendor

/ˌstriːt ˈvendə(r)/3

nouncountable

a person who sells food or goods from a stall or cart in the street

người bán hàng rong

  • A street vendor was selling banh mi on the corner.
  • Street vendors line the road near the market.
  • We bought sugarcane juice from a street vendor.

buy from a street vendor · a food vendor

Word family

  • vendor noun a person or company that sells something người bánThe vendor gave us a discount.

stall

/stɔːl/4

nouncountable

a table or small open shop in a market or at a fair, where things are sold

quầy hàng, sạp hàng

  • Her family runs a fruit stall in the market.
  • There were twenty stalls at the school fair.
  • The stall opens at five in the morning.

a market stall · run a stall · a food stall

customer

/ˈkʌstəmə(r)/5

nouncountable

a person who buys goods or services from a shop or business

khách hàng

  • The shop was full of customers on Saturday.
  • A good shop looks after its customers.
  • She is one of our regular customers.

a regular customer · customer service · serve a customer

Watch out

A customer buys goods; a client buys professional services; a guest stays at a hotel.

bargain

/ˈbɑːɡɪn/6
1

nouncountable

something bought for much less than its usual price

món hời, món mua được giá rẻ

  • These shoes were a real bargain at 200,000 dong.
  • The sale is full of bargains.
  • It's a bargain — buy it!

a real bargain · a bargain price · look for bargains

2

verbintransitive, + with / over

to discuss a price with a seller in order to pay less

mặc cả, trả giá

  • He bargained with the vendor for ten minutes.
  • She bargained the price down to half.

bargain over the price · bargain with a seller

Watch out

In British shops the price is fixed and you do not bargain; at a market you often can. Haggle means the same as the verb.

discount

/ˈdɪskaʊnt/7

nouncountable

an amount taken off the usual price

sự giảm giá, chiết khấu

  • Students get a 10% discount.
  • They offered me a discount because the box was damaged.
  • There is a discount on all winter coats.

get a discount · offer a discount · a 20% discount

Watch out

Stress the noun on the first syllable (DIS-count) and the verb on the second (dis-COUNT).

refund

/ˈriːfʌnd/8
1

nouncountable

money that is given back to you, especially because you have returned goods

tiền hoàn lại

  • I took the shirt back and asked for a refund.
  • You can get a full refund within 14 days.
  • They gave me a refund without any trouble.

ask for a refund · get a full refund · a refund policy

2

verbtransitive

to give someone their money back

hoàn tiền

  • The shop refunded the full amount.
  • They will refund you if the item is faulty.

refund the money

Watch out

Keep the receipt — most shops will not give a refund without one.

receipt

/rɪˈsiːt/9

nouncountable

a piece of paper showing that you have paid for something

hoá đơn, biên lai

  • Keep the receipt in case you want a refund.
  • The cashier printed the receipt.
  • I lost the receipt, so I couldn't return it.

keep the receipt · ask for a receipt

Watch out

The p is silent: /rɪˈsiːt/, ri-SEET. A receipt proves you paid; a bill (BrE) or check (AmE) asks you to pay.

cash

/kæʃ/10

noununcountable

money in the form of coins and notes rather than cards

tiền mặt

  • Do you want to pay by card or in cash?
  • Many street vendors only take cash.
  • I don't have enough cash on me.

pay in cash · pay cash · cash only

Watch out

Uncountable — never cashes. Say pay in cash or pay cash, but pay by card.

in stock

have something in stock · back in stock

Opposite: out of stock or sold out.

browse in a shop · just browsing

try on

/ˌtraɪ ˈɒn/14

phrasal verbseparable

to put on a piece of clothing to see whether it fits and suits you

mặc thử, thử (quần áo)

  • Can I try these shoes on?
  • She tried on four dresses and bought none.
  • Try it on before you buy it.

try on a jacket · a changing room to try things on

Watch out

Separable, and with a pronoun it must split: try it on, never try on it.

queue

/kjuː/15
1

nouncountable

a line of people waiting for something

hàng người xếp hàng

  • There was a long queue at the till.
  • Join the queue over there.
  • The queue moved surprisingly quickly.

join a queue · a long queue · jump the queue

2

verbintransitive, + for / up

to wait in a line

xếp hàng

  • We queued for twenty minutes.
  • People were queuing up outside the shop.

queue for tickets · queue up

British English; Americans say line and stand in line. Pronounced simply /kjuː/ — like the letter Q; the last four letters are silent.

convenient

/kənˈviːniənt/16

adjective

useful and easy because it saves you time or trouble

tiện lợi, thuận tiện

  • Online shopping is very convenient.
  • The shop is convenient for the bus station.
  • Would Saturday be convenient for you?

convenient for · a convenient time · quick and convenient

Word family

  • convenience noun the quality of being easy and useful sự tiện lợiPeople shop online for convenience.

Opposite: inconvenient. Stress the second syllable: con-VEN-ient.

expensive

/ɪkˈspensɪv/17

adjective

costing a lot of money

đắt, đắt đỏ

  • That jacket is too expensive for me.
  • Fruit is more expensive in winter.
  • It was the most expensive thing in the shop.

very expensive · too expensive · an expensive mistake

Watch out

Opposite: cheap or inexpensive. Things are expensive; prices are high, not expensive.

purchase

/ˈpɜːtʃəs/18
1

verbtransitive, formal

to buy something

mua (trang trọng)

  • Tickets may be purchased online.
  • The company purchased new equipment.
  • Goods purchased in the sale cannot be returned.

purchase tickets · purchase online

2

nouncountable, formal

something you have bought, or the act of buying it

món hàng đã mua; việc mua

  • Keep your receipt as proof of purchase.
  • She was pleased with her purchases.

proof of purchase · make a purchase

Watch out

Formal. In everyday speech use buy: you buy a coffee, but a company purchases equipment. Pronounced /ˈpɜːtʃəs/: PUR-chuss.

price tag

/ˈpraɪs tæɡ/19
1

nouncountable

a small label fixed to something in a shop, showing what it costs

nhãn ghi giá một mặt hàng

  • Every item on the shelf has a price tag, so nobody bargains here.
  • Turn the mug over — the price tag is on the bottom.
  • She put the shirt back when she saw the price tag.

check the price tag · look at the price tag · have a price tag

2

nouncountable, figurative

the cost of something large, especially when the cost is surprising

cái giá phải trả (nghĩa bóng)

  • The new shopping centre came with a price tag of forty million dollars.
  • Free delivery sounds good, but it carries a hidden price tag.

a price tag of · a hidden price tag

Watch out

Two words. A price tag is the label; the price is the number on it. Fixed price tags are what tell a supermarket from an open-air market, where goods carry none and buyers bargain.

on sale

/ɒn ˈseɪl/20

phrase

being sold for less than the usual price, for a short time

đang được bán hạ giá

  • These jeans are on sale this week: 400,000 dong instead of 600,000.
  • Winter coats go on sale in February.
  • I only bought it because it was on sale.

go on sale · be on sale · items on sale

Watch out

In British English on sale means reduced. To say that something can be bought at all, use for sale: That house is for sale. American English blurs the two, so read the price before you celebrate.

home-grown

/ˌhəʊm ˈɡrəʊn/21
1

adjective

grown at home or in your own garden or fields, not bought from a supplier

tự trồng

  • The tomatoes are home-grown — my grandfather grows them behind the house.
  • Most products at the fair were home-grown and cost very little.
  • She sells home-grown herbs at the farmers' market on Saturdays.

home-grown vegetables · home-grown fruit · home-grown rice

2

adjectivefigurative

coming from your own country or town rather than from abroad

cây nhà lá vườn, của địa phương

  • The team is proud of its home-grown players.
  • The festival gives home-grown musicians a stage.

home-grown talent · a home-grown player

Watch out

Keep the hyphen. Use it for things that grow: vegetables, fruit, herbs, rice. Cakes and jam are not grown — they are home-made.

home-made

/ˌhəʊm ˈmeɪd/22

adjective

made at home rather than bought from a shop or a factory

tự làm, làm ở nhà

  • My aunt bakes the cakes in her own kitchen, so they are home-made.
  • The stall sells home-made jam in old jam jars.
  • He took a jar of home-made chilli sauce back to school with him.

home-made bread · home-made jam · a home-made card

Watch out

Keep the hyphen. home-made = made by hand at home; home-grown = grown at home. A home-made cake may be made from vegetables that are home-grown.

shopaholic

/ˌʃɒpəˈhɒlɪk/23

nouncountable, informal

someone who shops far more than they need to and finds it hard to stop

người nghiện mua sắm

  • She buys three pairs of shoes a month — she is a real shopaholic.
  • Online shops make it easy to become a shopaholic.
  • My cousin calls herself a shopaholic, but she never spends much.

become a shopaholic · a bit of a shopaholic

Word family

  • shopaholism noun the habit of shopping too much chứng nghiện mua sắmThe talk was about shopaholism and how to spend less.

Watch out

Informal, and built from -aholic, the ending of alcoholic. The same ending gives workaholic and chocoholic. A shopaholic is addicted to shopping.

convenience store

/kənˈviːniəns stɔː(r)/24

nouncountable

a small shop that sells food and everyday goods and stays open very long hours

cửa hàng tiện ích

  • I need milk at eleven at night, so I go to the convenience store on the corner.
  • The convenience store near school is open 24/7.
  • A convenience store is quick, but its prices are higher than a supermarket's.

a 24-hour convenience store · run to the convenience store

Watch out

You pay for the convenience: the shop is small, the range of products is narrow, and the prices are higher than in a supermarket. Note that the adjective convenient loses the -ce: a convenient shop, but a convenience store.

speciality shop

/ˌspeʃiˈæləti ʃɒp/25

nouncountable

a shop that sells only one or two particular kinds of goods

cửa hàng bán đồ chuyên dụng

  • A florist's is a speciality shop: it sells flowers and very little else.
  • For running shoes she goes to a speciality shop, not a department store.
  • The old quarter is full of speciality shops — one street sells nothing but lanterns.

a speciality shop · a speciality food shop

Watch out

British speciality /ˌspeʃiˈæləti/ has five syllables; American English writes and says specialty /ˈspeʃəlti/ with three. The opposite kind of shop is a department store, which sells a wide range of products under one roof.

dollar store

/ˈdɒlə(r) ˌstɔː(r)/26

nouncountable

a shop where everything costs the same low price, or close to it

cửa hàng đồng giá

  • Everything in this shop is one dollar, so we call it a dollar store.
  • We bought the party plates and cups at a dollar store.
  • A dollar store is a cheap place to buy things you will only use once.

shop at a dollar store · a dollar store item

Watch out

Mainly American. British English says pound shop, and Vietnamese shops of this kind advertise as cửa hàng đồng giá. It is not the same as a discount shop, which sells ordinary goods at reduced prices rather than everything at one price.

farmers' market

/ˈfɑːməz ˌmɑːkɪt/27

nouncountable

a market, usually held on the same day each week, where farmers sell what they have grown themselves

chợ nông sản

  • The farmers sell their own vegetables at the farmers' market every Saturday.
  • My mother loves the farmers' market and rarely misses one.
  • Fruit at a farmers' market is picked the day before, not a week before.

a weekly farmers' market · go to the farmers' market

Watch out

The apostrophe comes after the s: the market belongs to the farmers, and there is more than one of them. Write farmers' market, not farmer's market or farmers market.

range of products

/ˌreɪndʒ əv ˈprɒdʌkts/28

phrase

the number of different kinds of goods a shop offers

các loại mặt hàng

  • A big supermarket offers a wide range of products, from rice to raincoats.
  • The range of products in a convenience store is small.
  • Online shops can carry a huge range of products because they need no shelves.

a wide range of products · a narrow range of products · offer a range of products

Watch out

Wide, not big or large: a wide range of products. The phrase is about variety, not quantity — a shop with a thousand identical umbrellas has a very narrow range.

quảng cáo

addicted (to)

Pronunciation — /sp/ and /st/

Both are consonant clusters at the start of a syllable: /s/ followed immediately by another consonant, with no vowel between them. Vietnamese has no clusters like this, so learners often add a small vowel ("sờ-pend") or drop the /s/ altogether.

/sp/ — spend /st/ — store
Second sound /p/ — both lips close, then open /t/ — tongue tip touches the ridge behind the teeth
Where you meet it spend, special, spare, sport, display, expensive store, stall, stock, style, customer, mistake, instead
Full list to drill spend, special, spare, sport, display, expensive store, stall, stock, style, customer, mistake, instead

Notice that the cluster does not have to start the word: display, expensive → /ɪkˈspensɪv/, customer → /ˈkʌstəmə(r)/, mistake → /mɪˈsteɪk/, instead → /ɪnˈsted/.

Mẹo: Sau /s/, âm /p/ và /t/ mất hơi bật (không bật hơi). Vì vậy spend nghe gần như "sbend" và store nghe gần như "sdore" với tai người Việt. Đó là điều bình thường — đừng cố bật hơi mạnh. Điều quan trọng là không thêm nguyên âm vào giữa /s/ và phụ âm sau nó.
