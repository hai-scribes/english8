Error hunt

This paragraph has seven mistakes. Read it, then put them right one line at a time below.

Four marks are left on the wall, and the stranger will not say his name. The market is still open, though. Bà Sáu goes twice a week there, and she usually get there before the rice truck. She can't hardly ever pay by card, because only two stalls take cards. Always she takes me with her when there is a discount. I have seen never so many discounts as this week. The new rice stall opens at five tomorrow and close at noon. The big sale begin at eight tomorrow.

MarkedCorrect the mistake

Each sentence has exactly one mistake. Tap the wrong word, then choose what should replace it. Each line is part of the paragraph, with the earlier mistakes already put right.

  • One mark each. Nothing is part-marked.
  1. twice a week there → there twice a week (the place comes before a frequency expression, and the frequency expression goes at the end)
  2. get → gets (the adverb does not change the verb: *she gets*)
  3. can't → can (*hardly ever* is already negative)
  4. Always she takes → She always takes (*always* cannot start the sentence)
  5. seen never → never seen (between the auxiliary and the main verb)
  6. close → closes (a timetable, so the present simple — with *-s*)
  7. begin → begins (a timetable, so the present simple — with *-s*)

Project — A shopping survey

Find out how your class, your family or your street really shops.

Step 1 — Ask. Interview eight people. Use these questions and record the answers in the table.

# Question
1 How often do you go to a market?
2 How often do you go to a shopping centre?
3 How often do you buy something online?
4 Which do you think is the most convenient? Why?
Person Market Centre Online Most convenient

…

Step 2 — Count it. For each of the three kinds of shopping, count how many people answered every day, once a week, once a month, or never.

Step 3 — Write it up. Write a report of 100–120 words. Use an adverb of frequency or a frequency expression in every sentence about your results. Answer:

  • Which kind of shopping is the most popular in your group?
  • Which is the least popular, and why do people avoid it?
  • Was any answer a surprise?

Step 4 — Present it. Present your findings aloud for one minute, without reading word for word. Record it. Listen back once and check your adverb positions.

Stretch: Ask the same eight people again in six months. Has online shopping gone up or down? Write three sentences comparing the two surveys.
