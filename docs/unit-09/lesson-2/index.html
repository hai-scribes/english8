A Closer Look 1

Vocabulary — Natural disasters

earthquake

/ˈɜːθkweɪk/1

nouncountable

a sudden violent shaking of the ground that can knock down buildings

trận động đất

  • A strong earthquake hit the city early on Sunday morning.
  • Thousands of people lost their homes in the earthquake.
  • During an earthquake, get under a table and hold on.

a strong earthquake · an earthquake hits · survive an earthquake

Watch out

Stress the first part: EARTHquake. In news reports you may also see the shorter word quake, which means the same thing.

flood

/flʌd/2
1

nouncountable

a large amount of water covering land that is usually dry

trận lụt, lũ lụt

  • The floods last October destroyed most of the rice crop.
  • Heavy rain caused a serious flood in the north.
  • After the flood, mud was everywhere in our street.

a serious flood · cause a flood · flood water

2

verbtransitive/intransitive

to fill a place with water, or to become full of water

làm ngập, bị ngập nước

  • The river flooded the whole village in one night.
  • Our kitchen floods every rainy season.
  • The road was flooded, so the bus could not pass.

be badly flooded · flood a village

Watch out

Pronounced /flʌd/ — it rhymes with blood, not with food. The adjective is flooded: a flooded street.

drought

/draʊt/3

nouncountable/uncountable

a long period of time with little or no rain, so that plants and animals suffer

hạn hán

  • The drought lasted for eight months and the wells dried up.
  • Farmers lost their coffee plants in the drought.
  • Long periods of drought are becoming more common.

a severe drought · suffer from drought · a long drought

Watch out

Pronounced /draʊt/ — the gh is silent and the ending sounds like out. Do not confuse it with draught (a cold current of air).

landslide

/ˈlændslaɪd/4
1

nouncountable

a mass of earth, mud and rock that falls down the side of a hill or mountain

sạt lở đất

  • A landslide blocked the mountain road for three days.
  • Heavy rain often causes landslides in this province.
  • Two houses were buried by the landslide.

cause a landslide · a landslide blocks a road · a deadly landslide

2

nounusually singular

a very large victory in an election

thắng lợi áp đảo (trong bầu cử)

  • Her party won the election by a landslide.
  • It was a landslide victory for the young candidate.

win by a landslide · a landslide victory

Watch out

The first meaning is the one you need in this unit. Mudslide is a similar word used when the material is mostly wet mud.

tsunami

/tsuːˈnɑːmi/5

nouncountable

an extremely large ocean wave, usually caused by an earthquake under the sea

sóng thần

  • The tsunami reached the coast twenty minutes after the earthquake.
  • A huge tsunami swept away hundreds of small boats.
  • Sirens warned the villagers that a tsunami was coming.

a huge tsunami · a tsunami warning · be hit by a tsunami

Watch out

Pronounced /suːˈnɑːmi/ — the t is not pronounced in English, so it begins like sue. The word comes from Japanese. An older word for it, tidal wave, is not really correct because tides do not cause it.

volcano

/vɒlˈkeɪnəʊ/6

nouncountable

a mountain with a hole at the top through which hot melted rock, gas and ash come out

núi lửa

  • Smoke rose from the volcano for several weeks.
  • There are more than a hundred active volcanoes in that country.
  • We climbed to the top of an old volcano.

an active volcano · a volcano erupts · climb a volcano

Word family

  • volcanic adjective connected with or produced by a volcano thuộc về núi lửaVolcanic ash covered the fields for miles.

Watch out

The plural is volcanoes (with -es), though volcanos is sometimes seen. Note the stress: vol-CA-no.

erupt

/ɪˈrʌpt/7
1

verbintransitive

to suddenly throw out hot rock, ash and gas, used about a volcano

phun trào (núi lửa)

  • The volcano erupted without any warning at midnight.
  • If that mountain erupts, the village must be evacuated.
  • Ash fell on the town for hours after the volcano erupted.

a volcano erupts · erupt suddenly · erupt violently

2

verbintransitive

to start suddenly and with a lot of force, used about noise, anger or fighting

bùng nổ, bùng phát

  • The crowd erupted in cheers when the team scored.
  • Fighting erupted in the streets after the announcement.

erupt into violence · erupt in laughter

Word family

  • eruption noun the act of a volcano throwing out rock, ash and gas sự phun tràoThe eruption lasted three days and covered the town in ash.

evacuate

/ɪˈvækjueɪt/8

verbtransitive

to move people away from a dangerous place to somewhere safer

sơ tán, di tản

  • The army evacuated two thousand people from the coast.
  • We were told to evacuate the building immediately.
  • Families were evacuated before the storm arrived.

evacuate a village · be evacuated to a shelter · evacuate immediately

Word family

  • evacuation noun the act of moving people away from danger cuộc sơ tánThe evacuation of the island took only six hours.
  • evacuee noun a person who has been moved away from a dangerous place người được sơ tánThe school is now home to two hundred evacuees.

Watch out

You can evacuate people or a place: evacuate the residents and evacuate the hotel are both correct.

shelter

/ˈʃeltə(r)/9
1

nouncountable

a place or building where people can stay to be safe from danger or bad weather

nơi trú ẩn

  • The school was used as an emergency shelter during the flood.
  • Families slept in a shelter for two weeks.
  • We ran to a bus shelter when the rain started.

an emergency shelter · a temporary shelter · build a shelter

2

noununcountable

protection from danger or bad weather

sự che chở, sự trú ẩn

  • We took shelter under a big tree.
  • The victims need food, water and shelter.

take shelter · provide shelter · food and shelter

3

verbtransitive/intransitive

to protect somebody from danger, or to stay somewhere to be safe

che chở, trú ẩn

  • Hundreds of people sheltered in the temple that night.
  • The high wall shelters the garden from the wind.

shelter from the rain · shelter people

Watch out

When it means protection in general it is uncountable: take shelter, not take a shelter. When it is a particular building it is countable: an emergency shelter.

rescue

/ˈreskjuː/10
1

verbtransitive

to save somebody or something from a dangerous or harmful situation

cứu, giải cứu

  • Soldiers rescued a family from the roof of their house.
  • The dog was rescued after two days under the rubble.
  • They used a boat to rescue people from the flooded village.

rescue somebody from danger · be rescued by helicopter

2

nouncountable/uncountable

the act of saving somebody from danger

cuộc giải cứu, sự cứu hộ

  • The rescue took more than ten hours.
  • A rescue team arrived from the capital that evening.

a rescue team · a rescue operation · come to somebody's rescue

Word family

  • rescuer noun a person who saves somebody from danger người cứu hộThe rescuers worked all night in the rain.

Watch out

Pronounced /ˈreskjuː/, with the stress on the first part. The -ing form drops the e: rescuing.

damage

/ˈdæmɪdʒ/11
1

noununcountable

physical harm caused to something, which makes it less useful or valuable

thiệt hại, hư hỏng

  • The storm caused serious damage to the school roof.
  • There was little damage to the houses on the hill.
  • Engineers are checking the damage to the bridge.

cause damage · serious damage · damage to property

2

verbtransitive

to harm something so that it does not work properly or look as good as before

làm hư hại, gây thiệt hại

  • The flood damaged hundreds of motorbikes.
  • Strong winds damaged the roof but did not destroy the house.

badly damaged · damage crops

Watch out

The noun is uncountable — say a lot of damage, never many damages. Compare with destroy: damage means to harm something partly, so it can be repaired; destroy means to ruin it completely.

destroy

/dɪˈstrɔɪ/12

verbtransitive

to damage something so badly that it no longer exists or can never be used again

phá huỷ, tiêu huỷ

  • The fire destroyed three houses in less than an hour.
  • The earthquake destroyed the old bridge completely.
  • Thousands of trees were destroyed by the landslide.

completely destroy · be destroyed by fire · destroy a building

Word family

  • destruction noun the act of destroying something, or the state of being destroyed sự phá huỷ, sự tàn pháThe typhoon left a trail of destruction along the coast.
  • destructive adjective causing a lot of damage có sức tàn pháIt was the most destructive storm in fifty years.

Watch out

There is no noun destroyment; the noun is destruction. Remember the difference: a damaged house can be repaired, a destroyed house cannot.

collapse

/kəˈlæps/13
1

verbintransitive

to fall down suddenly, used about a building, roof or structure

sụp đổ, đổ sập

  • The old wall collapsed during the second earthquake.
  • Part of the roof collapsed under the weight of the water.
  • Two bridges collapsed and the road was closed.

a building collapses · collapse suddenly · collapse completely

2

verbintransitive

to fall down because you are very ill, tired or weak

ngã quỵ, ngất xỉu

  • One rescuer collapsed from exhaustion after twelve hours.
  • She collapsed in the heat and was taken to hospital.

collapse from exhaustion · collapse in the street

3

nounusually singular

the sudden falling down of a building or structure

sự sụp đổ

  • The collapse of the bridge trapped several cars.
  • Nobody was hurt in the collapse of the old factory.

the collapse of a building · cause a collapse

Watch out

Stress the second part: col-LAPSE. As a verb it is intransitive — say the roof collapsed, not the storm collapsed the roof.

warning

/ˈwɔːnɪŋ/14

nouncountable/uncountable

a statement or sign telling people that something bad may happen so that they can prepare

lời cảnh báo

  • The government issued a flood warning for the whole province.
  • The storm arrived without any warning.
  • People ignored the warning and stayed in their homes.

issue a warning · a storm warning · ignore a warning

Word family

  • warn verb to tell somebody about a possible danger or problem cảnh báoRadio stations warned people to leave the coast.

Watch out

After warn you often use a to-infinitive: They warned us to stay indoors. Note without warning has no article.

forecast

/ˈfɔːkɑːst/15
1

nouncountable

a statement about what the weather or another situation will probably be like

dự báo

  • The weather forecast says there will be heavy rain tonight.
  • According to the forecast, the typhoon will reach us by Friday.
  • I always check the forecast before a long trip.

the weather forecast · check the forecast · according to the forecast

2

verbtransitive

to say what you think will happen in the future, using information you have now

dự báo, dự đoán

  • Scientists forecast stronger storms in the coming years.
  • Rain was forecast for the weekend, but the sky stayed clear.

forecast rain · as forecast

Word family

  • forecaster noun a person whose job is to say what the weather will be like người dự báo (thời tiết)The forecaster on TV said the storm would turn west.

Watch out

The verb is usually irregular: forecast → forecast → forecast. The form forecasted exists but is less common. Stress the first part: FOREcast.

victim

/ˈvɪktɪm/16

nouncountable

a person who has been hurt, killed or badly affected by an event or a crime

nạn nhân

  • The victims of the earthquake need clean water and blankets.
  • Most of the flood victims are living in temporary shelters.
  • A fund was set up to help the victims of the landslide.

flood victims · help the victims · the victims of a disaster

Watch out

A victim is someone harmed by the disaster — they may or may not have died. A survivor is someone who lived through it. Someone can be both: a victim who is now a survivor.

relief

/rɪˈliːf/17
1

noununcountable

food, money, medicine and other help given to people after a disaster

cứu trợ

  • Relief arrived by helicopter three days after the flood.
  • Our school collected clothes for the relief effort.
  • Relief workers are still handing out rice and water.

relief workers · disaster relief · send relief

2

noununcountable

the good feeling you have when something unpleasant stops or does not happen

sự nhẹ nhõm

  • It was a huge relief to hear that my cousin was safe.
  • She smiled with relief when the water finally went down.

a huge relief · with relief · to my relief

Watch out

Relief is uncountable in both meanings — never reliefs. The related verb is relieve (note the different spelling and the /v/ sound).

survivor

/səˈvaɪvə(r)/18

nouncountable

a person who is still alive after an accident, illness or disaster

người sống sót

  • Rescue teams found three survivors under the fallen building.
  • The survivors were taken to a shelter near the river.
  • She was the only survivor of the landslide.

the only survivor · look for survivors · search for survivors

Word family

  • survive verb to continue to live after a dangerous event sống sótThe family survived the flood by climbing onto the roof.
  • survival noun the state of continuing to live after danger sự sống sótClean water is the key to survival after a disaster.

Watch out

Note the spelling: surviv-or, not surviver. Stress the middle part: sur-VI-vor.

Pronunciation — Word stress in multi-syllable words

Every English word of two or more syllables has one syllable that is louder, longer and clearer than the others. That is the stressed syllable. Dictionaries mark it with a small vertical line ˈ placed before the stressed syllable:

disˈaster = dis-AS-ter · ˈhurricane = HUR-ri-cane

Get the stress wrong and a listener may not recognise the word at all, even if every sound in it is correct.

Rule 1 — two-syllable words: nouns Oo, verbs oO

Two-syllable nouns — stress 1st (Oo) Two-syllable verbs — stress 2nd (oO)
ˈdamage, ˈshelter, ˈwarning deˈstroy, eˈrupt, colˈlapse
ˈvictim, ˈdanger, ˈflooding preˈpare, surˈvive, esˈcape

This is a strong tendency, not a law — ˈrescue is a first-stressed verb, and beˈlief is a second-stressed noun. But it is right far more often than it is wrong.

Rule 2 — the same spelling, two stresses, two words

Noun — Oo Verb — oO
a ˈrecord /ˈrekɔːd/ — bản ghi, kỷ lục to reˈcord /rɪˈkɔːd/ — ghi âm, ghi lại
a ˈpresent /ˈpreznt/ — món quà to preˈsent /prɪˈzent/ — trình bày
an ˈincrease /ˈɪŋkriːs/ — sự gia tăng to inˈcrease /ɪnˈkriːs/ — tăng lên

Rule 3 — the suffixes that pull the stress

The endings -tion, -sion, -ic and -ity never take the stress themselves. They pull it onto the syllable immediately before them. This rule is reliable, so learn it once and you can stress hundreds of words correctly.

Word Stress marked Syllable that takes it
destruction deˈstruction /dɪˈstrʌkʃn/ -struc- (before -tion)
eruption eˈruption /ɪˈrʌpʃn/ -rup- (before -tion)
protection proˈtection /prəˈtekʃn/ -tec- (before -tion)
explosion exˈplosion /ɪkˈspləʊʒn/ -plo- (before -sion)
volcanic volˈcanic /vɒlˈkænɪk/ -can- (before -ic)
dramatic draˈmatic /drəˈmætɪk/ -mat- (before -ic)
ability aˈbility /əˈbɪləti/ -bil- (before -ity)
electricity elecˈtricity /ɪˌlekˈtrɪsəti/ -tric- (before -ity)

Notice what happens to a word family: volˈcano → volˈcanic; deˈstroy → deˈstruction; eˈrupt → eˈruption. Here the stress stays put — it was already on the syllable before the ending, which is what makes these three families easy. But when the stem is stressed somewhere else, the ending pulls the stress onto the syllable in front of it: ˈphotograph → photoˈgraphic, eˈlectric → elecˈtricity.
