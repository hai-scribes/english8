Error hunt

Find and correct six errors in this paragraph. All six are about the future simple or the first conditional.

Six marks are on our wall now, and I will not calls anything back again. If we will bring back another week like that one, half the lane will be ill and the other half won't speaks to us. My grandmother says she will to keep the oil lamps anyway. Unless we don't stop now, somebody will get hurt. I think I sleep better when the water stays where it is.

MarkedShort answer

Read the paragraph again and find the errors yourself — their places are not given. Write the correction only: the word or words that should replace the wrong ones, in the order the errors appear. Spelling counts.

  • Spelling counts.
  • British and American spellings are both accepted.
  1. will not call/won't call (will is followed by the bare infinitive)
  2. if we bring (no will inside the if-clause)
  3. won't speak (won't + bare infinitive)
  4. she will keep/will keep (will + bare infinitive — no to)
  5. unless we stop (unless is already negative)
  6. I will sleep/will sleep (a prediction about the future takes will)

Say it the other way

If and unless say the same thing from opposite ends, so turning a sentence from one into the other is the quickest way to find out whether the negative is where you think it is.

Project — Two lifestyles, one week

Compare a traditional lifestyle with your own, using real data about yourself.

Step 1 — Track it. For seven days, record how you spend your time in three categories: active (walking, sport, housework), screen, and with other people, face to face.

Step 2 — Interview. Ask an older relative — a grandparent if you can — what they did at your age. Note three clear differences.

Step 3 — Compare. Draw a two-column table, Their lifestyle at 14 vs My lifestyle at 14, with at least five rows: food, travel, school, free time, friends.

Step 4 — Predict. Write 100–120 words answering these, using will, won't and at least two first conditionals:

  • Which parts of your lifestyle will still exist in fifty years?
  • Which parts will disappear?
  • If nothing changes, what will happen to your well-being?

Step 5 — Say it. Present your comparison aloud for one minute, without reading word for word. Record it, listen back once, and count how many times you said will.

Stretch: Choose one habit from your relative's lifestyle that you think was better than yours. Try it for two weeks and write three sentences about what changed.
