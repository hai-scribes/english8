A Closer Look 1

Vocabulary — Teen life

teenager

/ˈtiːneɪdʒə(r)/1

nouncountable

a person between 13 and 19 years old

thanh thiếu niên, tuổi teen

  • Most teenagers in my class have a part-time hobby.
  • The club is for teenagers aged 14 to 18.
  • As a teenager he was very shy.

a typical teenager · teenagers today

Word family

  • teenage adjective aged between 13 and 19 ở tuổi teenShe has two teenage sons.

Watch out

The name comes from the numbers that end in -teen: thirteen to nineteen. Use the adjective teenage before a noun, not teenager: a teenage boy, never a teenager boy.

adolescence

/ˌædəˈlesns/2

noununcountable

the period of life when a child develops into an adult

tuổi dậy thì, giai đoạn vị thành niên

  • Adolescence can be a confusing time.
  • Many changes happen during adolescence.
  • He was quiet throughout his adolescence.

during adolescence · early adolescence

Word family

  • adolescent noun / adjective a young person going through this stage thiếu niên; thuộc tuổi vị thành niênAdolescents need plenty of sleep.

Watch out

Stress the third syllable: ad-o-LES-cence. More formal than teenage years.

peer pressure

/ˈpɪə ˌpreʃə(r)/3

noununcountable

the strong feeling that you must do what people of your own age are doing in order to be accepted

áp lực từ bạn bè đồng trang lứa

  • Peer pressure made him start smoking.
  • Learning to say no is the best answer to peer pressure.
  • She gave in to peer pressure and skipped the class.

give in to peer pressure · resist peer pressure

Word family

  • peer noun a person of the same age or position as you bạn đồng trang lứaHe is respected by his peers.

self-confident

/ˌself ˈkɒnfɪdənt/4

adjective

sure that you can do things well and that people will like you

tự tin

  • Speaking in class made her more self-confident.
  • He is self-confident enough to ask for help.
  • A self-confident student is not always the loudest one.

become self-confident · a self-confident manner

Word family

  • self-confidence noun belief in your own ability sự tự tinJoining the club gave him self-confidence.

Watch out

Keep the hyphen. Compare confident (sure about something in general) with self-confident (sure about yourself).

delighted

/dɪˈlaɪtɪd/5

adjective+ to-infinitive / + that

very pleased and happy

vui sướng, hài lòng

  • I was delighted to hear your news.
  • She was delighted that her team had won.
  • My parents were delighted with my report.

delighted to hear · delighted with something

Watch out

Stronger than pleased or glad. Feelings adjective — the -ed form describes the person; the -ing form (delightful, delighting) describes the thing.

embarrassed

/ɪmˈbærəst/6

adjective

feeling shy, awkward or ashamed, especially in front of other people

xấu hổ, ngượng ngùng

  • I felt embarrassed when I forgot her name.
  • He was too embarrassed to sing in front of the class.
  • She looked embarrassed and went very red.

feel embarrassed · embarrassed about something

Word family

  • embarrassing adjective making you feel embarrassed gây xấu hổIt was an embarrassing mistake.

Watch out

Two rs and two ss. The person is embarrassed; the situation is embarrassing.

frustrated

/frʌˈstreɪtɪd/7

adjective

annoyed or impatient because you cannot do or change something

bực bội, nản lòng

  • I get frustrated when the internet is slow.
  • She was frustrated by her own mistakes.
  • He felt frustrated that nobody would listen.

get frustrated · frustrated with something

Word family

  • frustrating adjective causing that feeling gây bực bộiLosing by one point was frustrating.

stressed

/strest/8

adjective

worried and tense because of pressure from work, study or life

căng thẳng, áp lực

  • Many students feel stressed before exams.
  • He gets stressed when he has too much homework.
  • You look stressed — take a break.

feel stressed · stressed out · stressed about exams

Word family

  • stress noun worry caused by a difficult situation sự căng thẳngExercise helps reduce stress.
  • stressful adjective causing stress gây căng thẳngExam week is stressful.

Watch out

The person is stressed; the situation is stressful. In phonetics, stress also means the strong syllable in a word — the sense used in Units 9–12.

curious

/ˈkjʊəriəs/9
1

adjective+ about

wanting to know or learn about something

tò mò, ham hiểu biết

  • Teenagers are naturally curious about the world.
  • I was curious to see what was inside.
  • She is curious about how things work.

curious about something · curious to know

2

adjective

strange or unusual

kỳ lạ, khác thường

  • There was a curious smell in the room.
  • It is curious that nobody noticed.

a curious thing · curiously enough

Word family

  • curiosity noun the wish to know about things sự tò mòCuriosity is the best reason to read.

socialise

/ˈsəʊʃəlaɪz/10

verbintransitive

to meet and spend time with other people in a friendly way

giao lưu, gặp gỡ mọi người

  • He loves to socialise with new people.
  • I don't socialise much during the week because of homework.
  • A club is a good place to socialise with people your own age.

socialise with someone · a chance to socialise

Word family

  • social adjective connected with meeting people and spending time with them thuộc về xã hội, giao tiếpShe has a busy social life.
  • sociable adjective friendly and enjoying the company of others hoà đồng, dễ gầnHe is quiet but very sociable once you know him.

Watch out

British spelling socialise; American socialize. Both are correct — just be consistent. Do not confuse social (about society or meeting people) with sociable (describing a friendly person).

informed decision

/ɪnˌfɔːmd dɪˈsɪʒn/11

nouncountable

a choice you make after finding out the facts you need

quyết định sáng suốt (dựa trên hiểu biết)

  • Read about the course first so you can make an informed decision.
  • Teenagers should learn to make informed decisions about money.
  • It was an informed decision, not a guess.

make an informed decision · an informed choice

Word family

  • informed adjective knowing about a subject because you have learned the facts hiểu biết, nắm rõ thông tinShe is well informed about the issue.

life skill

/ˈlaɪf skɪl/12

nouncountable, usually plural

a practical ability you need in order to manage everyday life

kỹ năng sống

  • Cooking and managing money are useful life skills.
  • The school runs a life skills class on Fridays.
  • Saying no politely is an important life skill.

learn life skills · essential life skills

housework

/ˈhaʊswɜːk/13

noununcountable

the work of cleaning and looking after a house

việc nhà

  • We all share the housework at home.
  • I do my housework before I go out.
  • He does more housework than anyone else in the family.

do the housework · help with the housework · share the housework

Watch out

Uncountable — never houseworks or a housework. Say a lot of housework. And you do housework; you don't make it. Not the same as homework, which is school work.

join a club

/ˌdʒɔɪn ə ˈklʌb/14

phraseverb + noun

to become a member of a group that meets for a shared activity

tham gia câu lạc bộ

  • Why don't you join a club and meet new people?
  • I joined the photography club last term.
  • She wants to join a chess club at school.

join a chess club · join the school club

Watch out

Join a club (become a member) is different from join in an activity (take part on one occasion).

get involved (in)

/ˌɡet ɪnˈvɒlvd/15

phrase+ in / with

to start taking part in an activity or become connected with it

tham gia vào, dấn thân vào

  • He got involved in a community project last year.
  • More students should get involved in school events.
  • Try not to get involved in their argument.

get involved in a project · get involved with a charity

keep up with

/ˌkiːp ˈʌp wɪð/16
1

phrasal verb

to move or make progress at the same speed as someone or something else

theo kịp, bắt kịp

  • I can't keep up with the rest of the class in maths.
  • Walk slower — I can't keep up with you!
  • It's hard to keep up with all the homework.

keep up with the class · keep up with the work

2

phrasal verb

to continue to learn about something as it changes

cập nhật, theo dõi (tin tức, xu hướng)

  • She keeps up with the news every morning.
  • It's impossible to keep up with every new app.

keep up with the news · keep up with technology

Watch out

Three parts, and they never separate: keep up with them, never keep them up with.

deal with

/ˈdiːl wɪð/17
1

phrasal verb

to take action in order to solve a problem or handle a situation

giải quyết, xử lý

  • How do you deal with stress before an exam?

  • I'll deal with it tomorrow morning.

deal with a problem · deal with stress

2

phrasal verb

đề cập đến, nói về

the book deals with

Pronunciation — /ʊə/ and /ɔɪ/

Both sounds are diphthongs: your mouth moves from one vowel to another inside a single syllable. The trap is that they start in almost the same place and travel in opposite directions.

/ʊə/ — as in tourist /ɔɪ/ — as in choice
Starts at /ʊ/ — high, back, lips rounded /ɔː/ — open, back, lips rounded
Moves to /ə/ — the flat, relaxed centre /ɪ/ — high, front, lips spread
Lips round → relax round → spread into a smile
Spelling our, ure, uri, Eu oi, oy
Examples tourist, pure, cure, curious, furious, during choice, join, voice, enjoy, avoid, noise

Mẹo: Với /ɔɪ/ môi bạn bè ngang ra như đang cười ở cuối âm (choice, boy). Với /ʊə/ môi giãn ra và thả lỏng, lưỡi lùi về giữa (tourist, pure). Hãy soi gương: nếu cuối từ mà môi bạn cười, đó là /ɔɪ/.

Lưu ý: một số người Anh ngày nay đọc sure và poor thành /ʃɔː/, /pɔː/. Trong bài này ta chỉ luyện những từ mà /ʊə/ luôn đúng.
