Grammar check

Part A. Write S (simple) or C (compound).

  1. Khoa and Hùng joined the wellbeing club.
  2. Khoa joined the wellbeing club, and Hùng joined the debate club.
  3. I was frustrated, yet I kept trying.
  4. She takes photos and edits them at home.

Part B. Add the missing comma, or write ✓ if the sentence is already correct.

  1. I felt stressed so I went for a walk.
  2. Đạt takes photos and posts the best ones.
  3. You can come on Thursday or you can come next week.
  4. He does his homework and his chores before dinner.

Part C. Join each pair with the word in capitals. Punctuate it correctly.

  1. HOWEVER — The website is user-friendly. I still could not find the form.
  2. THEREFORE — Hùng wants to be more focused. He has joined the chess club.
  3. OTHERWISE — Log on before Friday. You will miss the first session.
  4. SO — I had four tests in one week. I did not sleep much.

Part A. 1. S 2. C 3. C 4. S Part B. 5. I felt stressed, so I went for a walk. 6. ✓ (compound verb — no comma) 7. You can come on Thursday, or you can come next week. 8. ✓ (one clause, two objects) Part C. 9. The website is user-friendly; however, I still could not find the form. 10. Hùng wants to be more focused; therefore, he has joined the chess club. 11. Log on before Friday; otherwise, you will miss the first session. 12. I had four tests in one week, so I did not sleep much. (9–11 take a semicolon and then a comma; only 12 is a conjunction, so it takes a comma and nothing else.)

7.4

Error hunt

Find and correct six errors in this paragraph. Five are about joining clauses (comma splices, run-ons, missing commas); one is a subject–verb agreement slip.

Minh came back on Tuesday and he has not changed at all. He asked about the football final, there was no final this year. The rest of us are a year older so he had to hear it from us. Tí could have sent him back or he could keep him. He did not blow the lamp out, he put it down. Now Minh sit with us at lunch every day.

MarkedShort answer

Read the paragraph again and find the errors yourself — their places are not given. Write the correction only: the word or words that should replace the wrong ones, in the order the errors appear. Spelling counts.

  • Spelling counts.
  • British and American spellings are both accepted.
  1. Tuesday, and he/Tuesday, and (a comma is needed before and joining two clauses)
  2. final, but there/final, but (comma splice — but joins the two clauses)
  3. older, so he/older, so (missing comma before so)
  4. back, or he/back, or (missing comma before or)
  5. out, and he/out, and (comma splice — and joins the two clauses)
  6. sits (sit → sits — subject–verb agreement)

Project — Start a club

Design a club that your school does not have yet, and make the case for it.

Step 1 — Find the gap. Ask five classmates two questions: What do you do after school? and What club do you wish existed? Write the answers in a table.

Step 2 — Design it. Fill in a one-page plan:

Item Your club
Name of the club
What members do each week
Day, time and room
What you need (equipment, a teacher, money)
Who it is for

Step 3 — Write the poster. 60–80 words persuading students to join. Use at least three compound sentences — Come on Thursday, and bring a friend.

Step 4 — Say it. Present your club aloud for one minute, as if you were standing in front of your class. Record it. Listen back once and count how many compound sentences you actually produced.

Stretch: Write the short email you would send to your head teacher asking for permission. Keep it under 100 words, be polite, and answer the question they will certainly ask: who will supervise it?
