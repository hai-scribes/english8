Skills 1: Reading & Speaking

Reading — *Four clubs, one Thursday afternoon*

A report from the school magazine of Nguyen Du Secondary School.

At Nguyen Du Secondary School, Thursday afternoon belongs to the clubs. Lessons finish at half past two, and by three o'clock the classrooms have changed completely.

Room 12 is the debate club. Twenty students sit in two lines facing each other, and the topic is written on the board. Last week it was "Should students choose their own subjects?" Nobody wins a prize, but the members say they are far more self-confident than they were in September.

The cooking club works in the school kitchen. Ms Lan teaches one simple dish each week, and the members eat it together afterwards. "Some of us could not boil rice in September," says Khanh, 14. "Now I cook dinner on Sundays, so my mother gets an evening off." Cooking is a life skill, not only a hobby.

Downstairs, the volunteer club is quieter. The members collect old books, repair them, and send them to a primary school in the mountains. The work is slow, yet nobody gives up.

The newest club is the wellbeing club, and it may be the most useful one of all. Members meet for forty minutes, talk about their week, and learn one way to deal with stress: planning, breathing, or simply going to bed earlier.

Not every student joins a club, and that is fine. But Ms Lan has noticed something. "The ones who get involved keep up with their schoolwork better," she says. "They are busier, yet they seem calmer."

5.1

True, False, or Not Given

Reading item typeTrue/False/Not given

False means the text says the opposite. Not Given means the text does not say either way — and wanting to answer from what you know about clubs is exactly the pull this type is built to catch.

  • One mark each. Nothing is part-marked.
  • One answer per item — a second one scores zero.

One of the official eleven Reading question types, marked by the published rules rather than by ours.

  1. T
  2. F ("Nobody wins a prize")
  3. NG (he says some of us could not boil rice — not necessarily himself)
  4. F (it is the newest)
  5. T
5.2

Answer the questions

Reading item typeShort-answer questions

Take the words from the text. Do not rephrase them — this is the one place in the course where using your own words costs you the mark.

Write NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER for each answer.

  • One mark each. Nothing is part-marked.
  • Spelling and grammar mistakes lose the mark — here, because they do there.
  • British and American spellings are both accepted.
  • A hyphenated word counts as one word.
  • Two answers in one gap score zero, even if one of them is right.

One of the official eleven Reading question types, marked by the published rules rather than by ours.

  1. (by) three o'clock (lessons finish at half past two)
  2. (far) more self-confident
  3. eat it together/eats it together
  4. (a) primary school
  5. repair them/repairs them

Speaking — Asking and answering about a club

Working alone: You have no partner, so do this in three steps. It still works — speaking practice is mostly about producing language out loud.

Step 1 — Prepare. Choose one club: a real club at your school, or an imaginary one you would like to start. Write short notes (not full sentences).

Question Your notes
What is the club called, and what does it do?
When and where does it meet?
Why did you join it, or why do you want to?
What do you need in order to join?
What is the best thing about it?
Is there anything you don't like about it?

Step 2 — Speak. Answer each question aloud from your notes, in full sentences. Use at least one compound sentence in every answer — join two ideas with and, but, so, or, or yet.

Step 3 — Record and check. Record yourself on your phone. Listen back and check three things:

  • Did you actually produce compound sentences, or only short simple ones?
  • Did you pronounce /ʊə/ and /ɔɪ/ differently in curious and join?
  • Did your pauses fall between points rather than inside a sentence?

Repeat Step 2 until each answer runs clearly from its first word to its last.
