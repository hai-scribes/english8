Reading — The twelfth mark

Tí, 13, writes about the twelfth and last time the sea came back.

ReadingOne clock — 20 minutes

You have 20 minutes for the text and its questions. The clock covers everything: reading the text and answering. It does not stop, and there is no extra time at the end.

Reading passage

I have told this story eleven times and nobody believes the first part, so this time I will start at the end.

The moon came closest on the Thursday. Khoa had the numbers for it: gravity, he said, the moon pulling the water, and this week it pulls hardest. The night was clear, which he wanted, so that he could name things. He pointed at Venus, low over the roofs, and said that it sits inside the habitable zone and is still far too hot for anybody. Then he asked me twice whether I could see it. I could not. I asked him what a habitable zone was, and he asked me whether I had been awake in his class on Tuesday.

By that night our street was a bay. There were boats where the buses go, and a lamp in every upstairs window, and the whole lane out on the roofs talking, and somebody two roofs away asked whether anybody had seen her cat, and nobody frightened of it any more. The twelfth mark was four feet under, and Bống would not look at the wall.

We rowed out over our own market. Thảo rowed. Minh sat in the bow and did not say much; he asked once where the market used to be, and after that nothing. Bống asked us three times whether we were sure. Halfway out, something very large came up beside the boat, and stayed a moment, and went down again without hurrying, and nobody said one word.

Out there, nothing is lost for a long way in any direction. No houses, no wall, no market. Nothing lost anywhere near us except one name.

The keeper warned me about this months ago, in a car park: the half of the rule nobody passes on, and not allowed either. He was right. I went out there anyway, on purpose, for somebody else.

"You never choose what comes back," I said. "But you do choose where you are standing when you call."

The tide turned. It went over in about four seconds, and after that the water began going out, and it has kept going.

Nothing landed in the boat. The water did not give us one single thing. Bống lifted her head the way you do when somebody calls you from another room, and said, "Sao."

Then she sat down very suddenly, because a name does not come home by itself. Everything walked in behind it: the workroom, the far end of the line, the lesson she was never given, and the old man who never got as far as handing it over.

"I know where he is," Sao said. "And I know what he kept asking me."

Khoa asked whether she would go back for her training now that the name was hers. Sao said she would walk up with us and come home after. She lives here.

The keeper was on the wall when we came in, and nobody had asked him to come.

"Sao," he said. "That is a keeper's name."

"I know," she said.

He said nothing about the rule I broke, and he never asked me why I had done it. He will. It can wait.

Thảo asked how long the walk would take. Khoa already knew.

Writing — A descriptive paragraph about aliens

Task. Imagine the creatures that live on a planet you have invented. Describe them in a descriptive paragraph of 80–100 words. Describe what they look like, how they live, and one strange thing about them.

Model paragraph — read this first

The creatures on Zonia are small, and not one of them ever forgets a face. An adult stands about a metre tall, with soft blue-grey skin and four thin arms. Their eyes are enormous and completely black, because the planet orbits a weak star and its surface is always dark. They have no mouths; they take in food through their skin, and the thin atmosphere is enough for them to survive. When they are happy, tiny lights run along their backs like sparks. They move slowly, they hum instead of speaking, and they seem endlessly curious about every visitor.

Unit 12 · 25 words

Space and life beyond Earth

25 words · tap one to open it

planet

/ˈplænɪt/1

nounhành tinh

nouncountable

a very large round object in space that moves around a star such as the Sun

hành tinh

  • Mars is the planet that people talk about most.
  • Eight planets travel around the Sun.
  • Could there be life on another planet?

a distant planet · life on other planets · the red planet

Watch out

The names of planets are capitalised: Mars, Venus, Jupiter. We usually write Earth with a capital letter when we mean the planet, but the earth when we mean the ground or soil.

orbit

/ˈɔːbɪt/2

nounquỹ đạo

1

nouncountable

the curved path that a planet, moon or spacecraft follows around a larger object in space

quỹ đạo

  • The Moon takes about 27 days to complete one orbit of the Earth.
  • The satellite is now in orbit above us.
  • Each planet has its own orbit around the Sun.

in orbit · go into orbit · the earth's orbit

2

verbtransitive

to travel in a curved path around a planet, star or moon

quay quanh, bay quanh

  • The space station orbits the Earth sixteen times a day.
  • Two small moons orbit Mars.
  • The telescope has been orbiting our planet since 1990.

orbit the earth · orbit the sun

Watch out

As a verb, orbit takes an object with no preposition: orbit the Earth (not ~~orbit around the Earth~~). As a noun, use in orbit or an orbit around something.

gravity

/ˈɡrævəti/3

nountrọng lực, lực hấp dẫn

noununcountable

the natural force that pulls objects towards each other, and pulls things on Earth down to the ground

trọng lực, lực hấp dẫn

  • Things fall to the ground because of gravity.
  • The Moon has much weaker gravity than the Earth.
  • Astronauts float because there is almost no gravity in the space station.

the force of gravity · low gravity · zero gravity

Word family

  • gravitational adjective connected with gravity thuộc về trọng lực, hấp dẫnThe gravitational pull of the Moon moves the sea.

Watch out

Gravity is uncountable, so we never say ~~a gravity~~ or ~~gravities~~. Say there is no gravity or the force of gravity.

atmosphere

/ˈætməsfɪə(r)/4

nounkhí quyển, bầu khí quyển

1

nouncountable

the layer of gases that surrounds a planet

khí quyển, bầu khí quyển

  • The Earth's atmosphere protects us from the Sun.
  • The atmosphere on Mars is very thin.
  • The spacecraft burned as it entered the atmosphere.

the earth's atmosphere · a thin atmosphere · enter the atmosphere

2

nounusually singular

the feeling or mood that a place or situation gives you

bầu không khí, không khí (nơi chốn)

  • The little café has a warm, friendly atmosphere.
  • There was an exciting atmosphere before the launch.
  • I love the atmosphere of the night market.

a relaxed atmosphere · create an atmosphere

Watch out

Stress the first syllable: AT-mos-phere. The adjective is atmospheric.

oxygen

/ˈɒksɪdʒən/5

nounkhí ô-xy

noununcountable

a gas in the air that has no colour or smell and that people, animals and plants need to live

khí ô-xy

  • Humans cannot live without oxygen.
  • Astronauts carry oxygen in their suits.
  • There is not enough oxygen on Mars for us to breathe.

breathe oxygen · an oxygen tank · run out of oxygen

Watch out

Oxygen is uncountable: say some oxygen or a lot of oxygen, never ~~an oxygen~~. Stress the first syllable: OX-y-gen.

habitable

/ˈhæbɪtəbl/6

adjectivecó thể sinh sống được

adjective

good enough for people or animals to live in

có thể sinh sống được

  • Scientists are looking for a habitable planet.
  • After the storm, the old house was no longer habitable.
  • Only a small part of the desert is habitable.

a habitable planet · habitable land · the habitable zone

Word family

  • habitat noun the natural place where a plant or animal lives môi trường sốngPollution is destroying the habitat of these birds.
  • inhabit verb to live in a particular place cư trú, sinh sống ởNo one knows whether any creature inhabits that planet.
  • uninhabitable adjective impossible to live in không thể sinh sống đượcVenus is far too hot and is completely uninhabitable.

Watch out

Careful: inhabitable means the same as habitable, not the opposite. The opposite is uninhabitable.

surface

/ˈsɜːfɪs/7

nounbề mặt

1

nouncountable

the outside or top layer of something

bề mặt

  • The surface of the Moon is covered with dust and rocks.
  • Water covers most of the Earth's surface.
  • Please clean the surface of the table before you eat.

the surface of the moon · a flat surface · on the surface

2

verbintransitive

to come up to the top of the water from below

nổi lên (mặt nước)

  • The whale surfaced a few metres from our boat.
  • He swam down and then surfaced near the rocks.
  • The submarine surfaces once a day.

surface for air · surface slowly

telescope

/ˈtelɪskəʊp/8

nounkính thiên văn

nouncountable

a tube-shaped instrument that makes faraway things, especially stars and planets, look bigger and nearer

kính thiên văn

  • We looked at Saturn through a telescope.
  • The school has a small telescope on the roof.
  • A powerful space telescope took these pictures.

look through a telescope · a space telescope · a powerful telescope

Watch out

Built from tele- (far) + -scope (an instrument for looking), like television and telephone.

astronaut

/ˈæstrənɔːt/9

nounphi hành gia, nhà du hành vũ trụ

nouncountable

a person who is trained to travel and work in a spacecraft

phi hành gia, nhà du hành vũ trụ

  • The astronauts spent six months on the space station.
  • She wants to become an astronaut one day.
  • An astronaut must wear a special suit outside the spacecraft.

become an astronaut · train as an astronaut · an astronaut's suit

Watch out

Russian space travellers are often called cosmonauts, and Chinese ones taikonauts, but astronaut is the general word in English.

spacecraft

/ˈspeɪskrɑːft/10

nountàu vũ trụ

nouncountable

a vehicle that travels in space, with or without people inside

tàu vũ trụ

  • The spacecraft landed safely in the sea.
  • Two spacecraft are now travelling towards Jupiter.
  • Engineers are testing a new spacecraft this year.

land a spacecraft · an unmanned spacecraft · aboard the spacecraft

Watch out

Spacecraft has the same form in the plural: one spacecraft, two spacecraft (never ~~spacecrafts~~). Spaceship is a more informal word and its plural is spaceships.

extraterrestrial

/ˌekstrətəˈrestriəl/11

adjectivengoài Trái Đất, ngoài hành tinh

1

adjective

connected with life or things from outside the planet Earth

ngoài Trái Đất, ngoài hành tinh

  • Scientists are still searching for extraterrestrial life.
  • There is no clear proof of extraterrestrial visitors.
  • The film is about an extraterrestrial creature.

extraterrestrial life · extraterrestrial intelligence

2

nouncountable

a living being that comes from a planet other than Earth

sinh vật ngoài hành tinh

  • In the story, a friendly extraterrestrial lands in a garden.
  • Do you believe extraterrestrials really exist?

believe in extraterrestrials · an intelligent extraterrestrial

Word family

  • ET noun short form of extraterrestrial, often used in films and newspapers sinh vật ngoài hành tinh (viết tắt)The famous film ET is about a lost alien.
  • terrestrial adjective connected with the Earth or with land thuộc về Trái Đất, trên cạnTurtles lay their eggs in terrestrial nests on the beach.

Watch out

A useful word-building word: extra- means outside and terrestrial means of the Earth, so extraterrestrial = from outside the Earth. Compare extraordinary = outside the ordinary.

alien

/ˈeɪliən/12

nounngười ngoài hành tinh, sinh vật ngoài hành tinh

1

nouncountable

a living being that comes from another planet

người ngoài hành tinh, sinh vật ngoài hành tinh

  • The boy dreamed that aliens landed in his school yard.
  • Nobody has ever met an alien.
  • In the film, the alien speaks perfect English.

believe in aliens · an alien spaceship · meet an alien

2

adjective

strange and completely unfamiliar to you

xa lạ, không quen thuộc

  • At first, life in the big city felt alien to her.
  • The customs of the village were alien to the visitors.

alien to someone · an alien culture

Watch out

As a noun, alien usually means a being from space in everyday English and in films. As an adjective it more often means unfamiliar or strange, and it is followed by to: the idea was alien to me.

creature

/ˈkriːtʃə(r)/13

nounsinh vật, sinh linh

nouncountable

any living thing that can move, such as an animal or an imaginary being

sinh vật, sinh linh

  • Strange creatures live at the bottom of the ocean.
  • Could simple creatures survive on Mars?
  • The story is about a small green creature with three eyes.

a living creature · a strange creature · sea creatures

Watch out

Creature is used for animals and imaginary beings, not normally for plants and not for people (except in phrases like a creature of habit).

survive without something · survive an accident · manage to survive

solar system

/ˌsəʊlə ˈsɪstəm/17

nounhệ Mặt Trời

nounsingular

the Sun together with the planets and other objects that travel around it

hệ Mặt Trời

  • Jupiter is the largest planet in the solar system.
  • Our solar system formed billions of years ago.
  • Scientists have found other solar systems far away.

the solar system · in our solar system · the outer solar system

Normally used with the: the solar system, the planets of the solar system. The adjective solar means of the Sun, as in solar energy and solar panels.

galaxy

/ˈɡæləksi/18

nounthiên hà

nouncountable

a very large group of stars and planets held together in space

thiên hà

  • Our galaxy is called the Milky Way.
  • There are billions of galaxies in the universe.
  • The telescope photographed a galaxy far from Earth.

a distant galaxy · our galaxy · the milky way galaxy

Watch out

When we mean our own galaxy we say the galaxy. The plural is galaxies (y → ies). Do not confuse galaxy (one group of stars) with universe (everything that exists).

UFO

/ˌjuː ef ˈəʊ/19

nounvật thể bay không xác định, đĩa bay

nouncountable

an object seen in the sky that nobody can explain, and that some people believe comes from another planet

vật thể bay không xác định, đĩa bay

  • My uncle says he saw a UFO above the rice fields one night.
  • The photograph of the UFO turned out to be a weather balloon.
  • Hundreds of UFOs are reported every year, and almost all of them have an ordinary explanation.

see a UFO · report a UFO · a UFO sighting

Watch out

UFO stands for unidentified flying object — literally an object we have not identified, so calling something a UFO says only that nobody knows what it was, not that it came from space. Write it in capitals with no full stops. Because we say the letters yoo-ef-oh, it takes a, not an: a UFO.

Uranus

/ˈjʊərənəs/20

nounsao Thiên Vương

nounproper noun

the seventh planet from the Sun, a very cold blue-green planet that spins on its side

sao Thiên Vương

Neptune

/ˈneptjuːn/21

nounproper noun

người chỉ huy, người cầm đầu
