Pronunciation check

Mark the intonation. Write ↗ or ↘ on the line after each item.

  1. We need water , oxygen and warmth .
  2. She visited Ha Noi , Hue and Da Nang .
  3. Which item in question 1 tells you the list has finished?
  4. What does it mean if the last item rises instead of falling?
  1. water ↗, oxygen ↗, warmth ↘
  2. Ha Noi ↗, Hue ↗, Da Nang ↘
  3. The last item, warmth — the falling tone ↘ signals the end of the list.
  4. It means the list is open: there are more items the speaker has not named ("…and so on"), or the speaker has not finished.

7.4

Error hunt

Find and correct six errors in this paragraph. Five are about reported questions; one is a subject–verb agreement slip.

The lamp is cold, and last week it went home in a rice sack. Bà Sáu asked me did I know where the cat had got to. Then she asked where have I been all afternoon. Sao asked her if he could stay for dinner? I asked him when will he go back for his training. He said not yet. Minh comes over on Sundays now, and we play the wooden game in the yard. My uncle asked me why did I keep the old path clear. Everyone use it now.

MarkedShort answer

Read the paragraph again and find the errors yourself — their places are not given. Write the correction only: the word or words that should replace the wrong ones, in the order the errors appear. Spelling counts.

  • Spelling counts.
  • British and American spellings are both accepted.
  1. asked me if I knew/asked me whether I knew (a reported Yes/No question takes if or whether)
  2. asked where I had been (no inversion in a reported question, and the tense shifts back)
  3. asked her if he could stay for dinner (and it ends in a full stop, not a question mark)
  4. asked him when he would go back (statement order, and will backshifts)
  5. asked me why I kept (no did, and statement order)
  6. uses (Everyone takes a singular verb)

Project — Design a habitable planet

Invent a planet that could really support life, and present it.

Step 1 — Set the conditions. Fill in this table for your planet. Every answer must be a number or a short phrase, and it must be possible.

Condition Your planet Earth (for comparison)
Distance from its star inside the habitable zone
Length of one day about 24 hours
Length of one year 365 days
Surface gravity 100%
Main gases in the atmosphere nitrogen, oxygen
Surface: land / water 29% land, 71% water

Step 2 — Draw it. One page: the planet, its star, its moons, and one creature that lives there. Label everything in English.

Step 3 — Describe it. Write 100–120 words describing your planet and its creatures. Use at least two lists of three or more items.

Step 4 — Interview yourself. Write down five questions a journalist would ask you about your planet. Then report all five in one paragraph: She asked me how long a day was, whether the creatures could…

Step 5 — Present it. Speak for one minute without reading word for word. Record it. Listen back and check that every list ends with a falling tone.

Stretch: Swap your condition table with a classmate — or hand it to a family member — and ask them to find one reason why life on your planet would be difficult. Then redesign the planet to fix it.
