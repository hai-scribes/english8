A Closer Look 1

Vocabulary — Rural life

buffalo

/ˈbʌfələʊ/1

nouncountable, plural: buffalo or buffaloes

a large heavy animal of the cow family, used in Asia for pulling ploughs and carts

con trâu

  • A buffalo was standing in the middle of the paddy field.
  • My grandfather still uses a buffalo to plough the rice fields.
  • The boys rode the buffalo home along the river path.

ride a buffalo · a water buffalo · a herd of buffalo

Watch out

In Viet Nam this almost always means the water buffalo. The plural can be buffalo (unchanged) or buffaloes — both are correct.

paddy field

/ˈpædi fiːld/2

nouncountable

a field filled with shallow water where rice is grown

ruộng lúa nước, cánh đồng lúa

  • The paddy fields turn bright green in early summer.
  • We walked along the narrow path between two paddy fields.
  • Her parents work in the paddy fields from dawn.

work in the paddy fields · a terraced paddy field

Watch out

Also written rice paddy or just paddy. Paddy on its own can also mean rice that still has its husk on.

harvest

/ˈhɑːvɪst/3
1

nouncountable

the time when crops are gathered from the fields, or the crops that are gathered

vụ thu hoạch; mùa gặt

  • The rice harvest begins in October.
  • It was a good harvest this year — the best for a decade.
  • The whole village helps at harvest time.

a good harvest · a poor harvest · harvest time

2

verbtransitive

to gather a crop from the fields

thu hoạch, gặt hái

  • They harvest the rice twice a year.
  • The corn is harvested in late August.

harvest a crop · harvest rice

orchard

/ˈɔːtʃəd/4

nouncountable

a piece of land where fruit trees are grown

vườn cây ăn quả

  • My uncle has an orchard behind his house.
  • We picked apples in the orchard all morning.
  • The orchard is full of mango trees.

an apple orchard · pick fruit in the orchard

Watch out

An orchard is for fruit trees. A garden is a general word, and a vegetable garden grows vegetables instead.

cattle

/ˈkætl/5

nounplural noun — always plural

cows and bulls kept on a farm for their milk or meat

gia súc (trâu bò)

  • The cattle are grazing in the field.
  • His family keeps about forty cattle.
  • We drove the cattle down to the stream to drink.

keep cattle · a cattle farm · graze cattle

Watch out

Always plural — the cattle are in the field, never is. There is no form a cattle or cattles; for one animal say a cow or a bull.

herd

/hɜːd/6
1

nouncountable

a large group of animals of the same kind that live or are kept together

đàn, bầy (thú lớn)

  • A herd of buffalo crossed the road in front of us.
  • The farmer has a herd of thirty cows.
  • We watched a herd of elephants at the waterhole.

a herd of cattle · a herd of buffalo

2

verbtransitive

to move animals, or people, together as a group

lùa, dồn (đàn gia súc)

  • The boy herded the goats back to the barn.
  • We were herded onto the bus like sheep.

herd cattle · herd sheep

Watch out

Use herd for cows, buffalo and elephants; flock for birds and sheep; school for fish. Sounds exactly like heard.

barn

/bɑːn/7

nouncountable

a large farm building used for storing crops or keeping animals in

nhà kho, chuồng gia súc (ở trang trại)

  • The hay is kept in the barn.
  • We shelter the cattle in the barn in winter.
  • There is an old wooden barn behind the farmhouse.

an old barn · a hay barn

well

/wel/8
1

nouncountable

a deep hole dug in the ground to reach water

cái giếng

  • Every morning she draws water from the well.
  • The village well dried up during the drought.
  • There is an old stone well in the yard.

draw water from a well · a village well · dig a well

2

adverbcomparative: better, best

in a good or satisfactory way

tốt, giỏi

  • She sings very well.
  • He didn't sleep well last night.

do well · work well · as well as

Watch out

In this unit it is the noun — a hole for water. Do not confuse the adverb well (she plays well) with the adjective good (she is a good player).

stream

/striːm/9
1

nouncountable

a small narrow river

con suối, dòng suối

  • The children were playing in the stream.
  • A small stream runs behind our house.
  • We crossed the stream on stepping stones.

a mountain stream · cross a stream

2

verbtransitive / intransitive

to send or receive video or music over the internet as it plays

phát trực tuyến

  • We streamed the match on my phone.
  • The concert was streamed live.

stream a film · live-streamed

Watch out

Size order: a stream is smaller than a river; a brook is smaller still.

bamboo

/ˌbæmˈbuː/10

noununcountable / countable

a tall tropical plant with hard hollow stems, used for building and for making furniture

cây tre, tre nứa

  • The fence is made of bamboo.
  • Bamboo grows very quickly — up to a metre a day.
  • She was carrying two baskets on a bamboo pole.

a bamboo pole · a bamboo fence · a bamboo forest

Watch out

Usually uncountable when you mean the material: made of bamboo. Stress the second syllable: bam-BOO.

vast

/vɑːst/11

adjective

extremely large in area or amount

rộng lớn, mênh mông

  • A vast paddy field stretched to the mountains.
  • The country has vast areas of forest.
  • There is a vast difference between the two villages.

a vast area · a vast majority · vast amounts of

Word family

  • vastly adverb very much; to a great degree vô cùng, rất nhiềuLife here is vastly different from the city.

Watch out

Stronger than big or large, and used for open space or quantity — not usually for a single object. Rhymes with fast.

a peaceful village · peaceful and quiet

adjective

not involving war, violence or argument

a peaceful solution · a peaceful protest

load

/ləʊd/13
1

verbtransitive

to put a large quantity of things onto a vehicle or into a container

chất, xếp (hàng lên xe)

  • They loaded the rice onto the cart.
  • Help me load the truck before it rains.
  • The buffalo cart was loaded with bamboo.

load a truck · load something onto something

2

nouncountable

something heavy that is being carried

gánh nặng, chuyến hàng

  • He carried a heavy load of firewood.
  • That's the third load of hay today.

a heavy load · carry a load

Watch out

Opposite: unload. Note the pair load the cart (the cart is the object) and load the rice onto the cart (the rice is).

pick fruit

/ˌpɪk ˈfruːt/14

phraseverb + noun

to take fruit off a tree or plant by hand

hái quả, hái trái cây

  • In summer we pick fruit in my uncle's orchard.
  • The children love to pick fruit straight from the tree.
  • We spent the morning picking fruit and the afternoon swimming.

pick fruit from a tree · fruit picking

Watch out

Pick here means take off the plant. Pick up is different — it means lift something from the ground.

nhặt trứng, thu trứng

  • Every morning I collect eggs from the hen house.
  • It's my job to collect the eggs before school.
  • She collected six eggs today.

collect eggs from the hen house

ride a horse

/ˌraɪd ə ˈhɔːs/16

phraseverb + noun

to sit on a horse and control it as it moves

cưỡi ngựa

  • My cousin can ride a horse very well.
  • I learned to ride a horse when I was eight.
  • They rode horses along the beach at sunset.

learn to ride a horse · go horse riding

Watch out

Irregular: ride → rode → ridden. Use ride for horses, bikes and motorbikes; use drive for cars.

fly a kite

/ˌflaɪ ə ˈkaɪt/17

phraseverb + noun

to make a kite rise and stay in the air on the end of a string

thả diều

  • On windy afternoons we fly kites on the hill.
  • He taught his little sister to fly a kite.
  • It's too still today — you can't fly a kite without wind.

fly a kite in the wind · kite flying

Irregular: fly → flew → flown.

Pronunciation — /ə/ and /ɪ/

Both are short. The difference is stress and tongue position.

  • /ə/ (the schwa) only ever appears in an unstressed syllable. The mouth is completely relaxed — it is the laziest sound in English.
  • /ɪ/ is a real short vowel. It can be stressed (village, hill) or unstressed (chicken, farming). The tongue is higher and further forward, and the lips are slightly spread.
/ə/ — relaxed, never stressed /ɪ/ — short, tongue high and forward
Where it hides in weak syllables: -er, -ar, -our, a-, o- in strong syllables, and in -ing, -est
Mouth fully relaxed, jaw loose lips slightly spread, tongue raised
Examples farmer, water, brother, orchard, neighbour, about, collect village, chicken, hill, pick, milk, bridge

A useful pair to feel the difference — the endings of the comparative and the superlative:

/ə/ /ɪ/
bigger /ˈbɪɡə/ biggest /ˈbɪɡɪst/
farmer /ˈfɑːmə/ farming /ˈfɑːmɪŋ/
harder /ˈhɑːdə/ hardest /ˈhɑːdɪst/

Mẹo: /ə/ là âm "lười" — miệng thả lỏng hoàn toàn, không bao giờ được nhấn trọng âm. Còn /ɪ/ thì lưỡi nâng cao hơn, môi hơi bè ra. Hãy so sánh đuôi -er (/ə/) với đuôi -est và -ing (/ɪ/): bigger — biggest.
