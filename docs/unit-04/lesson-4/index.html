Reading — The house that stands on legs

Tí, 13, writes down the Saturday he went up the river with ten questions.

Timed test

18 minutes

The text and its questions open when you start. The clock does not stop.

Reading passage

AWe walked the old path for two hours and passed nothing — no village, no gate, no person. Ten questions in my pocket and nobody to ask them. What do you put in an answer column when there is nobody on the path? So I sat on the bank and waited for the water to turn, and Bống said the two words. This far up the river it still turns, twice a day, the same as at home. The bamboo across the water moved and stopped, because a house was standing in it: a stilt house, clear of the ground on tall wooden legs, with a wooden staircase to the door.

BThảo got there before I did. "Who built it? How long does wood like that last?" Neither of those was on my sheet.

CIt was built of wood and bamboo, and the roof was palm leaves. Underneath it people had kept tools and firewood and, by the smell, animals, and the floor above was dry, the way a floor stays dry in the rainy season. Cool air moved under it while we stood in the sun. Inside was one large room, an open fire in the middle, three baskets and a gong on the wall. How many people sleep around one fire? How much firewood does a room like that need in a wet month?

D"The sea gives back what it took," Bống said. "You don't choose which, and it comes back the way it went. That is four marks on our wall now." Cô Yến's sheet says there are fifty-four ethnic groups, that the Kinh are the majority, and that the other fifty-three are ethnic minorities. Which of the fifty-three put this house here? The sheet does not say, and neither did the house. Down in Bãi Sẻ the new houses go up in a month in brick and concrete; a few villages upriver have begun to restore their old ones. Nobody restored this one. Cô Yến calls a house like that heritage, and I did not understand the word until Saturday.

EThen I asked it my questions. Who lived here? How many people slept in this room? How much rice did they grow, and how many years did they grow it for? Thảo left the answer column empty. Two of the baskets were half finished. Somebody had put the work down meaning to come back to it.

Writing — A paragraph about what you do for your family

Task. Your class is making a booklet called At home, I help. Write a paragraph of 80–100 words about what you do for your family. Say what you do every day, what you do at the weekend, and how you feel about it.

Model paragraph — read this first

In my family everyone has a job to do, and mine is not the hardest one. Every morning I sweep the yard and feed the chickens before school. After dinner I carry the water in and wash the bowls, because my grandmother's knees hurt on the stairs. At the weekend I go with her to the market and carry the heavy baskets home. She is teaching me to weave bamboo in the traditional way, because it is an old custom here. My baskets are still crooked, but she says I ask better questions than I did.

ethnic group

/ˈeθnɪk ɡruːp/1

noundân tộc, nhóm dân tộc

nouncountable

a group of people who share the same language, culture and origins

dân tộc, nhóm dân tộc

  • Viet Nam has 54 ethnic groups.
  • Each ethnic group has its own costume and festivals.
  • The Tay are the largest ethnic group in the north.

a different ethnic group · belong to an ethnic group

Word family

  • ethnic adjective connected with a group that shares a culture and origin thuộc về dân tộcEthnic costumes are worn at the festival.

ethnic minority

/ˌeθnɪk maɪˈnɒrəti/2

noundân tộc thiểu số

nouncountable

an ethnic group that is small in number compared with the main group in a country

dân tộc thiểu số

  • Many ethnic minorities live in the mountains.
  • The school teaches children from three ethnic minorities.
  • Ethnic minority languages are taught in some provinces.

an ethnic minority group · ethnic minority languages

Watch out

Opposite of majority. The plural is minorities — change y to ies.

majority

/məˈdʒɒrəti/3

nounđa số, phần lớn

nounsingular, + of

the largest part of a group; more than half

đa số, phần lớn

  • The majority of people here are farmers.
  • The Kinh are the majority in Viet Nam.
  • A majority of students voted for the trip.

the vast majority · a majority of

Watch out

Opposite: minority. With the majority of + plural noun, the verb is usually plural: the majority of students are …

custom

/ˈkʌstəm/4

nounphong tục, tập quán

1

nouncountable

a way of behaving that a group of people has followed for a long time

phong tục, tập quán

  • It is a custom to give lucky money at Tet.
  • Every village has its own customs.
  • Taking off your shoes indoors is a local custom.

a local custom · an old custom · follow a custom

2

nounplural: customs

the place at a border or airport where officials check what you are bringing in

hải quan

  • We waited an hour to get through customs.
  • You must declare it at customs.

go through customs · a customs officer

Watch out

A custom is what a group does; a habit is what one person does. Compare tradition, which is usually older and passed down through generations.

/ˈkɒstjuːm/5

Costume is special or traditional clothing, not everyday clothes — for those use clothes. Careful with the spelling: cos-tume, not custom.

communal house

/kəˈmjuːnl haʊs/19

nounnhà rông, nhà sinh hoạt cộng đồng

nouncountable

a large building that belongs to a whole village and is used for meetings, festivals and ceremonies

Two words, and the stress falls on the second syllable of communal: /kəˈmjuːnl/. It is a building for the whole community, not a house anybody lives in — so my communal house is almost never right.

livestock

/ˈlaɪvstɒk/20

noungia súc, vật nuôi

noununcountable, plural verb

the animals a farm keeps for work, food or wool — cattle, buffaloes, pigs, goats, sheep

gia súc, vật nuôi

  • Her family's livestock graze on the hillside above the village.
  • In the dry season there is not much grass for the livestock.
  • Under the floor of the stilt house they used to keep tools and livestock.

Watch out

No plural -s: ❌ many livestocks → ✅ a lot of livestock. The word looks singular but takes a plural verb: the livestock are in the field. It does not cover chickens and ducks — those are poultry.
