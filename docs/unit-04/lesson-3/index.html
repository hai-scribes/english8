A Closer Look 2

Grammar A — Yes/No questions

A Yes/No question can be answered with yes or no. English makes one by putting an operator in front of the subject.

The statement has… Do this Example
be (am / is / are / was / were) move be to the front She is Tay. → Is she Tay?
a modal (can, could, will, should, must) move the modal to the front He can weave. → Can he weave?
only a main verb add do / does / did They live here. → Do they live here?

⚠️ When you add do / does / did, the main verb goes back to its base form.

✅ Does your grandmother weave baskets? ❌ Does your grandmother weaves baskets? ✅ Did you visit the village last summer? ❌ Did you visited the village last summer?

Short answers repeat the operator, never the main verb:

Is it a stilt house? — Yes, it is. / No, it isn't. Do they grow rice? — Yes, they do. / No, they don't. Can you play the gong? — Yes, I can. / No, I can't.

Grammar B — Wh- questions

A Wh- question asks for information. The pattern is:

Wh- word + operator + subject + main verb + …?

Wh- word Asks about Nghĩa Example
what a thing cái gì What is your costume made of?
where a place ở đâu Where do the Tay live?
when a time khi nào When does the festival start?
why a reason tại sao Why do they build houses on stilts?
who a person ai Who did you visit?
how many number (countable) bao nhiêu How many rooms are there?
how much amount (uncountable) bao nhiêu How much rice do they grow?
how long duration bao lâu How long did you stay?

Grammar C — Subject questions: the real trap

When who or what is the subject of the sentence — when it stands where the doer stands — you do not use do / does / did. The word order stays exactly like a statement.

My uncle repairs the roof. → Who repairs the roof? ✅ ❌ Who does repair the roof?

Bamboo makes the walls strong. → What makes the walls strong? ✅ ❌ What does make the walls strong?

Compare the two kinds carefully:

Question Notice
Subject question Who lives in the stilt house? no does; verb takes -s
Object question Who does she live with? does + base verb
Subject question What happened at the festival? no did
Object question What did you see at the festival? did + base verb

Grammar D — Countable and uncountable nouns

Countable nouns can be counted: one basket, two baskets. They have a plural form. Uncountable nouns cannot be counted directly: they have no plural -s and never take a / an.

Countable Uncountable
a basket / two baskets rice
a house / three houses food
a song / many songs music
a suggestion / some suggestions advice
a fact / some facts information
a shirt / two shirts clothing
a bracelet / five bracelets jewellery
Use With countable With uncountable
singular a / an basket —
indefinite quantity (+) some baskets some rice
indefinite quantity (– / ?) any baskets any rice
large quantity (– / ?) many baskets much rice
large quantity (+) a lot of baskets a lot of rice
asking How many baskets? How much rice?

To count an uncountable noun, put a unit in front of it:

a piece of information · a piece of advice · a bowl of rice · a bottle of water · a bar of soap · a loaf of bread

⚠️ Bẫy thường gặp: Sáu danh từ dưới đây không đếm được trong tiếng Anh, dù tiếng Việt vẫn nói "những/các": information, rice, food, clothing, advice, jewellery. Vì vậy: ❌ many informations → ✅ a lot of information ❌ an advice → ✅ a piece of advice ❌ many rices → ✅ a lot of rice ❌ many jewelleries → ✅ a lot of jewellery Nhớ cặp đôi: How many…? đi với danh từ đếm được (số nhiều), How much…? đi với danh từ không đếm được.

Make the question

MarkedBuild the sentence

Build a full sentence from the words you are given. Change the form of a word where you need to, and write the whole sentence. Here the words you are given are a statement. Turn it into the question that the bold part answers, and write the whole question. Two of them are subject questions, so watch what happens to do/does/did.

  • Spelling counts.
  • British and American spellings are both accepted.
  1. Who lives in the northern mountains? (who is the subject here, so no do — and the verb takes -s)
  2. When does she weave baskets? / What time does she weave baskets?
  3. How many people are there in his family?
  4. Why do they build houses on stilts?
  5. Who makes your festival costumes? / Who makes our festival costumes? (another subject question: no does, and makes keeps its -s)
  6. How long did you stay in the village? / How long did we stay in the village? (after did, the main verb goes back to stay)

Correct the mistake

MarkedCorrect the mistake

Each sentence has exactly one mistake. Write only the words that should replace the wrong ones — not the whole sentence. The mistake is always in the question form or in the countable/uncountable word.

  • Spelling counts.
  • British and American spellings are both accepted.
  1. do your grandparents live/do (a Wh- question needs an operator before the subject)
  2. weave (after does, the main verb goes back to its base form)
  3. who lives/lives (who is the subject here, so there is no does)
  4. how many/many (rooms can be counted)
  5. much useful information/a lot of useful information/some useful information/much information (information is uncountable and has no plural)
  6. visit (after did, the main verb goes back to its base form)

About you

Write true answers in full sentences. Then write two more questions you would like to ask a classmate from a different ethnic group.

  1. Where does your family live?
  2. How many people are there in your home?
  3. What traditional food does your family cook at Tet?
  4. Who does most of the cooking in your house?
  5. Your question 1:
  6. Your question 2:

Answers will vary. Check: every answer is a full sentence in the present simple with the -s where it is needed; and your two new questions have the order Wh- + do/does + subject + base verb — unless they are subject questions, which take no do/does.
