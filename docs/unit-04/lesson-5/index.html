Unit 04 · Ethnic Groups of Viet Nam · Lesson 5 of 7

Skills 1: Reading & Speaking

Reading — *The house that stands on legs*

From a school magazine, written by Hoa, 14.

Viet Nam is home to 54 ethnic groups. The Kinh are the majority, and the other 53 groups are called ethnic minorities. Some — the Tay, the Thai, the Muong, the Khmer, the H'Mong, the Dao — have quite large populations. Others are very small. Each group has its own customs, its own costumes, and in many cases its own language.

Travel into the mountains of the north and you will see wooden houses standing above the ground on tall legs. These are stilt houses — nhà sàn. Why build a house in the air? There are good reasons. The floor stays dry in the rainy season. Insects and animals find it harder to get in. Cool air moves under the floor on hot days. And the space underneath is useful: families keep tools, firewood and sometimes animals there.

A stilt house is usually built of wood and bamboo, and the roof may be palm leaves or tiles. You climb a wooden staircase to reach the door. Inside there is often one large room instead of several small ones. The family cooks, eats, talks and sleeps in that single space, and guests are welcomed near the fire.

Life in the mountains is changing. Some families now build houses of brick and concrete, which are quicker to put up and easier to repair. But many young people are proud of the old houses, and a few villages have begun to restore them. A stilt house is not only somewhere to sleep. It is a piece of heritage that a family can stand inside.

5.1

True, False, or Not Given

Write T, F, or NG.

  1. There are 54 ethnic groups in Viet Nam.
  2. The Kinh are one of the ethnic minorities.
  3. Every ethnic minority group has a small population.
  4. A stilt house is cooler because air moves under the floor.
  5. Most mountain families now live in brick houses.
  6. Hoa's own family lives in a stilt house.
  1. T 2. F (the Kinh are the majority) 3. F (some, such as the Tay and the Thai, have quite large populations) 4. T 5. NG (the text says some families now build brick houses; it does not say most) 6. NG (the text never says where Hoa lives)
5.2

Answer the questions

  1. What are the 53 groups other than the Kinh called?
  2. Give two reasons for building a house on stilts.
  3. What materials is a stilt house usually built of?
  4. What do families keep in the space under the floor?
  5. Why do some families now choose brick and concrete?
  1. Ethnic minorities. 2. Any two of: the floor stays dry in the rainy season; insects and animals find it harder to get in; cool air moves under the floor on hot days; the space underneath is useful for storage. 3. Wood and bamboo, with a roof of palm leaves or tiles. 4. Tools, firewood and sometimes animals. 5. Because brick and concrete houses are quicker to put up and easier to repair.

Speaking — Talking about homes

Working alone: You have no partner, so do this in three steps. It still works — speaking practice is mostly about producing language out loud.

Step 1 — Prepare. Write short notes (not full sentences) for each question.

Question Your notes
What kind of home do you live in?
How many rooms does it have, and who lives there?
What is your home made of?
What are two advantages of a stilt house?
Would you like to live in one? Why / why not?

Step 2 — Speak. Answer each question aloud from your notes, in full sentences. Then ask the five questions aloud as if you were interviewing someone — an interview is questions, and questions are this unit's grammar.

Step 3 — Record and check. Record yourself on your phone. Listen back and check three things:

  • Did every Wh- question have the right word order — *Wh- + operator + subject
  • verb? And did your subject questions correctly have no do/does*?
  • Did you say How many rooms but How much rice?
  • Did /k/ and /ɡ/ sound different in coat and goat, custom and gong?

Repeat Step 2 until you can do all five without stopping.
