A Closer Look 1

Vocabulary — Ethnic groups and their homes

ethnic group

/ˈeθnɪk ɡruːp/1

nouncountable

a group of people who share the same language, culture and origins

dân tộc, nhóm dân tộc

  • Viet Nam has 54 ethnic groups.
  • Each ethnic group has its own costume and festivals.
  • The Tay are the largest ethnic group in the north.

a different ethnic group · belong to an ethnic group

Word family

  • ethnic adjective connected with a group that shares a culture and origin thuộc về dân tộcEthnic costumes are worn at the festival.

ethnic minority

/ˌeθnɪk maɪˈnɒrəti/2

nouncountable

an ethnic group that is small in number compared with the main group in a country

dân tộc thiểu số

  • Many ethnic minorities live in the mountains.
  • The school teaches children from three ethnic minorities.
  • Ethnic minority languages are taught in some provinces.

an ethnic minority group · ethnic minority languages

Watch out

Opposite of majority. The plural is minorities — change y to ies.

majority

/məˈdʒɒrəti/3

nounsingular, + of

the largest part of a group; more than half

đa số, phần lớn

  • The majority of people here are farmers.
  • The Kinh are the majority in Viet Nam.
  • A majority of students voted for the trip.

the vast majority · a majority of

Watch out

Opposite: minority. With the majority of + plural noun, the verb is usually plural: the majority of students are …

custom

/ˈkʌstəm/4
1

nouncountable

a way of behaving that a group of people has followed for a long time

phong tục, tập quán

  • It is a custom to give lucky money at Tet.
  • Every village has its own customs.
  • Taking off your shoes indoors is a local custom.

a local custom · an old custom · follow a custom

2

nounplural: customs

the place at a border or airport where officials check what you are bringing in

hải quan

  • We waited an hour to get through customs.
  • You must declare it at customs.

go through customs · a customs officer

Watch out

A custom is what a group does; a habit is what one person does. Compare tradition, which is usually older and passed down through generations.

costume

/ˈkɒstjuːm/5

nouncountable / uncountable

the clothes worn by people of a particular place, period or group, especially at a special event

trang phục (truyền thống, biểu diễn)

  • The dancers wore traditional costume.
  • Each ethnic group has its own colourful costume.
  • She made a costume for the school play.

traditional costume · national costume · wear a costume

Watch out

Costume is special or traditional clothing, not everyday clothes — for those use clothes. Careful with the spelling: cos-tume, not custom.

stilt house

/ˈstɪlt haʊs/6

nouncountable

a house built on tall posts above the ground or above water

nhà sàn

  • Many ethnic groups live in stilt houses.
  • The stilt house keeps the family dry in the rainy season.
  • We climbed a wooden ladder into the stilt house.

live in a stilt house · a wooden stilt house

Word family

  • stilt noun one of the long posts a building stands on cột nhà sànThe house stands on twelve stilts.

terraced field

/ˌterəst ˈfiːld/7

nouncountable

one of a series of flat steps cut into a hillside so that crops can be grown on it

ruộng bậc thang

  • The terraced fields of Sa Pa are famous.
  • Rice is grown on terraced fields in the mountains.
  • The terraced fields look like giant steps.

terraced rice fields · grow rice on terraced fields

Word family

  • terrace noun a flat level area cut into a slope bậc thang, thềmEach terrace holds a few centimetres of water.

heritage

/ˈherɪtɪdʒ/8

noununcountable

the traditions, buildings and culture a country has had for many years and still values

di sản (văn hoá)

  • Ha Long Bay is a World Heritage Site.
  • Folk songs are part of our cultural heritage.
  • The village is proud of its heritage.

cultural heritage · a World Heritage Site · preserve our heritage

Watch out

Uncountable — never heritages. Stress the first syllable: HER-i-tage.

ritual

/ˈrɪtʃuəl/9
1

nouncountable

a set of actions always performed in the same way, especially as part of a ceremony

nghi lễ, nghi thức

  • The ritual is performed at the start of the harvest.
  • Lighting incense is an important ritual.
  • The wedding follows an ancient ritual.

perform a ritual · a religious ritual · an ancient ritual

2

nouncountable, everyday use

something you always do in the same way, out of habit

thói quen cố định

  • Sunday lunch with my grandparents is a family ritual.
  • Coffee at seven is his morning ritual.

a daily ritual · a family ritual

folk song

/ˈfəʊk sɒŋ/10

nouncountable

a traditional song of a particular region, usually passed on by being sung rather than written down

dân ca

  • She sang an old folk song at the festival.
  • Folk songs tell the story of the village.
  • My grandmother taught me three folk songs.

sing a folk song · a traditional folk song

Word family

  • folk adjective traditional, belonging to ordinary people of a region dân gianFolk tales are told at bedtime.

Watch out

The l in folk is silent: /fəʊk/.

bamboo

/ˌbæmˈbuː/11

noununcountable / countable

a tall tropical plant with hard hollow stems, used for building and for making furniture

cây tre, tre nứa

  • The fence is made of bamboo.
  • Bamboo grows very quickly — up to a metre a day.
  • She was carrying two baskets on a bamboo pole.

a bamboo pole · a bamboo fence · a bamboo forest

Watch out

Usually uncountable when you mean the material: made of bamboo. Stress the second syllable: bam-BOO.

weave

/wiːv/12

verbtransitive / intransitive

to make cloth or baskets by passing threads or strips over and under each other

dệt, đan

  • The women weave cloth on wooden looms.
  • She learned to weave baskets from her mother.
  • This scarf was woven by hand.

weave cloth · weave a basket · hand-woven

Word family

  • weaving noun the craft of making cloth nghề dệtWeaving is a traditional craft here.
  • weaver noun a person who weaves thợ dệtShe is the best weaver in the village.

Watch out

Irregular: weave → wove → woven.

basket

/ˈbɑːskɪt/13

nouncountable

a container made of thin strips of wood, bamboo or cane woven together

cái giỏ, cái rổ

  • She carried the fruit in a bamboo basket.
  • The baskets are woven by hand.
  • Put the eggs in that basket.

a bamboo basket · a shopping basket · a basket of fruit

gong

/ɡɒŋ/14

nouncountable

a round metal disc that makes a deep ringing sound when you hit it

cái cồng, cái chiêng

  • The gong is played at village ceremonies.
  • Gong culture in the Central Highlands is a World Heritage tradition.
  • He struck the gong three times.

beat a gong · play the gong · gong culture

cave

/keɪv/15

nouncountable

a large natural hole in the side of a hill or under the ground

hang, động

  • Son Doong is the largest cave in the world.
  • Early people lived in caves.
  • We explored a cave near the village.

explore a cave · a limestone cave

unique

/juˈniːk/16

adjective

being the only one of its kind; unlike anything else

độc đáo, độc nhất vô nhị

  • Each ethnic group has its own unique costume.
  • The festival is unique to this province.
  • Every fingerprint is unique.

absolutely unique · unique to a place

Watch out

Because it means the only one, careful writers avoid very unique or quite unique — something is either unique or it isn't. Pronounced /juˈniːk/: yoo-NEEK.

diverse

/daɪˈvɜːs/17

adjective

very different from each other; of many different kinds

đa dạng, phong phú

  • Viet Nam is culturally very diverse.
  • The country has a diverse range of landscapes.
  • Our class is diverse — six languages are spoken.

culturally diverse · a diverse range

Word family

  • diversity noun the fact of including many different kinds sự đa dạngCultural diversity is a strength.

traditional

/trəˈdɪʃənl/18

adjective

following ideas, customs or methods that have existed for a long time

truyền thống, cổ truyền

  • They wore traditional costumes for the festival.
  • Weaving is a traditional craft in this village.
  • Traditional music is played on the gong.

traditional dress · a traditional festival · traditional music

Word family

  • tradition noun a belief or custom passed from one generation to the next truyền thốngIt is a tradition to visit relatives at Tet.
  • traditionally adverb in the way that has been usual for a long time theo truyền thốngTraditionally, the house faces south.

Odd one out

MarkedChoose the right one

In each line, one word does not belong with the others. Pick it.

  • One mark each. Nothing is part-marked.
  1. c (the others are materials; a basket is a thing made from them)
  2. d (the others are cultural traditions; a market is a place)
  3. d (the others are places; a costume is clothing)

Pronunciation — /k/ and /ɡ/

These two sounds are made in exactly the same place — the back of the tongue against the soft palate. The only difference is voice.

/k/ — voiceless /ɡ/ — voiced
Voice box silent, no buzz buzzing
Air a small puff of air at the start of a word almost no puff
Test cover your ears and say it — quiet cover your ears — you hear a hum inside your head
Examples coat, custom, costume, cave, culture, market, basket, folk goat, group, gong, garment, guitar, ethnic group, language

Spelling traps — check the sound, not the letter:

  • gh in ghost /ɡəʊst/ is a plain /ɡ/ — the h is silent.
  • c in ceremony /ˈserəməni/ is /s/, not /k/. Before e, i and y, the letter c is usually /s/: ceremony, city, cycle.
  • g in village /ˈvɪlɪdʒ/ is /dʒ/, not /ɡ/.
  • l in folk /fəʊk/ is silent, but the /k/ at the end is not.

Mẹo: Đặt tay lên cổ họng. Nói coat — cổ họng im. Nói goat — bạn thấy rung. Người Việt thường phát âm /ɡ/ thành /k/ ở cuối từ; hãy giữ độ rung đến hết âm: dog, ba**g, flag**.
