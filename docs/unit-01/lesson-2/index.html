A Closer Look 1

Vocabulary — Leisure time

leisure

/ˈleʒə(r)/1
1

noununcountable

time when you are not working or studying, and can do what you enjoy

thời gian rảnh rỗi

  • In her leisure time, my sister does jigsaw puzzles.
  • Most teenagers have very little leisure during exam week.
  • The town has a new leisure centre with a pool and a gym.

leisure time · leisure activities · a leisure centre

2

noununcountable, formal

the state of having time to relax, with no work you must do

sự nhàn hạ, sự thảnh thơi

  • He retired early and now lives a life of leisure.
  • Read the contract at your leisure and sign it next week.

a life of leisure · at your leisure

Word family

  • leisurely adjective done slowly, without hurrying thong thả, thư tháiWe had a leisurely breakfast and left at ten.

Watch out

Usually sits before another noun: leisure time, leisure activities. The first syllable rhymes with measure, not with seize.

pastime

/ˈpɑːstaɪm/2

nouncountable

something you do regularly for pleasure when you are not working

thú tiêu khiển, trò giải trí

  • Fishing is his favourite pastime.
  • Board games are a cheap pastime for a rainy Sunday.
  • Collecting stamps was a popular pastime fifty years ago.

a favourite pastime · a popular pastime · a national pastime

Watch out

One word, one s — not past time. Close to hobby, but a pastime can be more casual: a hobby usually needs equipment or skill.

hang out (with)

/ˌhæŋ ˈaʊt/3
1

phrasal verbinformal

to spend time somewhere, or with someone, in a relaxed way with no particular plan

đi chơi, la cà, tụ tập

  • On Sundays I hang out with my cousins at the park.
  • They hang out at the shopping centre near the swimming pool.
  • We just hung out at home and watched films all afternoon.

hang out with friends · hang out at the park

2

phrasal verbliteral

to put washing outside on a line so that it dries

phơi (quần áo)

  • Could you hang out the washing before it rains?
  • My grandmother hangs the sheets out every morning.

hang out the washing

Word family

  • hangout noun a place someone often goes to relax chỗ hay lui tớiThat café is their usual hangout.

Watch out

Informal. Use with for people and at / in for places: hang out with Nam at the café. Irregular: hang → hung → hung.

socialise

/ˈsəʊʃəlaɪz/4

verbintransitive

to meet and spend time with other people in a friendly way

giao lưu, gặp gỡ mọi người

  • He loves to socialise with new people.
  • I don't socialise much during the week because of homework.
  • A club is a good place to socialise with people your own age.

socialise with someone · a chance to socialise

Word family

  • social adjective connected with meeting people and spending time with them thuộc về xã hội, giao tiếpShe has a busy social life.
  • sociable adjective friendly and enjoying the company of others hoà đồng, dễ gầnHe is quiet but very sociable once you know him.

Watch out

British spelling socialise; American socialize. Both are correct — just be consistent. Do not confuse social (about society or meeting people) with sociable (describing a friendly person).

take up

/ˌteɪk ˈʌp/5
1

phrasal verbseparable

to start doing a new hobby, sport or activity

bắt đầu (một sở thích, môn thể thao mới)

  • I want to take up photography next year — I've just bought a camera.
  • My brother has taken up woodwork.
  • She decided to take up the guitar after watching the concert.

take up a hobby · take up the guitar · take up cycling

2

phrasal verbseparable

to fill an amount of space or time

chiếm (chỗ, thời gian)

  • That old sofa takes up half the room.
  • Homework takes up most of my evening.

take up space · take up time

3

phrasal verbseparable

to accept an offer or an invitation

chấp nhận (lời mời, đề nghị)

  • Thanks for the offer — I'd like to take you up on it.
  • She took up the job in Da Nang.

take up an offer · take somebody up on something

Watch out

Separable: take up the guitar = take the guitar up. With a pronoun it must split: take it up, never take up it. Irregular: take → took → taken.

DIY

/ˌdiː aɪ ˈwaɪ/6

noununcountable, abbreviation

do-it-yourself: making or repairing things in your home yourself instead of paying someone

tự làm, tự sửa chữa (đồ trong nhà)

  • My dad spends every Sunday doing DIY.
  • She made the bookshelf herself — it was a DIY project.
  • There's a DIY shop at the end of our street.

do DIY · a DIY project · a DIY shop

Watch out

Uncountable — never a DIY on its own, but a DIY project is fine. Say the three letters: dee-eye-WHY, with the stress on the last one.

craft

/krɑːft/7
1

nouncountable / uncountable

an activity in which you make things skilfully with your hands; also the objects you make

nghề thủ công; đồ thủ công

  • My grandmother makes paper flowers. Her crafts are lovely.
  • We learn a traditional craft at school on Fridays.
  • The village is famous for the craft of silk weaving.

arts and crafts · a traditional craft · a craft fair

2

nouncountable, plural: craft

a boat, an aircraft or a spacecraft

tàu, thuyền, phi thuyền

  • Several small craft were sheltering in the harbour.
  • The landing craft reached the beach at dawn.

a small craft · a landing craft

3

verbtransitive

to make something skilfully, especially by hand

chế tác, làm thủ công

  • Each bowl is crafted from a single piece of wood.
  • She crafts jewellery from recycled metal.

hand-crafted · carefully crafted

Word family

  • craftsman noun a skilled worker who makes things by hand thợ thủ côngThe chair was made by a local craftsman.

Watch out

In the boat sense the plural is also craft, never crafts: three fishing craft.

cook

/kʊk/8
1

verbtransitive / intransitive

to prepare food by heating it

nấu ăn, nấu nướng

  • My dad and my brother cook lunch on Sundays.
  • The rice is still cooking — give it five more minutes.
  • My grandfather is teaching me to cook one new dish each week.

cook a meal · learn to cook · cook dinner

2

nouncountable

a person who cooks, especially as their job or at home

đầu bếp, người nấu ăn

  • He is a good cook, but he can't stand following a recipe.
  • The school has two cooks and they feed four hundred children.

a good cook · a terrible cook

Watch out

Short /ʊ/ — the vowel of book, not the long /uː/ of food. A cook works at home or in a small kitchen; a chef is trained and works professionally.

bookshop

/ˈbʊkʃɒp/9

nouncountable

a shop that sells books

hiệu sách, nhà sách

  • We bought the book at the new bookshop in town.
  • A new bookshop opened near the swimming pool.
  • There is a café upstairs in that bookshop.

a local bookshop · a second-hand bookshop

Watch out

British English; Americans say bookstore. Short /ʊ/ in book. Compare library, where you borrow books instead of buying them — a common false friend for Vietnamese learners.

football

/ˈfʊtbɔːl/10
1

noununcountable

a game played by two teams of eleven players who kick a ball into a goal

môn bóng đá

  • Last month we started playing football in the park.
  • My cousin enjoys playing football every Saturday.
  • He was too tired after the football match to do anything.

play football · a football match · a football team

2

nouncountable

the ball used in this game

quả bóng đá

  • My little brother got a new football for his birthday.
  • The football went over the fence and into the garden.

kick a football · a leather football

Watch out

Play football — no the. First vowel is the short /ʊ/ of put, not the long /uː/ of food. In American English this game is soccer, and football means a different sport.

board game

/ˈbɔːd ɡeɪm/11

nouncountable

a game such as chess, played on a marked board with pieces or counters

trò chơi cờ bàn (cờ, cá ngựa…)

  • In the evening we play board games.
  • There is a big table with board games upstairs in the café.
  • Chess is the only board game my grandmother ever loses.

play a board game · a board game night

Watch out

Two words. Board /bɔːd/ sounds exactly like bored — same sound, completely different word. You play a board game, but you do a jigsaw puzzle.

jigsaw puzzle

/ˈdʒɪɡsɔː ˌpʌzl/12

nouncountable

a picture printed on card or wood and cut into many small pieces that you fit back together

trò chơi xếp hình

  • In my free time I like doing jigsaw puzzles.
  • This jigsaw puzzle has a thousand pieces and I've already lost two.
  • We finished the jigsaw puzzle on the last day of the holiday.

do a jigsaw puzzle · a 500-piece jigsaw puzzle

Watch out

You do a jigsaw puzzle — never play one. Often shortened to just a jigsaw.

movie

/ˈmuːvi/13
1

nouncountable

a story told in moving pictures that you watch at a cinema or at home

bộ phim

  • I usually stay home and watch movies.
  • Why don't we watch a movie tonight?
  • I can't stand any movie that is longer than two hours.

watch a movie · a horror movie · an action movie

2

nounplural: the movies, especially American

the cinema, as a place you go to

rạp chiếu phim

  • Do you want to go to the movies on Saturday?
  • We hadn't been to the movies since March.

go to the movies

Watch out

Long /uː/ — the vowel of food, not book. British speakers usually say film, and go to the cinema rather than go to the movies.

music

/ˈmjuːzɪk/14

noununcountable

sounds arranged in patterns to be sung or played on instruments

âm nhạc

  • I like listening to music while I do my homework.
  • He can't stand loud music when he is trying to study.
  • She has been studying music since she was six.

listen to music · play music · loud music

Word family

  • musician noun a person who plays a musical instrument or writes music nhạc sĩ, nhạc côngHer brother is a professional musician.
  • musical adjective connected with music, or good at music thuộc về âm nhạc; có khiếu nhạcHe comes from a very musical family.

Watch out

Uncountable — never a music or musics. Say a piece of music or a song. Begins /mjuː/, with a y sound: myoo-zik.

swimming pool

/ˈswɪmɪŋ puːl/15

nouncountable

a large structure filled with water for people to swim in

bể bơi, hồ bơi

  • Look at the blue swimming pool near the bookshop.
  • We meet at the shopping centre near the swimming pool.
  • The water in the swimming pool was far too cold.

an indoor swimming pool · go to the swimming pool

Watch out

Pool has the long /uː/ of food. Stress the first word: SWIMming pool. Often shortened to the pool.

choose

/tʃuːz/16
1

verbtransitive / intransitive

to decide which thing or person you want from a number of them

chọn, lựa chọn

  • We couldn't choose between the film and the football match.
  • My mum and I choose the food at the market.
  • You can choose any three books from this shelf.

choose between A and B · choose from a list · hard to choose

2

verb+ to-infinitive

to decide to do something, when you could have done otherwise

quyết định (làm gì)

  • She chose to stay at home instead of going out.
  • He chose not to answer the question.

choose to do something

Word family

  • choice noun the act of choosing, or the thing chosen sự lựa chọnIt was a difficult choice.

Watch out

Irregular: choose → chose /tʃəʊz/ → chosen. Two os in the present, one in the past. Long /uː/, ending in a /z/ sound.

relax

/rɪˈlæks/17
1

verbintransitive

to rest and stop feeling worried or tense

thư giãn, nghỉ ngơi

  • I read comics to relax after school.
  • Sunday is the only day my whole family can relax together.
  • Just relax — the exam isn't until next week.

relax after work · sit back and relax · help you relax

2

verbtransitive

to make a rule, a muscle or a grip less tight or less strict

nới lỏng, làm giãn ra

  • The school has relaxed its rules about mobile phones.
  • Relax your shoulders and breathe slowly.

relax the rules · relax your muscles

Word family

  • relaxing adjective making you feel calm — describes a thing gây thư giãnA relaxing afternoon by the river.
  • relaxed adjective feeling calm — describes a person thoải mái, thư tháiHe looked relaxed and happy.

Watch out

Stress the second syllable: reLAX. Careful with the pair: a holiday is relaxing; you are relaxed.

can't stand

/kɑːnt ˈstænd/18

phrase+ V-ing or + noun

to dislike something very strongly; to be unable to bear it

không chịu được, ghét cay ghét đắng

  • I can't stand loud noise when I'm reading.
  • My dad can't stand following a recipe.
  • She can't stand queuing for tickets in the rain.

can't stand doing something · can't stand the noise

Watch out

Always followed by V-ing, never to-V: I can't stand queuing. ❌ I can't stand to queue. The full form is cannot stand; can't is normal in speech and in most writing.

Pronunciation — /ʊ/ and /uː/

Two sounds that are easy to merge, because English spells both of them "oo". The difference is length and tension.

/ʊ/ — short, relaxed /uː/ — long, tense
Lips slightly rounded, loose tightly rounded, pushed forward
Length very short held about twice as long
Examples book, cook, good, football, put, would food, movie, pool, choose, school, group

Mẹo: Cùng viết là "oo" nhưng phát âm khác nhau. book ngắn, food dài. Hãy đặt tay trước miệng — với /uː/ bạn sẽ thấy môi đẩy ra xa hơn.

⚠️ Don't learn this length from a computer voice. Text-to-speech tools are a good model of which word you are hearing, and a bad model of how long the vowel is: most voices make food and book almost the same length, and some read full as longer than fool — the reverse of the rule above. Use a voice to check the word; use the table, and a human speaker, to check the length.

Lưu ý: Đừng học độ dài nguyên âm từ công cụ đọc văn bản. Máy đọc đúng từ nào, nhưng đọc sai dài bao nhiêu — nhiều giọng đọc food và book dài gần bằng nhau. Hãy tin vào bảng trên và vào giọng người thật.
