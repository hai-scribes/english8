A Closer Look 1

Vocabulary — Communication

telepathy

/təˈlepəθi/1

noununcountable

the ability to send thoughts to another person, or to know what they are thinking, without speaking or writing

thần giao cách cảm

  • Some people believe that twins can use telepathy.
  • In the story, the two friends talk by telepathy across the sea.
  • Scientists have never proved that telepathy is real.

use telepathy · believe in telepathy · communicate by telepathy

Word family

  • telepathic adjective able to send or receive thoughts without words có khả năng thần giao cách cảmThe robot in the film has telepathic powers.

Watch out

Uncountable: say some telepathy, never a telepathy. Watch the stress: te-LEP-a-thy (noun) but tel-e-PATH-ic (adjective) — the stress moves.

telepathic

/ˌtelɪˈpæθɪk/2

adjective

able to share thoughts with someone without speaking, or connected with this ability

thuộc về thần giao cách cảm; có khả năng thần giao cách cảm

  • The alien sends a telepathic message to the captain.
  • My sister and I are almost telepathic — we say the same thing at the same moment.
  • In 2050, people may wear telepathic helmets to talk without words.

a telepathic message · telepathic powers

Word family

  • telepathy noun the ability to send or receive thoughts without words thần giao cách cảmHe says he can read minds by telepathy.
  • telepathically adverb by sending thoughts, not words bằng thần giao cách cảmThe two robots speak to each other telepathically.

Watch out

Stress: tel-e-PATH-ic. Compare the noun te-LEP-a-thy, where the stress is on a different syllable.

video call

/ˈvɪdiəʊ kɔːl/3

nouncountable

a phone or internet call in which you can see the other person on a screen while you talk

cuộc gọi video (cuộc gọi có hình)

  • I have a video call with my cousin in Australia every Sunday.
  • We had a short video call to plan the class project.
  • Her video call froze because the wifi was slow.

make a video call · have a video call · be on a video call

Word family

  • video-call verb to call someone so that you can see and hear each other gọi video cho aiI'll video-call you after dinner.

Watch out

Two words as a noun (a video call), but hyphenated when used as a verb: I video-called my grandma. We make or have a video call — not do a video call.

social media

/ˌsəʊʃl ˈmiːdiə/4

nounusually uncountable

websites and apps that people use to share messages, photos and videos with lots of other people

mạng xã hội

  • My parents limit my time on social media to one hour a day.
  • News travels fast on social media.
  • She learnt about the concert from social media.

on social media · a social media account · spend time on social media

Watch out

Usually uncountable, so we say social media is popular — but some writers treat it as plural (social media are changing news). Both are used; keep one style in a piece of writing. Do not say a social media; say a social media site or a social network.

instant message

/ˌɪnstənt ˈmesɪdʒ/5

nouncountable

a short written message sent over the internet that reaches the other person immediately

tin nhắn tức thời

  • She sent me an instant message to say she was late.
  • I got three instant messages during the lesson.
  • An instant message is quicker than an email.

send an instant message · get an instant message

Word family

  • instant messaging noun the activity of sending short messages over the internet in real time nhắn tin tức thờiInstant messaging has almost replaced text messages in my family.

Watch out

Often shortened to IM in informal writing (I'll IM you). Remember: instant means it arrives at once, so do not add quickly — he sent an instant message quickly sounds strange.

face-to-face

/ˌfeɪs tə ˈfeɪs/6
1

adjectivebefore a noun

happening when two people are together in the same place, not by phone or over the internet

trực tiếp, mặt đối mặt

  • We had a face-to-face meeting instead of a video call.
  • Face-to-face lessons started again after the storm.
  • A face-to-face interview can feel more friendly than an online one.

a face-to-face meeting · face-to-face communication · a face-to-face interview

2

adverb

with the other person in front of you, in the same place

gặp trực tiếp, mặt đối mặt

  • We finally met face-to-face after two years online.
  • Some things are easier to say face-to-face.
  • I'd rather talk to my teacher face-to-face than send an email.

meet face-to-face · talk face-to-face

Watch out

Keep the hyphens: face-to-face (adjective and adverb). Some writers drop them in the adverb (meet face to face), but hyphens are always correct before a noun: a face-to-face chat.

interact

/ˌɪntərˈækt/7

verbintransitive, + with

to talk to and spend time with other people, or to work together with them

tương tác, giao tiếp qua lại

  • Children need to interact with other children, not only with screens.
  • In the new app you can interact with students in other countries.
  • He is shy and finds it hard to interact in a big group.

interact with people · interact with each other · interact online

Word family

  • interaction noun the activity of talking to or working with other people sự tương tácThere is very little interaction between the two classes.
  • interactive adjective allowing information to go both ways between a person and a computer, or between people có tính tương tácOur English lessons use an interactive whiteboard.

Watch out

Interact is followed by with, not to: interact with your classmates (not interact to).

communicate

/kəˈmjuːnɪkeɪt/8
1

verbintransitive, + with

to share information, ideas or feelings with other people by speaking, writing or moving your body

giao tiếp, liên lạc

  • Deaf people communicate with sign language.
  • We communicate with our relatives abroad by video call.
  • In the future we may communicate without opening our mouths.

communicate with someone · communicate by email · communicate effectively

2

verbtransitive

to make an idea or a feeling clear to other people

truyền đạt, bày tỏ

  • A good speaker can communicate difficult ideas simply.
  • Her face communicated how tired she was.
  • Try to communicate your opinion politely.

communicate an idea · communicate a message

Word family

  • communication noun the activity of sharing information with other people sự giao tiếp, sự truyền thôngGood communication is important in every family.
  • communicative adjective willing to talk to people and share information cởi mở, thích giao tiếpMy little brother is very communicative with strangers.

Watch out

Spelling: two ms and two c sounds — commu-ni-cate. The noun communication is uncountable when it means the activity, but plural communications can mean systems such as phones and the internet.

gesture

/ˈdʒestʃə(r)/9
1

nouncountable

a movement of your hand, head or body that shows what you mean or how you feel

cử chỉ, điệu bộ

  • A thumbs-up is a friendly gesture in Viet Nam.
  • He made a gesture towards the door to show us the way out.
  • Some gestures mean different things in different countries.

make a gesture · a friendly gesture · a rude gesture

2

verbintransitive

to move your hand, head or body in order to tell someone something

ra hiệu, khoát tay ra dấu

  • She gestured to us to sit down.
  • The guide gestured at the old temple on the hill.
  • He gestured wildly because the music was too loud to talk.

gesture to someone · gesture at something

Watch out

We make a gesture (not do a gesture). Gestures are part of body language — the way we communicate without words.

signal

/ˈsɪɡnəl/10
1

nouncountable

a movement, sound or light that gives information or tells someone to do something

tín hiệu, dấu hiệu

  • When I raise my hand, that's the signal to start.
  • The teacher gave a signal and everybody stood up.
  • A red light is a signal to stop.

give a signal · wait for the signal

2

nouncountable or uncountable

radio waves that carry sound, pictures or data to a phone, TV or computer

sóng, tín hiệu (điện thoại, truyền hình)

  • There is no signal up in the mountains, so I can't call you.
  • The signal is weak inside the lift.
  • Move to the window for a better signal.

a weak signal · a strong signal · lose the signal

3

verbtransitive or intransitive

to make a movement or sound in order to tell someone something

ra hiệu, báo hiệu

  • The driver signalled that he was turning left.
  • She signalled to me to be quiet.
  • A bell signals the end of the lesson.

signal to someone · signal the end of something

Watch out

British English doubles the l: signalled, signalling; American English writes signaled, signaling. The g is silent-ish: say SIG-nuhl.

translate

/trænzˈleɪt/11

verbtransitive

to change words from one language into another language

dịch (từ ngôn ngữ này sang ngôn ngữ khác)

  • Can you translate this sentence into Vietnamese?
  • The app can translate a menu from a photo.
  • Her book has been translated into ten languages.

translate something into English · translate a text · translate word for word

Word family

  • translation noun a text that has been changed into another language, or the act of doing this bản dịch; việc dịchI read an English translation of the poem.
  • translator noun a person or machine that changes writing into another language người dịch, phần mềm dịchIn the future, a tiny translator in your ear may do the work.

Watch out

Translate into a language, from another: translate from English into Vietnamese. A translator works with writing; an interpreter works with speech.

device

/dɪˈvaɪs/12

nouncountable

a machine or piece of equipment made to do one particular job

thiết bị

  • This small device records your voice and turns it into text.
  • Please switch off all electronic devices during the test.
  • In 2050, we may wear a device that translates every language.

an electronic device · a mobile device · use a device

Watch out

Device is the noun; devise (with an s) is the verb meaning to invent. A device is any tool or machine, often serious or technical; a gadget is usually small, clever and a bit fun — a phone is a device, a tiny camera pen is a gadget.

network

/ˈnetwɜːk/13
1

nouncountable

a group of computers or phones that are joined together so that they can share information

mạng (máy tính, điện thoại)

  • All the school computers are on the same network.
  • Don't share your password on a public network.
  • The network went down and nobody could send an email.

a computer network · a mobile network · connect to a network

2

nouncountable

a group of people or organizations that are connected and help each other

mạng lưới (người, tổ chức)

  • She has a wide network of friends around the world.
  • The club is part of a network of youth groups.
  • A good network of volunteers helped after the flood.

a network of friends · a support network

3

verbintransitive

to meet and talk to people who may be useful to you in work or study

kết nối, mở rộng quan hệ

  • Students go to the fair to network with companies.
  • He is good at networking at conferences.
  • You can network online without leaving your house.

network with people · network online

Watch out

One word, no hyphen: network. Note social network (one website or app) versus social media (all of them together).

connection

/kəˈnekʃn/14
1

nouncountable

a link between two things, in which one thing is related to or affects the other

mối liên hệ, sự liên quan

  • Is there a connection between screen time and poor sleep?
  • I can't see any connection between the two stories.
  • There is a close connection between language and culture.

a connection between two things · a close connection · make a connection

2

nouncountable

the way a computer or phone joins the internet or a phone line

kết nối (mạng, internet)

  • Our internet connection is very slow today.
  • I lost the connection in the middle of the video call.
  • You need a strong connection to watch films online.

an internet connection · a slow connection · lose the connection

3

nouncountable, usually plural

a person you know who may be able to help you

mối quan hệ (người quen có thể giúp đỡ)

  • He got the job through family connections.
  • She has useful connections in the music world.
  • Good connections can open doors, but hard work matters more.

family connections · useful connections

Word family

  • connect verb to join two things together, or to join a device to the internet kết nối, nốiConnect the headphones to your phone.
  • connected adjective joined to something, especially to a network được kết nốiMy laptop isn't connected to the wifi.

Watch out

Spelling trap: connection has two ns in the middle and ends in -tion (the older form connexion is no longer used).

virtual

/ˈvɜːtʃuəl/15
1

adjectivebefore a noun

made by a computer so that it seems real, although it does not exist in the real world

ảo (do máy tính tạo ra)

  • We took a virtual tour of the museum from our classroom.
  • In the future, students may meet in a virtual classroom.
  • He plays football with friends in a virtual world.

virtual reality · a virtual classroom · a virtual assistant

2

adjectivebefore a noun

almost what you are describing, but not exactly or officially

gần như, thực tế là

  • The old phone box is a virtual museum piece now.
  • Losing the last match made him a virtual unknown again.
  • Her silence was a virtual answer to the question.

a virtual certainty · a virtual impossibility

Word family

  • virtually adverb almost, very nearly hầu như, gần nhưVirtually everyone in my class has a smartphone.
  • virtual reality noun computer images and sounds that make you feel you are in a real place thực tế ảoWe tried virtual reality glasses at the science fair.

Watch out

Careful: the adverb virtually usually means almost (virtually finished), not by computer. For the computer meaning we normally say online or in virtual reality.

holographic

/ˌhɒləˈɡræfɪk/16

adjective

connected with a hologram — an image made with laser light that looks solid and three-dimensional

thuộc ảnh ba chiều, dạng hình nổi ba chiều

  • In the film, a holographic teacher appears in the middle of the room.
  • One day we may send a holographic image of ourselves to a meeting.
  • The concert used a holographic singer on stage.

a holographic image · a holographic display · a holographic call

Word family

  • hologram noun a picture made with laser light that looks three-dimensional ảnh ba chiều, hình nổiThere is a small hologram on my bank card.

Watch out

Stress: hol-o-GRAPH-ic. The noun is hologram (HOL-o-gram), so say a hologram of my father, but a holographic message.

smartphone

/ˈsmɑːtfəʊn/17

nouncountable

a mobile phone that works like a small computer, with apps, a camera and the internet

điện thoại thông minh

  • She reads the news on her smartphone on the bus.
  • My first smartphone was a present from my uncle.
  • Smartphones are not allowed during the exam.

use a smartphone · a smartphone app · on your smartphone

Watch out

One word: smartphone, not smart phone. It is countable, so use a/an or a plural: I need a new smartphone.

interviewee

/ˌɪntəvjuːˈiː/18

nouncountable

the person who answers the questions in an interview

người được phỏng vấn

  • The interviewee looked nervous at first, then relaxed.
  • Each interviewee had ten minutes to answer.
  • As the interviewee, you should listen carefully before you speak.

a nervous interviewee · the next interviewee

Word family

  • interviewer noun the person who asks the questions in an interview người phỏng vấnThe interviewer smiled and asked about my hobbies.
  • interview noun a meeting in which someone is asked questions, for a job or for the news cuộc phỏng vấnI have a job interview on Friday morning.

Watch out

The ending -ee shows the person who receives the action: interviewee = the one who is asked, employee = the one who is employed, trainee = the one who is trained. The ending -er shows the person who does it: interviewer, employer, trainer. Pronunciation: -ee is stressed and long — in-ter-view-EE — while -er is weak: IN-ter-view-er.

Pronunciation — Stress in *-ese* and *-ee* words

This is a stress rule, not a new sound. When a word ends in the suffix -ese or -ee, the stress falls on that final syllable. The last syllable becomes long and loud: /iːz/ or /iː/.

-ese / -ee → stress on the LAST syllable spelt -ee, but NOT stressed at the end
The final vowel long and strong — /iːz/ or /iː/ short and weak — /i/
Where the stress goes on the very last syllable on an earlier syllable
Examples Vietnaˈmese /ˌvjetnəˈmiːz/ · Japaˈnese /ˌdʒæpəˈniːz/ · Chiˈnese /ˌtʃaɪˈniːz/ · Portuˈguese /ˌpɔːtʃʊˈɡiːz/ ˈcoffee /ˈkɒfi/ · comˈmittee /kəˈmɪti/ · ˈtoffee /ˈtɒfi/
More examples refuˈgee /ˌrefjuˈdʒiː/ · guaranˈtee /ˌɡærənˈtiː/ · trainˈee /ˌtreɪˈniː/ · interviewˈee /ˌɪntəvjuːˈiː/ · aˈgree /əˈɡriː/ ˈFrisbee /ˈfrɪzbi/

Mẹo: Hãy nghe độ dài của âm cuối. Với -ese và -ee thật, âm cuối là /iː/ dài và được nhấn: VietnaMESE, refu**GEE. Còn coffee, toffee, committee chỉ kết thúc bằng /i/ ngắn, không nhấn. Vỗ tay vào âm tiết mạnh nhất khi đọc — bạn sẽ nghe ngay sự khác nhau.

(Lưu ý nhỏ: employee có hai cách nhấn đều được chấp nhận — /ˌemplɔɪˈiː/ nhấn cuối, và /ɪmˈplɔɪiː/ nhấn giữa. Bài tập dưới đây không dùng từ này.)
