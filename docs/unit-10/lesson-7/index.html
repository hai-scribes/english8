Error hunt

Find and correct six errors in this paragraph. Three are prepositions of place or time; three are possessive forms.

Two marks are left on the wall. The tin box came home in my pocket in Tuesday evening, and the small brass thing from inside it sits in the shelf above my bed. Thảo says hers grandmother still remembers the old post office. "Is this box your's?" Bà Sáu asked at breakfast. Its not mine, and it is not hers either. We are walking upriver in Saturday morning.

MarkedShort answer

Read the paragraph again and find the errors yourself — their places are not given. Write the correction only: the word or words that should replace the wrong ones, in the order the errors appear. Spelling counts.

  • Spelling counts.
  • British and American spellings are both accepted.
  1. on Tuesday evening/on (a named day takes on)
  2. on the shelf/on (a surface takes on)
  3. her grandmother/her (hers takes no noun after it)
  4. yours (a possessive pronoun takes no apostrophe)
  5. It's not mine/it's/it is (it's = it is; its is the possessive)
  6. on Saturday morning/on (a named day takes on)

Project — A message across a hundred years

Build a two-page display comparing how a message travelled in 1925, in 2025, and how it might travel in 2125.

Step 1 — Research. Choose one simple message, for example: "Grandmother is ill. Please come home." Find out how that message would have reached a person 200 km away in 1925.

Step 2 — Fill the table.

1925 2025 2125 (your prediction)
Device used
How long it took
Cost
What could go wrong
What the receiver felt

Step 3 — Write it up. Write 100–120 words answering: which of the three would you choose if the message really mattered, and why? Use at least four prepositions of time and two possessive pronouns.

Step 4 — Present it. Present your display aloud for ninety seconds without reading word for word. Record it. Listen back once and count how many times you said in, on and at correctly.

Stretch: Interview the oldest person you know. Ask them about the first telephone call they ever made — where they stood, who they called, and what it cost. Add their answer to your display as a short quotation, and mark them as the interviewee.
