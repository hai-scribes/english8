7.4

Error hunt

Find and correct six errors in this paragraph. Five are about articles; one is a subject–verb agreement slip.

Five marks are on our wall now, and the whole market is asking questions. Last night my grandmother went to the bed at midnight, still folding leaves. She says the customs like ours never really die. She is oldest person on our lane, so she lit an first stick of incense at dawn. Nobody sweep the yard afterwards, and we ate the breakfast very late. Lanterns were still burning at our gate when we finished. Bống showed me how to send a thing back, so I carried them down and set them on the water — "Go well." — and the whole night went out with them, one lantern at a time.

MarkedShort answer

Read the paragraph again and find the errors yourself — their places are not given. Write the correction only: the word or words that should replace the wrong ones, in the order the errors appear. Spelling counts.

  • Spelling counts.
  • British and American spellings are both accepted.
  1. bed/to bed (go to bed is the activity, not the furniture)
  2. customs/customs like ours (plural, general meaning — no article)
  3. the oldest person/the (a superlative takes the)
  4. the first stick/the (an ordinal number takes the)
  5. sweeps (Nobody sweep → sweeps — subject–verb agreement)
  6. breakfast/ate breakfast (meals take no article)

Project — A custom in your family

Record one custom properly, before the person who knows it best stops being able to explain it.

Step 1 — Choose and ask. Pick one custom your family keeps. Interview the oldest person you can reach about it. Ask at least these five questions:

  • What exactly do we do, and in what order?
  • Who taught you this?
  • Has anything about it changed since you were my age?
  • What must you never do?
  • What would be lost if we stopped?

Step 2 — Write it down. Produce a one-page record with three parts:

Part Content
What The custom, step by step, in order
Why The meaning, in your interviewee's own words
Warnings The taboos — what a guest must not do

Step 3 — Illustrate. Add one drawing or photograph, and label at least five objects in English (altar, incense, offering, tray, lantern …).

Step 4 — Present. Explain your custom aloud for one minute to someone at home, without reading word for word. Record it and listen back once, checking your articles.

Stretch: Ask a classmate from a different province or a different ethnic group about their version of the same kind of event. Write 60–80 words on one thing that is the same and one thing that is different.
