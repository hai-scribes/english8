A Closer Look 1

Vocabulary — Customs and traditions

custom

/ˈkʌstəm/1
1

nouncountable

a way of behaving that a group of people has followed for a long time

phong tục, tập quán

  • It is a custom to give lucky money at Tet.
  • Every village has its own customs.
  • Taking off your shoes indoors is a local custom.

a local custom · an old custom · follow a custom

2

nounplural: customs

the place at a border or airport where officials check what you are bringing in

hải quan

  • We waited an hour to get through customs.
  • You must declare it at customs.

go through customs · a customs officer

Watch out

A custom is what a group does; a habit is what one person does. Compare tradition, which is usually older and passed down through generations.

tradition

/trəˈdɪʃn/2

nouncountable / uncountable

a belief, custom or way of doing things that has been passed from one generation to the next

truyền thống

  • It is a tradition to visit relatives at Tet.
  • The family has kept this tradition for a hundred years.
  • By tradition, the youngest child speaks first.

keep a tradition · a family tradition · by tradition

Word family

  • traditional adjective following long-established custom truyền thốngTraditional food is served at the festival.

Watch out

A tradition is older and passed down; a custom is simply what a group usually does. A habit belongs to one person.

ancestor

/ˈænsestə(r)/3

nouncountable

a person in your family who lived a long time before you

tổ tiên, ông bà tổ tiên

  • We honour our ancestors on the death anniversary.
  • His ancestors came from a village in Ha Nam.
  • The altar holds photographs of our ancestors.

worship one's ancestors · an ancestor altar

Word family

  • ancestral adjective belonging to your ancestors thuộc về tổ tiênThis is our ancestral home.

Watch out

Opposite: descendant (someone who comes after you). Stress the first syllable: AN-ces-tor.

worship

/ˈwɜːʃɪp/4
1

verbtransitive / intransitive

to show respect and love for a god or for your ancestors, especially through ceremonies

thờ cúng, thờ phụng

  • Many Vietnamese families worship their ancestors.
  • People come here to worship at the temple.
  • The village worships a local guardian spirit.

worship ancestors · a place of worship

2

noununcountable

the act of showing respect and love for a god

sự thờ phụng

  • The pagoda is a place of worship.
  • Ancestor worship is common in Viet Nam.

ancestor worship · a place of worship

Watch out

British spelling doubles the p: worshipped, worshipping.

offering

/ˈɒfərɪŋ/5

nouncountable

something such as food, flowers or money that is given as part of a religious ceremony

đồ cúng, lễ vật

  • The family placed fruit and flowers as offerings on the altar.
  • Offerings are made before the ceremony begins.
  • She brought an offering of rice and incense.

make an offering · place offerings on the altar

Word family

  • offer verb to give or present something dâng, mời, đề nghịThey offer fruit to their ancestors.

incense

/ˈɪnsens/6

noununcountable

a substance that produces a sweet smell when burned, used in religious ceremonies

hương, nhang

  • She lit three sticks of incense.
  • The temple smelled of incense.
  • Burning incense is part of the ritual.

burn incense · light incense · a stick of incense

Watch out

Uncountable — say a stick of incense, not an incense. Stress the first syllable: IN-cense.

lunar

/ˈluːnə(r)/7

adjectivebefore a noun

connected with the moon, or measured by the moon's cycle

thuộc về mặt trăng, âm lịch

  • Tet is the lunar New Year.
  • The festival follows the lunar calendar.
  • The lunar month has about 29 days.

the lunar calendar · the lunar New Year · a lunar month

Watch out

Compare solar (connected with the sun). Pronounced /ˈluːnə/: LOO-nuh.

festival

/ˈfestɪvl/8

nouncountable

a special day or period when people celebrate something, often with music, food and ceremonies

lễ hội

  • The village holds a festival every spring.
  • The Mid-Autumn Festival is popular with children.
  • Thousands of people attend the festival.

hold a festival · attend a festival · a music festival

ritual

/ˈrɪtʃuəl/9
1

nouncountable

a set of actions always performed in the same way, especially as part of a ceremony

nghi lễ, nghi thức

  • The ritual is performed at the start of the harvest.
  • Lighting incense is an important ritual.
  • The wedding follows an ancient ritual.

perform a ritual · a religious ritual · an ancient ritual

2

nouncountable, everyday use

something you always do in the same way, out of habit

thói quen cố định

  • Sunday lunch with my grandparents is a family ritual.
  • Coffee at seven is his morning ritual.

a daily ritual · a family ritual

generation

/ˌdʒenəˈreɪʃn/10
1

nouncountable

taboo

/təˈbuː/11
1

nouncountable

something that people avoid doing or talking about because it is thought to be wrong or offensive

điều cấm kỵ

  • Sweeping the floor on the first day of Tet is a taboo.
  • In some cultures it is a taboo to point at people.
  • Money used to be a taboo subject at dinner.

break a taboo · a taboo subject

2

adjective

avoided because it is considered wrong or embarrassing

cấm kỵ

  • Death is still a taboo topic in many families.
  • Those words are taboo in polite conversation.

a taboo topic · taboo words

Watch out

Stress the second syllable: ta-BOO. Plural taboos.

superstition

/ˌsuːpəˈstɪʃn/12

nouncountable / uncountable

a belief that certain events or objects bring good or bad luck, not based on reason

sự mê tín, điều mê tín

  • It is a common superstition that black cats bring bad luck.
  • Old superstitions are still strong in some villages.
  • He laughed and called it pure superstition.

an old superstition · believe in superstitions

Word family

  • superstitious adjective believing in superstitions mê tínMy aunt is very superstitious about numbers.

wrap

/ræp/13

verbtransitive

to cover something completely by folding paper, cloth or leaves around it

gói, bọc

  • We wrap the sticky rice cakes in banana leaves.
  • She wrapped the present in red paper.
  • Wrap the fish in foil before you cook it.

wrap a present · wrap something in paper

Word family

  • wrapping noun the paper or material used to cover something giấy gói, vật liệu góiTake the wrapping off carefully.

Watch out

The w is silent: /ræp/. Double the p: wrapped, wrapping. Sounds the same as rap.

decorate

/ˈdekəreɪt/14

verbtransitive

to make something look more attractive by adding things to it

trang trí, trang hoàng

  • We decorate the house with peach blossom at Tet.
  • The children decorated the classroom for the festival.
  • The streets are decorated with red lanterns.

decorate a house · decorate with flowers

Word family

  • decoration noun something added to make a place look attractive đồ trang tríThe decorations stay up for a week.

Watch out

Stress the first syllable: DEC-o-rate.

celebrate

/ˈselɪbreɪt/15

verbtransitive / intransitive

to do something enjoyable to show that a day or an event is important

kỷ niệm, ăn mừng

  • We celebrate Tet with our whole family.
  • How do you celebrate your birthday?
  • The village celebrated the good harvest.

celebrate a festival · celebrate a birthday

Word family

  • celebration noun an occasion when people celebrate lễ kỷ niệm, sự ăn mừngThe celebrations lasted three days.

pass down

/ˌpɑːs ˈdaʊn/16

phrasal verbusually passive

to give knowledge, skills or objects to younger people so that they continue

truyền lại (cho đời sau)

  • These songs have been passed down for generations.
  • My grandmother passed the recipe down to my mother.
  • The craft is passed down from parent to child.

passed down from generation to generation

Watch out

Often in the passive: the story was passed down. Compare pass on (give to the next person) and pass away (a polite way of saying die).

get together

/ˌɡet təˈɡeðə(r)/17

phrasal verb

to meet in order to spend time with each other

tụ họp, sum họp

  • The whole family gets together at Tet.
  • We got together at my aunt's house.
  • Let's get together after the exams.

get together with friends · the family gets together

Word family

  • get-together noun an informal social meeting buổi họp mặtWe're having a small get-together on Sunday.

first-footer

/ˌfɜːst ˈfʊtə(r)/18

nouncountable

the first person to enter a house in the new year, believed to bring good or bad luck for the year

người xông đất, người xông nhà

  • The family chose a cheerful neighbour as their first-footer.
  • A good first-footer is said to bring luck all year.
  • Being the first-footer is a great responsibility.

choose a first-footer · be the first-footer

Watch out

The custom itself is first-footing. In Viet Nam it is xông đất at Tet; the same belief exists in Scotland at New Year.

admire

/ədˈmaɪə(r)/19
1

verbtransitive

to look at something and enjoy how beautiful or impressive it is

ngắm, chiêm ngưỡng

  • We stopped on the bridge to admire the lanterns on the water.
  • Visitors come to the village to admire the blooming peach blossom.
  • She stood back and admired the decorated altar.

admire the view · stop to admire something

2

verbtransitive

to respect somebody for what they are or for what they have done

ngưỡng mộ, khâm phục

  • I admire my grandmother for keeping every one of these customs alive.
  • The whole village admires the old drum maker.

admire somebody for something · greatly admire

Word family

  • admiration noun the feeling of admiring somebody or something sự ngưỡng mộShe spoke of her teacher with real admiration.

Watch out

Two different meanings that share one word: you admire a view with your eyes, and you admire a person with your respect. Stress the second syllable: ad-MIRE.

pray

/preɪ/20

verbintransitive

to speak to a god, or to those you worship, to give thanks or to ask for something

cầu nguyện, khấn

  • Many families go to the pagoda to pray for good luck at the start of the year.
  • My grandmother prays in front of the altar every morning.
  • They prayed for a good harvest and for the health of the elders.

pray for good luck · pray for somebody · pray at a temple

Word family

  • prayer noun the words you say when you pray lời cầu nguyệnHe said a short prayer and lit the incense.

Watch out

Pray for what you want, not pray something. ✅ pray for good luck ❌ pray good luck. Do not confuse it with prey (an animal that is hunted) — the two sound the same.

chase away

/ˌtʃeɪs əˈweɪ/21

phrasal verbtransitive

to make a person, an animal or a bad thing go away by frightening it

xua đuổi, đuổi đi

  • The noise of the drums is meant to chase away bad spirits.
  • A bamboo pole in the yard chases away bad luck for the coming year.
  • The dogs chased the birds away from the rice.

chase away bad spirits · chase away bad luck

Watch out

Separable: chase the birds away and chase away the birds are both correct, but a pronoun must go in the middle — ✅ chase them away, ❌ chase away them.

release

/rɪˈliːs/22
1

verbtransitive

to let a person or an animal go free

thả, phóng thích

  • The referee releases a duck into the middle of the river.
  • Families release live carp into the lake before Tet.
  • The bird was released at the temple gate.

release a bird · release somebody from prison

2

verbtransitive

to make a film, a record or a piece of news available to everyone

phát hành, công bố

  • The programme was released the week before the festival.
  • They release the results on Monday morning.

release a film · release the results

Watch out

Stress the second syllable: re-LEASE, /rɪˈliːs/. The s at the end is /s/, not /z/.

bad spirit

/ˌbæd ˈspɪrɪt/23

nouncountable, usually plural

a being that people believe cannot be seen and brings harm or bad luck

tà ma, điều xấu xa

  • The lion dance is performed to drive bad spirits out of the house.
  • People once hung small bells on the pole to keep bad spirits away.
  • Nobody in the village will move house on that day, for fear of bad spirits.

chase away bad spirits · keep bad spirits away

Watch out

Nearly always plural: bad spirits. In this sense spirit is a being, not the spirit that means someone's mood — compare team spirit.

bamboo pole

/ˌbæmˈbuː pəʊl/24

nouncountable

a long straight bamboo stem stood up in a yard at Tet and hung with small objects

cây nêu

  • My uncle put up the bamboo pole on the morning of the twenty-third.
  • Small bells and lanterns hang from the bamboo pole in the yard.
  • The bamboo pole stays up until the seventh day of the new year.

put up a bamboo pole · hang something on a bamboo pole

Watch out

English has no single word for cây nêu, so this two-word phrase does the job; say what it is for when you use it with a foreign visitor.

ornamental tree

/ˌɔːnəˈmentl triː/25

nouncountable

a small tree grown for how it looks rather than for fruit or wood

cây cảnh

  • He carried the ornamental tree home on the back of his motorbike.
  • A kumquat is the ornamental tree most families choose at Tet.
  • The ornamental trees are sold at the flower market for a week.

buy an ornamental tree · an ornamental kumquat tree

Watch out

Ornamental describes anything grown or made to be looked at: an ornamental tree, an ornamental pond. A tree grown for its fruit is a fruit tree.

decorative

/ˈdekərətɪv/26

adjective

made or added to make something look attractive rather than to be useful

dùng để trang trí, có tính trang trí

  • The pole is hung with decorative items: bells, small fish, and strips of red paper.
  • These bowls are decorative — nobody eats out of them.
  • She sewed a decorative border along the edge of the cloth.

decorative items · purely decorative

Word family

  • decoration noun something added to make a place look attractive đồ trang tríThe decorations go up a week before the festival.

Watch out

Decorative is an adjective and goes before the noun: decorative items. Do not use it where you mean the verb — ❌ we decorative the house → ✅ we decorate the house.

blooming

/ˈbluːmɪŋ/27

adjective

with flowers fully open

đang nở, đang nở rộ

  • People drive out to the flower village to photograph the blooming peach trees.
  • The blooming apricot branches were sold out by noon.
  • A blooming kumquat tree stood at the door of every house.

blooming flowers · a blooming tree

Word family

  • bloom verb to produce flowers nở hoaThe apricot trees bloom in the first week of the lunar year.

Watch out

Use blooming before the noun (blooming flowers) and in bloom after the verb (the flowers are in bloom). ❌ the flowers are blooming beautiful.

carp

/kɑːp/28

nouncountable, plural usually *carp*

a large freshwater fish, kept in ponds and used in Vietnamese offerings

cá chép

  • The offerings include three live carp in a bowl of water.
  • On the twenty-third day, families release the carp into a river or a lake.
  • He caught a carp almost as long as his arm.

a live carp · release a carp

Watch out

The plural is usually carp, not carps: three carp. Like fish, sheep and deer.

young rice

/ˌjʌŋ ˈraɪs/29

noununcountable

green rice picked before it hardens and then flattened, eaten as a delicacy in autumn

cốm

  • The elders put a little young rice into each child's mouth and ask what they wish for.
  • Young rice is wrapped in lotus leaves and sold by the roadside in autumn.
  • Refusing young rice when an elder offers it would be rude.

a handful of young rice · offer somebody young rice

Watch out

Uncountable, like rice itself: some young rice, never a young rice. Vietnamese cốm; the Khmer name at the Ok Om Bok Festival is om bok.

wedding ceremony

/ˈwedɪŋ ˌserəməni/30

nouncountable

the formal part of a wedding, in which two people are married in front of their families

lễ cưới

  • A lion dance is often performed outside a wedding ceremony.
  • The wedding ceremony was held at the bride's family home.
  • Photographs of both wedding ceremonies hang in the front room.

hold a wedding ceremony · attend a wedding ceremony

Watch out

The wedding ceremony is the formal part; the meal and the music afterwards are the wedding reception or simply the wedding party.

family reunion

/ˌfæməli ˌriːˈjuːniən/31

nouncountable

an occasion when members of a family who live apart come together again

cuộc sum họp gia đình

  • The first day of Tet is a family reunion more than anything else.
  • Everyone travels home for the family reunion, however far it is.
  • We held a small family reunion at my aunt's house in July.

a family reunion dinner · hold a family reunion

Word family

  • reunite verb to bring people together again after a time apart đoàn tụTet reunites families who have not met all year.

Watch out

Built from re- + union: a coming together again. The people already knew each other — a first meeting is not a reunion.

family bonding

/ˌfæməli ˈbɒndɪŋ/32

noununcountable

the building of close feelings between members of a family by spending time together

sự gắn kết tình cảm gia đình

  • These parties are a great time for family bonding.
  • Cooking the cakes together is really family bonding, not just work.
  • She says the long drive home is the best family bonding of the year.

a time for family bonding · good for family bonding

Word family

  • bond verb to form a close relationship with somebody gắn bóThe two cousins bonded over a board game.
  • bond noun a close connection between people mối gắn kếtThere is a strong bond between the three sisters.

Watch out

Uncountable — ❌ a family bonding. Related but not the same: family bonds (countable) are the connections themselves; family bonding is the making of them.

festival goer

/ˈfestɪvl ˌɡəʊə(r)/33

nouncountable

somebody who goes to a festival

người đi xem lễ hội

  • The riverside was packed with festival goers by seven in the morning.
  • Drums, cheering, and a thousand festival goers all shouting at once.
  • Most of the festival goers had come from the next province.

crowds of festival goers · a festival goer

Watch out

-goer builds freely: a cinema goer, a churchgoer, a theatre goer. Written as two words, or with a hyphen (festival-goer); both are accepted.

contestant

/kənˈtestənt/34

nouncountable

somebody who takes part in a competition

thí sinh, người dự thi

martial arts

/ˌmɑːʃl ˈɑːts/35

nounplural

the traditional Asian fighting sports, such as karate, judo or Vietnamese vovinam

võ thuật

  • The unicorn dancers are trained in martial arts as well as acrobatics.
  • He broke with family tradition by not practising martial arts.
  • Martial arts are taught in the yard behind the communal house.

practise martial arts · a martial arts club

Watch out

Plural in form and usually in agreement: martial arts are popular. The first word is /ˈmɑːʃl/ — no r sound in British English, and nothing to do with marital.

acrobatics

/ˌækrəˈbætɪks/36

noununcountable

difficult jumps, turns and balancing done as a performance

  • Whoever wears the head has to be excellent at acrobatics.

  • She learned acrobatics before she ever learned to dance.

lion dance

/ˈlaɪən dɑːns/37

nouncountable

a dance performed in a lion costume at New Year and other big occasions in several Asian countries

múa sư tử

  • A lion dance was performed at their opening ceremony.
  • The Japanese lion dance is called shishi-mai.
  • Two lion dances passed our street before breakfast.

perform a lion dance · a lion dance team

Say do or perform a lion dance, not dance a lion dance. The performers are a lion dance troupe or team.

unicorn dance

/ˈjuːnɪkɔːn dɑːns/38

nouncountable

the Vietnamese dance performed in a lân costume at the Mid-Autumn and New Year Festivals

múa lân

  • The unicorn dance comes down our street on the night of the full moon.
  • A unicorn dance has a partner, ông Địa, who walks in front and fans the head.
  • The drum for the unicorn dance can be heard three streets away.

perform a unicorn dance · a unicorn dance troupe

Watch out

Múa lân is translated into English in several ways — unicorn dance and lion dance are both used. Note the article: a unicorn dance, because unicorn begins with the consonant sound /j/.

whale worship

/ˈweɪl ˌwɜːʃɪp/39

noununcountable

the custom among Vietnamese coastal fishing communities of honouring whales as protectors at sea

tục thờ cá Ông

  • The tradition of whale worship goes back many generations here.
  • A temple for whale worship stands at the end of the beach.

the tradition of whale worship · a whale worship festival

Watch out

Uncountable. The verb pattern doubles the p: the village has worshipped whales for centuries.

monk

/mʌŋk/40

nouncountable

a man who lives in a temple or monastery and has given his life to his religion

nhà sư, thầy tu

  • Keep quiet while the monks and the elders are talking.
  • A young monk swept the temple ground before the ceremony.
  • The monks eat their last meal of the day before noon.

a Buddhist monk · show respect to the monks

Watch out

Rhymes with drunk; the n before /k/ is /ŋ/, so it is /mʌŋk/ — not /mʌnk/. The word for a woman is nun.

longevity

/lɒnˈdʒevəti/41

noununcountable

long life, or the fact of living a long time

sự sống lâu, tuổi thọ

table manners

/ˈteɪbl ˌmænəz/43

nounplural

the way you are expected to behave while eating with other people

phép tắc ăn uống

  • Tom asked Mai about Vietnamese table manners before the dinner.
  • Good table manners here start with waiting for the oldest person.
  • His table manners improved after a week at his grandmother's.

good table manners · bad table manners

Watch out

Always plural — ❌ a table manner. In this phrase manners means polite behaviour, not the manner that means a way of doing something.

traditionally

/trəˈdɪʃənəli/44

adverb

in the way something has been done for a long time; according to tradition

theo truyền thống, theo lệ xưa

  • Traditionally, children in the US go from house to house asking for sweets at Halloween.
  • The pole is traditionally put up seven days before the new year.
  • This dish is traditionally eaten cold.

traditionally, … · be traditionally made

Watch out

Very often the first word of the sentence, with a comma after it: Traditionally, we visit our father's family first. That opening position is what makes it useful in writing.

Pronunciation — /n/ and /ŋ/

Both sounds come out through the nose. The difference is where your tongue touches.

/n/ — front of the mouth /ŋ/ — back of the mouth
Tongue tip presses the ridge just behind the top teeth tip stays down; the back of the tongue presses the soft palate
Spelling n, nn ng, and also n before /k/ or /ɡ/
Examples thin, sun, moon, dinner, banner, incense, lantern thing, sing, spring, offering, wrapping, during, gong, young, along, thank, finger

Notice the last two: thank is /θæŋk/ and finger is /ˈfɪŋɡə(r)/. Neither is written with ng at the end, but both have /ŋ/, because the n sits right before /k/ or /ɡ/.

One more pair worth learning by heart:

Word IPA Is there a /ɡ/ after the /ŋ/?
singer /ˈsɪŋə(r)/ No — one who sings
finger /ˈfɪŋɡə(r)/ Yes — part of your hand

Mẹo: /ŋ/ chính là âm "ng" quen thuộc của tiếng Việt (như trong vâng, sang), còn /n/ là âm "n" (như trong nan, tan). Người Việt phát âm được cả hai — vấn đề là đừng đổi chỗ chúng ở cuối từ tiếng Anh. Đặt tay lên mũi: cả /n/ và /ŋ/ đều làm mũi rung, vì đều là âm mũi. Khác nhau là ở lưỡi — với /n/ đầu lưỡi chạm lợi trên, còn với /ŋ/ lưỡi lùi hẳn về sau.

2.7

Say these sentences

Read aloud three times, faster each time. Record yourself if you can.

  1. The young singer sang a long song beside the gong.
  2. Nine thin banners hung along the lane.
  3. My grandmother lights the incense at dinner, and we thank our ancestors.

Answers will vary in speed. Check: every -ng word ends at the back of your mouth with the tongue tip down, and thin, banners, lane, incense, dinner, nine all have the tongue tip up behind the teeth. Thank has /ŋ/, not /n/.
