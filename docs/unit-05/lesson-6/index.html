Listening — A market seller tells a neighbour what she saw

You get one play. The speaker is talking fast and doubling back, so read both sets of questions in the preview window first and answer as you listen rather than afterwards. The script stays locked until you have marked every question.

Nghe một lần duy nhất. Đọc trước câu hỏi, rồi vừa nghe vừa trả lời.

ListeningPlays once

Answer as you listen. You then have two minutes to check what you wrote.

First you hear a short spoken introduction — it is not written down, so listen to it. Then you get 30 seconds to read the questions, and then the recording plays once.

Practising does not use up your one play. When you want the real thing, take it once below.

The voice is your device's speech synthesiser. It says the words clearly, but it is not a real speaker: no accent range, and none of the run-together sounds of natural speech.

Script

Listen to me. I am not a woman who invents things, and I am telling you what I saw with my own eyes.

I walked down to the communal house on the night of the festival, the way I do every year. Six old women and a plate of fruit — that is what it has been since my son was small. Instead there were three hundred people in that lane. There were lanterns all the way from the gate down to the water. Drums until it was light, and cakes I have not eaten since I was a girl. And I did not know one single face. I have lived in this lane for fifty-one years.

Near the water there was a boy from our own street, and beside him a small girl nobody in this lane has ever seen. Wet hair. A shirt three sizes too big. And when the water went out, it went out around her feet last.

Now. My grandmother worked in a house upriver when she was young, and she told me about a child like that one. She said the sea sends them up and then comes after them, and everything it ever took comes with it. I laughed at her. I was eleven.

I am not laughing now. Ask anybody who stood in that lane. And then ask yourself what else the sea can bring back, because I asked myself that all week, and so did everybody at the market.

Writing — An email of advice

Task. A friend from another country is coming to your family's festival for the first time. Write an email of 80–100 words telling them what to do and what not to do.

Model email — read this first

Hi Khoa,

I'm glad you are coming to our village festival on Saturday! Here is some advice.

Do bring a small gift for my grandmother — fruit or flowers is perfect. Do copy me when we light the incense and bow to our ancestors at the altar.

Don't wear a hat inside the communal house, and please don't point at the offerings. Older people really mind that.

One more thing: don't eat before the ceremony. You will need room for the sticky rice cakes!

See you soon,

Articles are a spine, not a single lesson

Articles are the small words that go wrong most often, and unlike most grammar points you meet them in every sentence you write. One lesson is not enough for that, so this one does not end here: every writing task from Unit 6 to Unit 12 carries a five-item article check, and this is where that starts.

Tiếng Việt: Mạo từ là trục chính, không phải một bài lẻ. Từ Unit 6 trở đi, mỗi bài viết đều có phần kiểm tra 5 mạo từ.

Go further

Report it as a fraction, not as a mark

When you check your email, do not give yourself a score. Count instead:

Articles supplied in of places that required one.

Find every place an article was obligatory, then count how many you actually supplied. That fraction is the measurement — not a percentage, not a grade.

And read it correctly, because this is the part that misleads people. At the level you are working at, the fraction is expected to wobble, and more mistakes can be a sign of progress rather than of decline. When you start attempting harder sentences, you make more mistakes in them — that is what moving up looks like. Watch the fraction over a month, not from one paragraph to the next.

Tiếng Việt: Ghi kết quả dạng phân số (đúng / vị trí bắt buộc), không phải điểm số. Ở trình độ này, tỉ lệ lỗi tăng lên là dấu hiệu tiến bộ — vì bạn đang thử những câu khó hơn.

80–100 words

Before you finish

  • 80–100 words
  • Paragraphs, not one solid block
  • A greeting at the top
  • A sign-off at the end
  • At least four words from the Lesson 2 vocabulary table
  • At least two don'ts
  • At least two dos — things you tell them to do
  • Articles checked: a/an by sound, the for the one we both know, no article for general plurals

6 of these 8 are checked for you, from what you actually wrote. The rest you tick yourself — they need your judgement, not a count. Nothing here gives your writing a score.

Trying longer sentences and newer words usually means more mistakes for a while. That is normal at this stage — keep going.
